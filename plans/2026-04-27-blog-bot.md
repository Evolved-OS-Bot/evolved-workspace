# Plan: Blog Bot — Automated Content Pipeline
**Created:** 2026-04-27
**Status:** Scoped — Not Started

---

## Objective

Replace the manual blog writing step with a Claude-powered content pipeline that produces one publication-ready article per week, aligned to The Evolved Strength Training Doctrine, optimised for SEO/AIO/GEO, and delivered as a Google Doc for review before publishing to WordPress via RankMath REST API. Trainers, owners, and the bot itself all feed the topic queue. The richest source — pre-qualification conversations — flows in automatically.

---

## SEO/AIO/GEO Strategy (Claude-Native, No Third-Party Tools)

### SEO (Search Engine Optimization)
Claude handles keyword strategy, search intent analysis, title and meta writing, H-tag hierarchy, internal linking, and featured snippet optimisation natively. The doctrine + blog hierarchy provides the topical authority framework. Every article is mapped to one primary keyword and 4–6 secondary/long-tail keywords selected for intent match, not just volume.

### AIO (Google AI Overview Optimization)
Google's AI Overviews favour content that:
- Directly answers specific questions (structured Q&A format)
- Uses clear definitions at the top of each section
- Cites named experts and peer-reviewed research
- Covers topics comprehensively within a well-structured hierarchy

The existing blog format already satisfies these requirements. The bot enforces this structure on every article.

### GEO (Generative Engine Optimization)
For Perplexity, ChatGPT search, and other AI answer engines:
- Authoritative, well-cited content gets quoted
- Named expert references (Dr Stacy Sims, Dr Gabrielle Lyon, Dr Vonda Wright, Dr Peter Attia) signal credibility
- Factual, structured statements are preferred over narrative
- Schema markup (FAQ, Article, LocalBusiness) added via RankMath

The Evolved's citation-heavy, expert-aligned approach is already positioned correctly for GEO. The bot systematises it.

## Topic Queue System

Topics enter the queue from four sources. All land in a shared **Google Sheet** (Topic Queue tab) accessible to Peter, trainers, and Amind.

### Source 1: Pre-Qualification Conversations & Objections (Richest)

Two dedicated tabs in the Topic Queue Google Sheet are written automatically by GHL:

**Pre-Qual Insights tab** — one row per SA booking where goals are submitted. GHL writes: Date, First Name, Life Stage (from contact tag, applied at lead intake), Primary Goal, Training History, Support Preference, Obstacles, Readiness. Trigger: `goals submitted` tag added. When the pre-qual AI agent goes live, trigger switches to `pre-qual complete` and the full SA: conversation transcript is included for richer extraction.

**Objections Log tab** — one row per no-sale consultation. GHL writes: Date, First Name, Life Stage tag, Main Objection, Secondary Objections 1–3. Trigger: SA Consultation Feedback form submitted with Sales Outcome = No Sale.

The blog bot's `prequal_extractor.py` scans both tabs weekly and extracts:
- Recurring questions and concerns (exact language of the target demographic)
- Knowledge gaps ("I didn't know that", "nobody told me")
- Objections that reveal misunderstandings — these map directly to myth-busting and objection article topics
- Life stage is already tagged — bot maps directly to the correct demographic cluster

Each extracted theme becomes a topic suggestion in the Topic Queue. `Added to Queue?` column is marked Y once processed so rows aren't picked up twice. These are the highest-value topics because they are written in the exact language of the target audience.

### Source 2: Trainer and Owner Input
A simple Google Sheet tab: **Topic Suggestions**

| Column | Content |
|---|---|
| Topic / Question | What the article should answer |
| Demographic | Which life stage it targets |
| Context | Why this is relevant right now (optional) |
| Added By | Who suggested it |
| Priority | High / Normal |

Trainers add rows from their phone. No login, no system — just the sheet. The bot checks this tab on every weekly run and pulls any unprocessed rows into the queue.

### Source 3: Bot Keyword Research (Doctrine-Driven Gap Analysis)
Each week, if the human-sourced queue is thin, the bot runs a gap analysis:
1. Reviews the blog hierarchy: which demographic guides lack supporting long-tail articles?
2. Identifies the highest-value gap (most searched life stage × least covered topic)
3. Generates a topic + primary keyword targeting that gap
4. Adds it to the queue with source tagged as "Bot Research"

This ensures the pipeline never stalls and systematically builds topical authority toward the pillar page.

### Source 4: Seasonal and Timely Triggers
Some topics have natural timing: New Year (goal-setting), February (Valentine's fitness), World Osteoporosis Day (October 20), Menopause Awareness Month (October). The bot maintains a simple calendar of these and surfaces them 3 weeks before the relevant date.

## Weekly Pipeline Flow

```
Monday — Topic Selection
      │
      ▼
Bot reads Topic Queue Google Sheet
  Priority order: High → pre-qual sourced → trainer suggested → bot research → seasonal
  Selects top topic for the week
  Logs selection to Sheet (marks row as In Progress)
      │
      ▼
Tuesday — Research + Outline
      │
      ▼
Bot runs doctrine alignment check:
  - Which life stage does this serve?
  - Which execution pillar? (Exercise / Nutrition / Recovery / Mindset)
  - Which hierarchy position does this fill?
  - What is the primary keyword and search intent?
  - Which expert references are relevant?
  - Which internal links should this article support?

Bot generates:
  - Primary keyword
  - 4–6 secondary / long-tail keywords
  - Target reader persona (e.g. "postmenopausal woman, 55–65, active but undertrained")
  - Proposed H-tag structure (H1, H2s, H3s)
  - Research citations to include (peer-reviewed where possible)
  - Internal linking targets (existing articles + pillar page)
  - Photo library tags (suggested images by demographic + topic)
      │
      ▼
Wednesday — Full Article Draft
      │
      ▼
Bot writes complete article following the established format:

  1. Title (SEO-optimised, 55–65 characters)
  2. Introduction — hook, problem statement, doctrine alignment, local The Evolved reference
  3. Body sections (H2/H3 structure) — each section:
       - Answers a specific question
       - Cites named experts where relevant
       - Includes peer-reviewed reference where appropriate
       - Written in short paragraphs (mobile-readable)
  4. What Most Women Get Wrong — addresses common misconceptions (increases AIO eligibility)
  5. How We Train at The Evolved — connects doctrine to The Evolved method
  6. Client Story — anonymised or composite (Amind replaces with real client story if available)
  7. CTA — Strength Assessment booking
  8. References (APA format, with DOI links)
  9. SEO Summary block:
       - Primary keyword
       - Secondary keywords
       - Suggested internal links (anchor text + target URL)
       - RankMath meta title (55–60 chars)
       - RankMath meta description (150–160 chars)
       - Focus keyword for RankMath
  10. Optional Expansion Sections — flagged for future articles
      │
      ▼
Bot creates Google Doc in designated Drafts folder
  File name: [DRAFT] YYYY-MM-DD — [Article Title]
  Shares with Peter and Amind (edit access)
      │
      ▼
Bot posts to Discord #blog-drafts:
  "📝 Blog draft ready for review — [Title]
   [Google Doc link]
   Demographic: [Life Stage] | Keyword: [Primary Keyword]
   React ✅ to approve for publishing queue | ❌ to request revision"
      │
      ▼
Peter reviews Google Doc
  Edits directly in Doc if needed
  Reacts ✅ in Discord when approved
      │
      ▼
Bot receives approval reaction
  Reads final Doc content (in case edits were made)
  Creates WordPress post via REST API:
    - status: draft (Amind does final publish check + featured image selection)
    - title, content, excerpt
    - categories and tags
    - RankMath fields: focus_keyword, meta_title, meta_description
    - suggested_images note in post meta (for Amind to select from photo library)
  Posts to Discord: "✅ WordPress draft created — [WP Admin link]
                     Amind to add featured image and publish."
      │
      ▼
Amind:
  Opens WordPress draft
  Selects featured image from photo library (bot suggests tags)
  Final check
  Publishes
      │
      ▼
Bot updates Google Sheet — marks row as Published, logs date
```

## Article Format Standard

Based on the existing example output. Every article produced matches this structure:

| Element | Standard |
|---|---|
| Word count | 1,800–2,500 words |
| Paragraphs | Short (2–4 lines), mobile-readable |
| H-tags | H1 (title), H2 (main sections), H3 (subsections) |
| Expert citations | Minimum 2 named experts per article |
| Research citations | Minimum 3 peer-reviewed studies, APA format with DOI |
| Local references | West End Brisbane gym reference in intro and CTA |
| Client story | 1 per article (composite or real — Amind substitutes real if available) |
| CTA | Strength Assessment booking in closing section |
| Internal links | Minimum 3 (pillar + demographic guide + related article) |
| SEO block | Always included (keywords, meta, RankMath fields, internal link anchors) |
| Expansion sections | Always included (future article opportunities) |
| Tone | Direct, warm, evidence-based — no fluff, no hedging |

## RankMath WordPress Integration

RankMath fields written via WordPress REST API on draft creation:

```python
post_data = {
    "title": seo_title,
    "content": article_html,
    "excerpt": meta_description,
    "status": "draft",
    "categories": [category_id],
    "tags": [tag_ids],
    "meta": {
        "rank_math_focus_keyword": primary_keyword,
        "rank_math_title": seo_title,          # 55–60 chars
        "rank_math_description": meta_description,  # 150–160 chars
        "rank_math_robots": ["index", "follow"],
    }
}
```

WordPress auth: Application Password (generated in WordPress Users settings — no OAuth needed).

## Notes

- **Citation accuracy is critical** — Claude's knowledge of peer-reviewed research in women's strength training, bone density, and hormonal health is strong but must be verified. The human review step (Peter reads the Google Doc) is the citation quality gate. Hallucinated studies are the main risk; the format standard (APA with DOI) makes fake citations easy to spot.
- **Client stories** — the bot writes a composite/anonymised story based on the demographic. Amind replaces with a real client story if one is available and consented. Real stories always preferred — bot version is a placeholder.
- **Internal linking** — the bot can only suggest internal links to articles that exist. As the library grows, internal linking becomes richer. The first 6–8 articles (demographic guides) are the foundation — the bot should prioritise these.
- **Pillar page first** — the Ultimate Guide to Strength Training for Women should be the first article built. It anchors the entire hierarchy. All subsequent articles link up to it.
- **Amind's role post-automation** — shifts from writing and researching to: reviewing featured images, adding real client stories, making light edits if needed, and publishing. Estimated time per article drops from several hours to 20–30 minutes.
- **Pre-qual content flywheel** — this is the long-term competitive advantage. As pre-qual conversation volume grows, the topic queue fills with increasingly specific, intent-matched topics written in the exact language of the target demographic. Generic competitors cannot replicate this.
- **AIO/GEO positioning** — the combination of doctrine-aligned topical authority, named expert citations, and peer-reviewed research makes The Evolved's blog highly citable by AI answer engines. This is an emerging channel that most small gyms are not thinking about. First-mover advantage in "women's strength training Brisbane" on AI search is achievable within 6–12 months of consistent publishing.
