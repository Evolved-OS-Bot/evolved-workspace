# The Evolved — Strength Training Blog Doctrine
**Last Updated:** 2026-04-27
**Source:** The Evolved Strength Training Doctrine for Women

---

## Core Principle

Every blog published by The Evolved must reinforce one core truth:

> **Muscle is the foundation of female health, longevity, and aesthetics.**

Strength training is not simply exercise. It is the primary intervention for maintaining health, independence, and physical capability across a woman's entire life.

All articles must reinforce that:
- Muscle improves metabolic health
- Muscle improves bone density
- Muscle improves hormonal resilience
- Muscle improves longevity and quality of life

This principle applies to every demographic, but the reason for training evolves across life stages.

---

## The Postmenopausal Truth

Postmenopausal women are the hardest demographic to build muscle.

Because of hormonal changes, postmenopausal women require:
- Higher intensity
- Greater effort
- Sufficient training volume

In many cases, they require **more** structured strength training than younger women — not less. This directly contradicts the outdated belief that women should slow down, do less, or switch to walking and Pilates only.

As women age, intelligent strength training becomes more important.

---

## The Intensity Standard

Research shows postmenopausal women must train at sufficient intensity to stimulate muscle growth.

**Evidence-based threshold: 70–85% of 1-rep max (1RM)**

This intensity must be performed multiple times per week across several sets per exercise. Training below this threshold produces minimal or no muscle growth.

**Example:** A woman whose 1RM is 50kg should train with approximately 35–45kg for multiple sets of 8+ reps.

This intensity standard forms the foundation of The Evolved programming philosophy.

## The Powerbuilding Method

The Evolved training philosophy blends two proven methods:

**Bodybuilding** — builds muscle shape, muscular development, aesthetic physique

**Powerlifting** — builds strength, neuromuscular efficiency, progressive overload capacity

The combination produces the **Powerbuilding Effect**: women build muscle, become stronger, improve longevity, and maintain a vibrant and capable body — without aggressive calorie restriction that strips muscle away.

The emphasis is:
- Feed the body to build muscle
- Train with progressive overload
- Develop strength as the foundation of health

## Periodised Strength Training

The Evolved programs follow a periodised strength training model. Intensity and repetition ranges evolve across training blocks:

| Phase | Reps | Intensity |
|---|---|---|
| Phase 1 | 12–10 reps | ~70% 1RM |
| Phase 2 | Moderate reps | Increased intensity |
| Phase 3 | Lower reps | 80–85% 1RM |

This progression allows women to develop skill, increase strength, and build muscle safely.

---

## Skill Development Timeline

Training with high intensity requires technical skill. Learning to lift properly often takes **2–3 years of consistent training**.

This means waiting until postmenopause to begin serious strength training is far from ideal. Women benefit enormously from learning these skills earlier in life — building muscle more efficiently, accumulating strength, and entering later decades from a higher baseline of muscle mass.

## Life Stage Strategy

### Teenagers
Strength training during this stage:
- Improves brain health and athletic performance
- Reduces injury risk
- Builds confidence
- Establishes movement skills that support strength training for life

### Women in Their 20s and 30s
Primary motivators: energy, confidence, aesthetic physique.

Strength training delivers: tight waist, strong glutes, toned arms, metabolic health.

The deeper benefit: **future-proofing the body**. Muscle built in this period becomes a critical reserve for pregnancy, postpartum recovery, perimenopause, and postmenopause.

### Pregnancy and Postpartum
One of the largest hormonal shifts in a woman's life.

Muscle loss does not occur gradually — it often occurs in sudden drops ("cliffs"). Pregnancy and postpartum represent one of these cliffs.

Strength training during this period helps women maintain muscle reserves, recover faster postpartum, and rebuild strength safely.

### Perimenopause
Typically lasts 5–10 years before menopause. **The most critical window for building muscle** before hormonal changes accelerate muscle loss.

Women in perimenopause benefit from training as if already postmenopausal:
- They develop the skill to lift with intensity
- They build muscle more efficiently
- They enter postmenopause with a higher muscle baseline

This dramatically improves long-term outcomes.

### Postmenopause
Strength training becomes non-negotiable.

Without adequate resistance training: muscle declines rapidly, bone density declines, metabolic health worsens.

With sufficient training intensity: muscle can still be built, strength can still increase, independence can be preserved for decades.
