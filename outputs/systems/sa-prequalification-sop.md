# SA Pre-Qualification Conversation SOP
**Version:** 1.3
**Last Updated:** 2026-07-23
**Owner:** The Evolved — Sales & Admin
**Used by:** Owner, admin and coaching team (current) | SA Pre-Qual AI Agent (planned)

---

## Purpose

Run a structured, warm pre-qualification conversation with every prospect who has booked a Strength Assessment. The goal is to gather the information needed to:

1. Prepare the trainer for a high-quality, personalised session
2. Anchor the prospect's commitment before they walk through the door
3. Identify red flags early — price sensitivity, low commitment, or poor fit — and escalate to admin
4. Pre-frame the assessment so the prospect arrives with the right expectations

This conversation happens via SMS between booking confirmation and the session. It is guided, not scripted — tone should always feel personal and human.

---

## Outcomes This SOP Delivers

- No surprises in the assessment
- Higher show-up rate
- Clear trainer positioning
- Smoother close
- Stronger client-trainer trust
- Reduced tyre-kickers

---

## Conversation Structure Overview

```
Stage 1 — Acknowledge & Anchor
Stage 2 — Context Gathering
  A. Goals & Motivation
  B. Medical (conditional — only if disclosed)
  C. Injury (conditional — only if disclosed)
  D. Exercise History
  E. Support Preference
  F. Social Proof
  G. Obstacles & Readiness Check
Stage 3 — Pre-Frame the Assessment
Stage 4 — Trainer Summary (internal — not sent to prospect)
```

## Shared Completion Map

Use this map to locate the prospect's current position before every reply. It defines when each existing stage is complete without turning the conversation into a rigid questionnaire.

| Stage | Complete when |
|---|---|
| Stage 1: Acknowledge & Anchor | The prospect's previous answer has been acknowledged specifically, and any vague opening information has been clarified |
| Stage 2A: Goals & Motivation | The primary goal, desired outcome, reason it matters now, and commitment score are clear |
| Stage 2B: Medical | If a medical condition was disclosed, relevant medication effects and current symptom status have been clarified |
| Stage 2C: Injury | The trainer has enough actionable context to personalise the assessment safely. For recurring niggles, this normally includes what happened and when, the attempted activity, symptom pattern and location, the prospect's main concern, and any known professional restrictions or unresolved safety red flags |
| Stage 2D: Exercise History | Current or previous strength training, training structure, PT experience, group experience, and last consistent training period are known |
| Stage 2E: Support Preference | Preference for 1:1 support, a group environment, or a combination is known |
| Stage 2F: Social Proof | A relevant success story has been sent once sufficient context is available |
| Stage 2G: Obstacles & Readiness | Anything that could prevent the prospect from starting has been surfaced, and readiness is understood |
| Stage 3: Pre-Frame | The prospect understands what the Strength Assessment is and what happens next |
| Stage 4: Trainer Summary | The internal trainer brief has been completed |

## Before Every Reply: Locate the Current Stage

Any trained team member may continue the conversation. The conversation history is the shared state that determines what comes next.

Before sending a message:

1. Refresh and read the complete conversation from the original goals reply.
2. Identify which stage requirements have already been answered naturally.
3. Check whether the latest question is still waiting for the prospect's answer.
4. If another team member has progressed the conversation, reassess it from the newest message.
5. If the newest message introduces a specific goal, injury or concern that needs connected clarification, complete that line of questioning while it is current.
6. Then continue from the first incomplete requirement.
7. Acknowledge the prospect's previous answer before asking the next one or two connected questions.
8. Never repeat an answered question, restart the sequence, or jump ahead merely because a different team member is responding.

The first-incomplete-requirement rule protects completeness, but it is not a reason to make the conversation feel mechanical. A current connected clarification may be completed first, provided the responder records and returns to the earlier outstanding requirement before Stage 2 is marked complete.

## Stage 1 — Acknowledge & Anchor

**Purpose:** Build rapport and authority before any questioning begins.

**What to do:**
- Acknowledge what they've shared in their goals reply — be specific, not generic
- Highlight the area the Strength Assessment will provide clarity on for their specific situation
- When joining after an automated acknowledgement or another team member's reply, introduce yourself and explain your role in one sentence
- Lead with the outcome the prospect is excited about, then connect it to why the next clarification matters
- If their reply is vague or missing key context, ask one clarifying question before moving on
- Express genuine excitement for them coming in

**What not to do:**
- Don't skip straight to questions
- Don't give generic acknowledgement ("That's great!") — reference what they actually said
- Don't open with more than one question at a time

**Example:**
> "Hi [Name], thanks so much for sharing that — the fact that you've been feeling weak and want to build real strength is exactly what we work with every day. Your assessment is going to give us a really clear picture of where you're starting from and what's going to make the biggest difference for you. Looking forward to it."

**Shared-team handoff example:**

> "Hi [Name], I'm Eve. I help gather information for our trainers so they can completely personalise your Strength Assessment."

This introduction explains the responder's contribution without implying that one person owns the conversation. Do not reintroduce yourself in every message.

## Stage 2 — Context Gathering

These are guided clarification questions, not an interrogation. Work through each section in order. Skip sections that have already been answered naturally. Never ask more than one or two questions per message.

---

### A. Goals & Motivation

The initial goal question is already automated (SMS Goals in the workflow). Use the reply to guide the following clarifications:

| Question | When to ask |
|---|---|
| "Of those goals you mentioned, which one is the most important to you right now?" | When multiple goals are mentioned |
| "Are you wanting to lose more than 5kg or less than 5kg?" | When weight loss is mentioned |
| "Is there anything that's making you feel weak right now, or is the want to gain strength coming from something else?" | When strength is mentioned but motivation isn't clear |
| "What does your dream body actually look like? What would need to happen to get there?" | When goal is vague (e.g. "grow my glutes") — see Red Flags |
| "What has made this important enough for you to do something about it now?" | Always, unless the reason it matters now is already clear from the conversation |
| "On a scale of 1–10, how committed are you to changing this right now?" | Always, after the goal and motivation are clear and before moving to medical/injury |

**Completion criteria:** Do not move on until the primary goal, desired outcome, reason it matters now, and commitment score are clear. If the prospect has already explained her motivation naturally, acknowledge it rather than asking again.

---

### B. Medical

**Only ask if a medical condition is disclosed.** Do not proactively screen for medical conditions.

| Question | Purpose |
|---|---|
| "Are you on any medication for [condition] that affects your blood pressure, heart rate or anything else during physical activity?" | Safety — trainer needs to know |
| "Are the symptoms of [condition] currently flared up, or have you got it under control?" | Determines session modification needs |

---

### C. Injury

**Only ask if an injury is disclosed.**

| Question | Purpose |
|---|---|
| "What originally happened, and roughly when did it occur?" | Injury context and timing |
| "Are there any movements you've been told to avoid or work around?" | Program modification |
| "Is it currently painful or flared up?" | Current assessment modification needs |
| "Are there any movements that regularly aggravate it?" | Current functional limitation |
| "What type of training have you tried returning to?" | Identifies the attempted return-to-training pattern |
| "Where do you feel the niggle: front, side, back or all over?" | Gives the trainer useful symptom-location context without diagnosing |
| "How many times have you tried returning to training?" | Use when repeated return attempts are mentioned and the pattern is unclear |

#### Enough-Information Threshold

Pre-qualification is not an injury consultation. Stop asking injury questions once the trainer has enough information to understand the history, tailor the assessment and identify whether a safety clarification is required.

Stage 2C is normally complete when the following relevant information is known:

- What happened and approximately when
- The activity or return-to-training attempt that triggered the issue
- The recurring symptom pattern and location
- The prospect's main concern about training
- Any known professional restrictions or unresolved safety red flags

Continue deeper only when current pain or a flare-up, unexplained symptoms, a recent or significant injury, a professional restriction, or another safety issue remains unclear. Do not keep collecting detail merely because another injury question exists in this SOP.

When multiple injuries are disclosed, clarify each one without sending a long list of questions. Split the conversation across messages.

**First message:**

> "Could you tell me what originally happened with your [injury areas], and roughly when each injury occurred?"

**Second message:**

> "Are any of those areas currently painful or flared up? Are there movements you've been told to avoid or that regularly aggravate them?"

If an injury is recent, significant, or currently limiting, ask whether a health professional has provided exercise restrictions. Do not diagnose, interpret medical information, or promise that training will resolve the injury.

**Example response when injury is disclosed:**
> "Ok great, I'll pass that on to your trainer. They'll be able to look for movements you're unable to do, along with movements we can do to strengthen the area."

**Example when an aspirational goal and recurring niggle are disclosed:**

> "Amazing, aerials and pole are super cool, but you'll need some serious shoulder stability for that. When you say you get niggles every time you return, what type of training have you tried going back to, and where do you feel the niggles: at the front, side or back of your shoulders, or more all over?"

#### Confidence-Building Transition

When Stage 2C is trainer-actionable, close the injury branch before moving to Exercise History:

1. Acknowledge the prospect's concern and show that it makes sense.
2. Offer a tentative, non-diagnostic training explanation using words such as `could` or `may`.
3. Recognise an observation from a trusted allied-health or manual-therapy provider as useful context without independently confirming the diagnosis.
4. State what will be added to the trainer's notes or observed during the assessment, then ask the first Stage 2D question.

Use approved life-stage context only when it is relevant and already known from the conversation or contact record. This is a confidence-building bridge, not permission to diagnose, contradict another practitioner or promise that training will resolve the condition.

**Example:**

> "Okay, that makes sense: it sounds like there could be a combination of mobility, strength and control factors involved, particularly around how your shoulder blades and shoulders are working together. The thoracic outlet observation from your massage therapist is really useful context. Shoulders can also be more susceptible to persistent issues as we enter perimenopause, so I'll note that for your trainer to look at your scapular function and overall shoulder movement during the assessment. Have you ever followed a structured, progressive strength-training program using barbells and dumbbells?"

---

### D. Exercise History

Ask in this order — move on once each is answered:

1. "Have you ever followed a structured, progressive strength-training program using barbells or dumbbells?"
2. If yes: "Was that with a coach or program, or was it more self-guided?"
3. "Are you doing that currently? If not, when was the last time you trained consistently?"
4. "Have you done 1:1 personal training before?"
5. "Have you trained in a group environment before?"

---

### E. Support Preference

**Question:** "Do you think you need 1:1 support, are you looking for a group environment, or a bit of both?"

**Purpose:**
- Identifies confidence level
- Surfaces support expectations
- Signals potential premium readiness (PT vs SGPT)

**Important:** Do not promise a structure or price yet. This is information gathering only.

**Follow-up if they express a preference:** "And have you done [1:1 PT / group training] before?" — this helps determine whether they understand the commitment level (time and cost) that their preference implies.

---

### F. Social Proof

Once context gathering is mostly complete, send a success story that mirrors their goals or obstacles.

**Script:**
> "Okay great, I'll note that for your trainer. I thought I'd send you this — have a read, it's one of our success stories from a client who [mirror their goal / obstacle]."

**What to send:** A link to the relevant social proof page (see Social Proof Page Index below). Match the story to their primary goal and life stage where possible.

> **Note:** Social proof pages are SEO/AIO/GEO-optimised web pages, each featuring one member story. They serve both the pre-qual conversation and organic search. See `outputs/systems/social-proof-pages.md` (to be created) for the full index of available pages and their matching goal/demographic tags.

---

### G. Obstacles & Readiness Check

**Question:** "If you're offered a spot after your strength assessment, would you be in a position to get started — or is there anything coming up that might stop you?"

**Purpose:** Surfaces genuine objections before the session so the trainer is prepared, and identifies price or commitment barriers early.

---

## Stage 3 — Pre-Frame the Assessment

After context gathering is complete, reinforce what the assessment actually is. This protects trainer time and increases perceived value.

**Script (adapt naturally — don't send verbatim):**
> "Just so you know what to expect — the strength assessment isn't a casual gym tour or a free trial. It's a structured evaluation where we measure your current strength, mobility and movement quality. We'll show you exactly where you're strong and what needs attention. From there, if we believe we can help you, we'll map out your next step."

## Red Flags & Response Guides

These are patterns that indicate a prospect may be a poor fit, low commitment, or require pricing disclosure before the session proceeds. Handle carefully — escalate to admin where noted.

---

### Red Flag 1 — Recently Started Another Exercise Modality

**Signal:** "I just started Pilates / yoga / CrossFit / another gym a few weeks ago."

**Why it matters:** We need to confirm they have the time and willingness to commit to at least 3x per week at The Evolved to see results. Otherwise we're competing with their existing activity.

**Response:**
> "Okay, that sounds great and I hear that you're motivated. Do you see yourself having the time in the week to be able to strength train at least three times per week to get [their goal]?"

---

### Red Flag 2 — Vague or Intangible Goal

**Signal:** Goal lacks specificity — e.g. "grow my glutes", "get fit", "tone up", "just feel better."

**Why it matters:** Vague goals don't anchor commitment. If they can't articulate what success looks like, they're harder to close and harder to retain.

**Response:**
> "Earlier you mentioned [vague goal] — what does your dream body actually look like? What would need to happen to feel like you'd achieved it?"

Push for a specific, visual or felt outcome before moving on.

---

### Red Flag 3 — Price Sensitivity

**Signal:** Prospect says affordability matters, is unsure whether the service fits her budget, or implies that cost may prevent her from proceeding. A general request for pricing without an affordability concern follows the Pricing Decision Rule instead.

**Why it matters:** This is a strong disqualifier. The Evolved is a premium service. Price-sensitive prospects who discover cost at the assessment stage waste trainer time.

**Response:**
> "No worries — we are a premium service and our pricing reflects that. At minimum you'd be looking to spend at least $99 per week for an effective program to reach your goals. Is that within your budget?"

This is an early qualification exception. Do not use it as a reason to introduce pricing when the prospect has not raised affordability.

**If budget doesn't work:**
- Do not cancel the appointment
- Escalate to admin immediately: flag the conversation with a note that the prospect raised price sensitivity and the outcome of the pricing disclosure
- Admin decides whether to proceed or cancel

### Pricing Decision Rule

Pricing is not a standard stage of pre-qualification. Do not introduce it when the prospect has not raised it.

| Situation | Correct action |
|---|---|
| Price has not been mentioned | Do not introduce pricing |
| Prospect asks generally about price before context gathering is complete | Acknowledge the question, explain that the relevant guide depends on the context being gathered, and continue from the next incomplete requirement |
| Prospect expresses genuine affordability concern | Use the existing minimum-$99 price-sensitivity response and record the outcome |
| Context gathering is complete and the prospect still wants pricing | Provide the approved category range or minimum without itemising package names and prices |
| Prospect asks what an amount already mentioned includes | Answer that inclusion question accurately, correct any misunderstanding, and continue from the next incomplete requirement |
| The appropriate pricing information is unclear | Pause and escalate to Peter |

Never present the full package-price ladder during pre-qualification. The assessment determines the specific recommendation.

**Early general price question:**

> "Absolutely, I can give you a useful guide once I understand what level of support may apply to you. I don't want to give you a number without enough context and leave you comparing options that may not be appropriate."

Connect the next sentence to the first incomplete requirement. For example:

> "You mentioned injuries to your lower back, shoulder and wrist. What originally happened with each one, and roughly when?"

**Price-ready response:**

> "Thanks, that gives me a much clearer picture. Our coached membership options range from $69 to $149 per week, depending on the level of support involved."

> "Your Strength Assessment is where the coach determines which level of support is appropriate."

If standalone PT is relevant, provide its approved category range separately. Do not turn the conversation into a package-by-package presentation.

## Conversation Rules

These apply whether the conversation is run by admin or the AI agent:

1. **One or two questions per message maximum.** Never send a list of questions at once.
2. **Always reference what they said.** Don't ask a question in isolation — connect it to their previous answer.
3. **Never interrogate.** This is a guided, warm conversation. If someone doesn't answer a question, gently follow up once, then move on.
4. **Never promise a specific structure, package, or outcome** before the assessment. Discuss pricing only under the Pricing Decision Rule.
5. **Australian English.** No emojis in bot messages. Clean, professional, warm.
6. **SMS length.** Keep each message to 2–4 sentences. Long messages get ignored.
7. **Escalate to admin** for: price sensitivity red flags, unusual medical disclosures, angry or confused prospects, any situation outside normal script.
8. **If unsure — pause and ask Peter.** See Unsure Escalation Protocol below.
9. **Read before replying.** Use the shared completion map and continue from the first incomplete requirement.
10. **Reassess fresh activity.** If another team member has replied since the task was created, work from the newest message rather than the task's original wording.
11. **Do not reveal unprompted pricing.** Price is discussed only when directly asked or when genuine price sensitivity surfaces.
12. **Do not present package prices.** Pre-qualification uses an approved category range or minimum, never the full package-price ladder.
13. **Stop when the information is trainer-actionable.** Do not turn pre-qualification into an injury consultation or collect details that will not change preparation or safety.
14. **Build confidence without diagnosing.** Acknowledge trusted practitioner input, use only tentative training language and make the resulting trainer note or assessment focus visible to the prospect.

## Conversation Repair Protocol

Use this when the prospect says she has already answered a question, or when a previous reply overlooked information already in the thread.

1. Acknowledge the mistake.
2. Apologise once without becoming defensive.
3. Demonstrate that the information has now been read by referencing it accurately.
4. Answer any current direct question that is appropriate to answer at that point.
5. Continue from the next incomplete requirement.

**Example:**

> "You're right, you had already included those details, and I'm sorry we asked you to repeat yourself. I can see you're looking to [goal], and that you've mentioned [relevant context]."

Do not ask the prospect to restate information simply because a different team member has entered the conversation.

## Unsure Escalation Protocol

When the AI agent encounters a situation not covered by this SOP — an unexpected response, an unusual objection, an edge case, or anything it is not confident handling — it must not guess or improvise. It pauses, escalates, and waits.

### Escalation Flow

```
1. PAUSE — do not respond to the prospect yet
2. NOTIFY Peter via Discord with:
   - Contact name and conversation context
   - The prospect's message that triggered the uncertainty
   - What the agent was unsure about
   - 2–3 suggested response options (if possible)
3. WAIT for Peter's reply in Discord
4. DRAFT new Coaching Note — write a new rule based on Peter's guidance
   using the standard Coaching Notes format
5. CONFIRM — send the draft rule to Peter for approval before saving
6. SAVE — write the confirmed rule to the Coaching Notes section of this file
7. RESPOND — send the approved reply to the prospect
```

### Holding Message (sent to prospect while paused)

If a reply is delayed more than a few minutes during business hours, send:

> "Thanks for that — just checking in with the team on the best way to support you here. Won't be a moment."

If outside business hours, do not send a holding message — reply the next morning with the approved response.

### What counts as "unsure"

- The prospect raises something not mentioned anywhere in this SOP
- The prospect's response is ambiguous and multiple interpretations are possible
- The prospect expresses strong emotion (frustration, anxiety, grief) that requires a sensitive response
- The prospect asks a specific question about training, medical advice, or pricing that isn't covered in the Pricing Reference
- The agent's confidence in its intended response is below a defined threshold

### What does not require escalation

- Any situation already covered by the SOP stages, Red Flags, or Coaching Notes
- Standard clarification questions within Stages 1–3
- Sending social proof links from the index
- Completing the conversation and generating the trainer brief

**Pattern:** A task or notification was created before another team member sent a newer reply.

### Aspirational Goal With Recurring Niggles on Return to Training

**Pattern:** The prospect names an exciting performance goal and says niggles recur whenever she tries to resume training.

**Signal:** The goal is clear, but the attempted training, recurrence pattern or symptom location is not.

**Best response found:** Introduce yourself if joining after automation, acknowledge the aspirational goal with genuine enthusiasm, connect it to the relevant physical quality, then ask what training she has tried returning to and where the niggle is felt. Ask how many return attempts there have been only if that pattern remains unclear; do not diagnose the symptom or promise that training will resolve it.

**Added:** 2026-07-23

**Added by:** Peter Brown

### Close the Injury Branch Once It Is Trainer-Actionable

**Pattern:** The prospect has provided enough injury context for the trainer, but additional injury questions remain available in the SOP.

**Signal:** The history, attempted activity, symptom pattern and location, main concern, and relevant professional context are clear, with no unresolved safety issue that requires another pre-assessment question.

**Best response found:** Close the branch by acknowledging the concern, offering a tentative non-diagnostic training explanation, respecting any trusted practitioner observation and naming what will be included in the trainer's assessment focus. Then move directly to the structured progressive-strength question in Stage 2D instead of continuing to interrogate the injury.

**Added:** 2026-07-23
