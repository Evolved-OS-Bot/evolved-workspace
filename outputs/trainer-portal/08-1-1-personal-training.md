### Lesson 2: Session Delivery

**Session duration:** 30–60 minutes depending on the client's program

**Before the session:**
- Review goals, progress, and previous session notes in Trainerize
- Have a minimum 12-week program periodised and ready for your client at all times
- Prepare equipment and session layout
- Greet the client promptly: quick verbal check-in on energy, mood, injuries

**Warm-Up (5–10 minutes):**
- Dynamic stretches, mobility, activation, and/or light cardio tailored to the program
- Engage key muscle groups and assess movement patterns

**Main Session (30–60 minutes):**
- Deliver the Trainerize program
- **Record all sessions in Trainerize at the time of completion**
- Demonstrate each exercise with proper form before the client begins
- Provide real-time feedback and cues throughout
- You may progress, regress, or change any exercise at any point in the session: as long as the substitute remains in the same movement pattern
- If you change an exercise, swap it in the Trainerize workout at the time of recording; all substitutions must be documented

**Cool-Down (5–10 minutes):**
- Static stretches and breathing exercises
- Recap session highlights and discuss next steps

**Track everything:** training data, biometrics, photos. Note achievements and areas for improvement each session.

### Lesson 3: Breathing & Bracing

Breathing and bracing is a foundational coaching expectation at The Evolved. It is taught in every Intro Session, and in 1:1 PT, you are responsible for reinforcing and refining it.

**Why it matters**

Breathing in and bracing before a lift creates intra-abdominal pressure (IAP): a pressurised cylinder of support formed by the diaphragm, pelvic floor, abdominal wall, and spinal extensors. This pressure reduces spinal shear forces, increases lumbar stability, and enables greater force transfer. Research confirms it can increase 1RM squat performance by an average of 12%.

**The standard technique**

Inhale and brace before initiating the lift. Maintain the brace through the effort. Exhale at the top once the repetition is complete.

Coaching cue: *"Big breath in, fill your belly, hold it tight: then lift."*

**Your expectation in PT sessions:** every working set, every exercise under load. You do not need to cue it every rep for experienced members, but if it breaks down under heavier loads, intervene immediately. A member lifting without a brace is a risk, not just a coaching miss.

**The pelvic floor modification**

For women with pelvic floor symptoms, leaking, heaviness, or discomfort during loading, intense bracing and breath-holding places additional downward pressure on the pelvic floor and can exacerbate symptoms.

The evidence-based modification is **"blow as you go"**: exhale through the exertion phase instead of holding the breath. This reduces peak pelvic floor loading while maintaining adequate spinal support.

Apply this for:
- Postpartum members
- Members in perimenopause or post-menopause
- Any member who reports pelvic floor symptoms during or after loaded exercise

**Referral rule:** If a member reports persistent symptoms, refer them to a women's health physiotherapist and document in Trainerize. Notify management via email.

### Lesson 4: Coaching with PRIME

PRIME applies to every strength training session at The Evolved: Sculpt & Strength, Build & Balance, and 1:1 PT. It is not a group class tool. It is the coaching lens every Evolved trainer uses whenever they are on the floor with a member under load.

**In SGPT (group classes), coaching is delivered outside in:**
- Position yourself so your back is never turned to any member
- Have all members start and stop working sets at the same time: separated into groups to manage rest/work timing and use the space efficiently
- Move through the group: assess Posture → assess Range of Motion → Identify the limiting factor → deliver the cue (the touch point) → move to the next member

**In 1:1 PT, the same process applies, with one difference:**

Instead of moving to the next member after delivering a cue, you stay with the client. Give the cue → observe the next rep → assess again → repeat. The 1:1 format allows you to work through multiple iterations of the same correction within a single set, which is not possible in a group of 15.

This is where 1:1 PT produces outcomes that SGPT cannot. Use the time.

**The Performance Warm-Up applies to all strength training sessions.** Run it before the main lift in every 1:1 PT session. It is not optional. It primes the nervous system, establishes the working weight, and gives you a full set at 20%, 60%, and 100% to observe the client's movement pattern before the working sets begin. Use those warm-up sets to run through P → R → I before the first working set starts.

### Lesson 5: The Program: Setting Client Expectations

The Evolved 1:1 Periodised Program is a 12-month program pre-built in Trainerize in three versions: 1, 2, or 3 sessions per week. Assign the correct version before the client's first session.

When a new 1:1 PT client begins their program in Trainerize, your job is not just to deliver it: it is to explain it. A client who understands the structure of their program is more likely to stay consistent, trust the process during harder phases, and not request changes mid-block.

**The conversation to have in the first session:**

Explain the 13-week block structure:

| Phase | Weeks | Rep Range | What it feels like |
|---|---|---|---|
| Accumulation | 1–4 | 10–12 RM | Higher volume, manageable weight: building the base |
| Intensification | 5–8 | 8–10 RM | Weight increases, volume drops slightly: getting harder |
| Realisation | 9–12 | 6–8 RM | Heaviest loads, lowest reps: strength is expressed |
| Deload & Testing | 13 | Reduced load | Recovery and reassessment: you'll feel fresher than you expect |

*Example script:*

> *"The program is built in 13-week blocks. The first four weeks might feel like you're not going as heavy as you could, that's intentional. We're building your base and getting your technique locked in at volume. By weeks nine to twelve, you'll be lifting loads that would have felt impossible in week one. Week thirteen we deload, your body consolidates the adaptation. Then we go again."*

**Why this matters:**

Members who don't understand periodisation often push for more intensity in weeks 1–4, or feel "bored" with higher-rep work. Setting expectations in the first session prevents this. The phases only work when they're followed. Your job is to explain them well enough that the client trusts the system.
