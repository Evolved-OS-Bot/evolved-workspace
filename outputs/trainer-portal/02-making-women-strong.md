## Lessons

### Lesson 1: Why Women Need Strength

Most women who walk through our door have spent years doing cardio, low-load circuits, or group fitness classes that felt challenging but didn't produce lasting results. That's not their fault: it's the industry failing them.

The Evolved exists to correct it.

**The problem: sarcopenia**

From her 30s onward, a woman begins losing muscle mass. Without an adequate training stimulus, she loses **3–8% per decade**. Through menopause, that rate accelerates.

This isn't just a cosmetic problem. Muscle loss compounds into:

- A slower metabolic rate: making body composition harder to manage year on year
- Decreased bone density: increasing fracture risk and osteoporosis
- Hormonal disruption: muscle tissue plays a direct role in hormonal regulation
- Loss of functional independence: the ability to carry, move, lift, and live without assistance in the final decades of life

**The solution: strength training**

Structured strength training at the right parameters is the single most effective evidence-based intervention. Not yoga. Not spin. Not walking. Strength: applied consistently, with progressive overload, at the right intensity.

This is why The Evolved is a strength-first gym. Every decision we make, programming, class design, onboarding, coaching, is built around this.

**As a trainer at The Evolved, you are not just delivering sessions. You are intervening in the long-term health trajectory of every woman you work with.** That is the weight of this role. Take it seriously.

**The Eight Physical Assets of Longevity**

The Evolved Standards Framework is built around eight physical assets. Every exercise we program and every standard we set exists because it measures one or more of these:

| Asset | What it represents |
|---|---|
| **1. Strength** | The foundation of physical capability: everything else depends on it |
| **2. Muscle Mass** | The organ of longevity: drives metabolism, bone density, and hormonal health |
| **3. Bone Density** | Bone responds to load. Strength training is the most accessible intervention for osteoporosis prevention |
| **4. Mobility** | Usable range of motion under control. Without it, strength cannot be expressed |
| **5. Balance & Coordination** | Most falls happen in single-leg stance. This asset is what prevents them |
| **6. Work Capacity** | The ability to sustain output. Walking airports, keeping up with grandchildren, exploring cities |
| **7. Independence** | The ultimate outcome: the ability to remain capable and self-sufficient through every decade |
| **8. Grip Strength & Carrying Capacity** | One of the strongest predictors of longevity, frailty, and loss of independence in research literature |

When you understand these eight assets, you stop coaching exercises and start coaching outcomes. Every session is an investment in one or more of these. That is the lens every Evolved trainer applies.

Asset 8 deserves special attention. Grip strength and carrying capacity, measured by our **Walk of Independence**, is one of the most consistent longevity biomarkers in the research. Multiple large-scale studies have found low grip strength to be a stronger predictor of cardiovascular mortality than systolic blood pressure. A woman who cannot carry 75% of her bodyweight for 60 seconds is not failing a gym test. She is demonstrating a systemic deficit that, if left unaddressed, will quietly erode her physical independence over the years ahead.

This is not fitness industry language. This is longevity science. Coaches at The Evolved carry this understanding into every session.

### Lesson 2: Key Performance Habits

The Key Performance Habits (KPH) are the two non-negotiable standards that every trainer applies across every client, every session. They are the minimum threshold for producing results.

**KPH 1: Training Intensity**

Women must lift with adequate intensity **2–3 times per week** to achieve optimal results.

| Parameter | Target |
|---|---|
| Rep range | 6–12 RM |
| Intensity | 70–85% of 1RM |
| Volume | 6–8 sets per session |

Below this threshold, a session feels like exercise but doesn't produce the physiological stimulus needed for adaptation. Clients may feel like they're working hard, but without the right load, results don't follow.

**KPH 2: Movement Pattern Coverage**

Every week, sessions must cover all 6 foundational movement patterns at least once:

**Squat · Hinge · Lunge · Push · Pull · Carry**

These patterns map to how the body moves in real life. Together, they ensure balanced development, reduce injury risk, and build the functional strength that serves women across decades, not just in the gym.

Intensity without breadth creates imbalance. Breadth without intensity creates activity. Both together create results.

**Every client you work with must meet these standards.** It is your job to ensure they do.

### Lesson 3: Applying KPH and Holding the Standard

Knowing the KPH is straightforward. Holding them when a client pushes back is where most trainers fall short.

**How KPH is applied across membership types**

| Membership | How KPH is applied |
|---|---|
| **Sculpt & Strength** | KPH applies. Members attending 2–3 classes per week meet minimum KPH if classes are delivered to standard |
| **Build & Balance** | KPH applies. Same standard as Sculpt & Strength |
| **Fast Track (S&S + PT)** | The 1:1 session must also meet intensity and movement criteria. If the client has NOT attended all 3 S&S sessions that week, use a Sculpt & Strength workout from Trainerize (Kettlebell icon). If the client HAS completed all 3 sessions, choose one of: a Build & Balance workout, a Metabolic Burn or Hybrid Fitness Racing workout, or a custom workout of the day targeting a movement they are struggling with or a standard they are lagging on. A custom workout of the day must be built at least 24 hours in advance and recorded in the client's Trainerize account before the session. |
| **PT only: 2+ sessions/week** | Base all sessions on the Evolved 1:1 Periodised Program. Hit intensity and movement benchmarks every session |
| **PT only: 1 session/week** | Follow the 1:1 Periodised Program. Hit intensity and movement benchmarks |
| **Metabolic Burn / Hybrid Racing only** | KPH does **not** apply to ICE classes. If a member attends only these classes, they are not meeting the strength minimum. Educate and guide toward adding strength sessions |

**When a client wants something else**

A client will sometimes ask for cardio, HIIT, or a "lighter" session. This is the moment that defines you as a trainer.

Your role is not to give clients what they want in the moment: it is to deliver what they need to reach their goals.

When this happens:

1. **Acknowledge**: *"I hear you"*
2. **Educate**: explain the sarcopenia risk, the KPH threshold, and why strength is the most effective intervention for their goal
3. **Hold the standard**: our expertise is strength-based transformation. That doesn't change based on a client's mood

> *"Our specialty is strength-based transformation. The research shows that for your goals, [insert their goal], strength training produces better long-term results than cardio alone. Can we try it our way and see how it feels?"*

**A trainer who softens the standard to keep a client comfortable is not serving that client: they are failing them.**

### Lesson 4: The PRIME Framework

PRIME is how every coach at The Evolved delivers a session. It is not a checklist: it is a coaching lens. A decision-making framework that ensures consistent outcomes regardless of which coach is on the floor.

**Why PRIME exists**

The challenge at The Evolved was never programming. The challenge was: how do we deliver a world-class strength session to 15 women regardless of which coach is standing on the floor?

Most gyms coach exercises. PRIME coaches **movement quality, observation, and decision-making.**

Every coach using PRIME is looking at the same things, asking the same questions, making decisions the same way. That consistency is what produces results.

**The framework**

| Stage | Coach Question |
|---|---|
| **P: Posture** | Are they in the right position? |
| **R: Range of Motion** | Can they access the movement safely? |
| **I: Identify Areas of Improvement** | What is the limiting factor? |
| **M: Measure & Monitor** | How do we know they're improving? |
| **E: Evaluate & Evolve** | What does their performance show, and what is the next progression? |

**P: Posture**
The foundation. Position determines performance. Before anything else: is the spine neutral? Is the pelvis controlled? Are the shoulders stable? Never progress past Posture until the position is owned.

**R: Range of Motion**
Not flexibility for its own sake, usable range. Can they squat to depth? Can they hinge with a neutral spine? Work within their available range and expand it progressively. Never force range, earn it.

**I: Identify Areas of Improvement**
This is where coaching becomes individual. Two women doing the same exercise may have completely different limiting factors: one is limited by mobility, another by confidence under load, another by coordination. Observe first. Diagnose before you intervene.

**M: Measure & Monitor**
Progress must be visible. Track load, reps, attendance, assessment scores. Record everything in Trainerize every session. If it isn't tracked, it isn't coached.

**E: Evaluate & Evolve**
First evaluate the member's performance against posture, range, competency, load, reps, and movement quality. Then decide whether to consolidate, regress, or evolve the movement.

The member earns progression. Once posture is owned, range is accessible, and competency is demonstrated consistently: progress load, complexity, or volume. Premature progression produces injury, not adaptation.

**Where PRIME fits**

```
Assessment → Programming → PRIME → Results
```

Assessment tells you what needs improving. Programming tells you which exercises will improve it. PRIME tells you how to coach it in real time. It is the delivery engine of the Evolved Method.

> *PRIME ensures every coaching decision at The Evolved follows a consistent process. Coaches do not simply instruct exercises. They observe posture, assess range, identify limitations, measure progress, evaluate performance, and evolve the member toward greater strength, health, and independence.*
