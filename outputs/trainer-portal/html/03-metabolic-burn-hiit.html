Class Overview

  • Duration: 60 minutes
  • Capacity: 15 members
  • Format: Interval-based: work sets at high intensity followed by structured rest breaks
  • Equipment: Kettlebells, dumbbells, resistance bands, mats

Metabolic Burn is fast-paced, energetic, and adaptable to all fitness levels. Your energy sets the tone: arrive ready to bring it.

Where Metabolic Burn Fits in the Evolved Method

Metabolic Burn operates under The Evolved's Intense Cardio Exercise (ICE) framework: a separate standard from the strength KPH.

What ICE Means

  • Intensity of RPE 7 or higher throughout the session
  • Interval structure: work sets push intensity high, rest breaks allow partial recovery, then repeat: members should not be fully recovering before the next work set
  • Work-to-rest ratio of approximately 1:1 to 1:1.5

ICE Is Complementary to Strength: Not a Substitute

Strength training (Sculpt & Strength, Build & Balance, 1:1 PT) builds the physiological foundation: muscle mass, bone density, hormonal health, and progressive relative strength. This requires a periodised program, heavier loads, and a 1:3–1:4 work-to-rest ratio that allows full neuromuscular recovery between sets.

Metabolic Burn develops Work Capacity, the ability to sustain output, which is one of the Eight Physical Assets of Longevity. But it cannot replicate the progressive loading structure that drives the strength adaptation.

The trainer's role: Members who attend only Metabolic Burn sessions are not meeting the strength KPH. If you notice this pattern, from Trainerize records or conversation, educate them and encourage them to add strength sessions.

ICE is most beneficial once the strength habit is established.

Delivering the Session

Member Arrival

  • Greet everyone warmly as they arrive
  • Overview of the session: address any questions
  • Brief safety and equipment briefing before starting
  • Identify emergency exits and first aid kit location

Warm-Up (5–10 minutes)

  • Use the prescribed warm-up in Trainerize
  • Give alternatives within your scope for individual mobility needs
  • If unsure of an alternative: deliver what's prescribed, apologise, ask management ASAP (provide client name and area of immobility)

Main Session (20–30 minutes)

  • Follow the Trainerize workout: adhere to sets, timing, and movement patterns
  • Give progressions or regressions within your scope
  • If unsure: deliver what's prescribed, apologise, ask management ASAP
  • Monitor every participant for proper form throughout high-intensity intervals
  • Progressions: jump squats, heavier loads, time-based challenges
  • Regressions: bodyweight squats, reduced range of motion, lower intensity

Cool-Down (5–10 minutes)

  • Use the prescribed cool-down from Trainerize

Monitoring Intensity

Your job in a Metabolic Burn class is to make sure every member is getting an effective workout. The ICE framework requires RPE 7+: it is your responsibility to push members toward that, not simply permit them to move at their own pace.

What an Effective Metabolic Burn Looks Like

  • Members are sweating visibly by the midpoint of the session
  • Huffing and puffing, especially during and immediately after work sets
  • By the end of a work set, members should not be comfortable: they should be working to recover
  • Members are not fully recovering before the next work set begins: incomplete recovery between sets is part of the design
  • Red faces, elevated breathing rate, effort visible in body language

What to Watch For

  • Members going through the motions at low intensity: intervene with encouragement and specific cues to lift effort
  • Anyone who looks faint, pale, or overly distressed: offer water or glucose if needed, and pull them back if necessary
  • Members who need to sit down mid-session: check in, allow a brief recovery, then encourage them back in

Pushing vs protecting: Your default position is to push members harder, not hold them back: encouraging language, calling out effort, and leading from the front with your own energy are your primary tools. That said, members can sometimes push themselves too hard.

If a member looks faint, pale, or overly distressed: slow them down immediately, get them a glass of water, and offer fast-absorbing carbohydrates like Powerade to support recovery.

When this happens, acknowledge their effort positively: pushing that hard is a sign of real commitment. Reassure them that it doesn't happen every session, and let them know that next session you'll use it as a reference point to calibrate their intensity better.

How you handle the moment is what keeps members coming back to this type of training.

Post-Session & Troubleshooting

Farewell

  • Tell everyone what a great job they did
  • Congratulate 2–3 members individually: identify 1 win or 1 improvement and offer a class recommendation or complimentary 1:1 PT session. To action a complimentary session: name one specific time you are already paid to be in the gym and have free time, do not offer to the same member more than once per 6 months, and book it on the spot in Lead Connector (the gym's CRM and booking system).
  • Ask when they're coming in next

Clean-Up

  • Wipe down and sanitise all equipment
  • Check the timetable and set the gym up for the next class. The last class of the day sets up for the first class of the following morning. There is always a next class.
  • Check for forgotten belongings: store in lost and found
  • Wash cool towels in bacterial wash: roll and return to fridge
  • Ensure toilet is closed

Record

  • Record attendance in Trainerize
  • Email info@theevolvedgym.com.au with any session issues
