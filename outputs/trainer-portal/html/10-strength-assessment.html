  2. Physical Assessment

The Consultation

First Impression

This space can feel hard to find and intimidating. A big, warm smile fixes that immediately.

Be the coach they hoped they'd meet.

Pre-Frame the Session

"Today I'll be working with you to find your starting point. By the end you'll know exactly where you're strong, what needs strengthening, and how long it will take to reach your goals. At the end, if I think we can help you, I'll ask: and you can say yes or no. Either answer is fine. Does that sound fair?"

Wait for a yes. Then ask for height and weight and take a seat.

The Consultation is information gathering, not coaching. Go deeper on what you already know. Do not offer advice or recommendations.

9 Consultation Questions

  1. What is your main fitness goal? (reference what you already know: don't re-ask)
  2. Why are these goals important to you? (slow down: let them open up)
  3. When would you like to achieve your goal by? (pick up the pace: action energy)
  4. What have you tried in the past? (build the case for structure and expertise)
  5. What do you do for work? (sedentary vs active: ability to invest and show up)
  6. Lifestyle: smoke? Drink more than 7 standard drinks/week? (where money and habits are directed)
  7. What do you need from us to make this a success? (connect dots from what they've told you)
  8. Any existing injuries or conditions? (reference their file: don't re-ask what you have)
  9. Any roadblocks in the next 6 months? (handle objections here: work, travel, time)

Timeframe Guide to Share

  • Months 1–3: Building habits and movement patterns
  • Months 4–6: Results become visible and compound
  • 12 months+: Every successful member has stayed at least this long

"Excellent: thank you for going through that with me. Let's move on to the physical assessment. Are you ready?"

The Physical Assessment

Mandatory PAR-Q gate: The PAR-Q must be complete and reviewed before testing begins. Clarify every Yes answer; do not test when an issue is current, uncontrolled, recent, unexplained, materially changed, unclear, or requires written medical or allied-health clearance.

Modify or omit a test when pain, apprehension, restriction, poor control, or a managed condition makes the standard test unsuitable. Stop immediately for chest pain or pressure, fainting or near-fainting, severe unusual breathlessness, sudden neurological symptoms, acute or rapidly increasing pain, loss of function, or any new unsafe symptom.

The consultation opens up the gap. The physical assessment makes it undeniable. It holds up a mirror and shows the prospect, in real time, exactly where they are.

Test 1 & 2: Single-Leg Capacity Test (R + L)

Exercise: ATG Split Squat

Below-Live regression: Front-foot-elevated ATG Split Squat

What it reveals: Unilateral leg strength, balance, ankle mobility, hip mobility, symmetry

Why it matters: Glute strength, running, carrying kids, chronic back/knee/hip pain reduction. Assess each side independently: a left/right asymmetry is as important as the overall standard.

LevelStandard
Live10 reps each side: full depth (ATG)
Long10 reps each side + 50% bodyweight
Perform10 reps each side + 100% bodyweight

How to run it:

  • Run right side first, then left
  • Record elevation, reps, load, and highest fully attained level for each side
  • Note any asymmetry between sides
  • After adequate recovery, progress through Live, Long, and Perform while technique and safety permit
  • Stop when the next standard is not fully attained or continuing would be unsafe

Test 3: Grip Endurance Test

Exercise: Farmers Carry (Walk of Independence)

What it reveals: Grip strength, carrying capacity, whole-body endurance

Why it matters: This is The Evolved's signature assessment. Grip strength is one of the most consistently cited biomarkers in the longevity research literature.

Multiple large-scale studies have found low grip strength to be a stronger predictor of cardiovascular mortality than systolic blood pressure. It is closely associated with frailty, disability, falls, and all-cause mortality.

When you look at a woman who cannot carry 75% of her bodyweight for 60 seconds, you are not observing weak hands. You are observing a systemic deficit: one that, if unaddressed, will quietly erode her independence over the years ahead.

This is not a fitness test. It is a longevity test. Communicate it that way.

LevelStandard
Live1:00 + 75% of bodyweight
Long1:00 + 100% of bodyweight
Perform1:00 + 150% of bodyweight

(Example: 60kg client = 45kg total = 22.5kg each hand for Live)

How to present the result:

If below Live:

"This test is called the Walk of Independence: and this result tells me something important. Right now your grip and carry capacity is below where we'd want it for long-term independence. Grip strength is one of the strongest predictors of how well a woman ages. The good news: it's one of the most trainable things we do here. We'll have this in your program every week."

If at Live or above:
"You hit the Live standard on the Walk of Independence: you're carrying your weight in the world. We'll keep building this. The difference between Live and Long in real life is whether you arrive at the end of your day still capable."

How to run it:

  • State the target upfront
  • Ask if they think they can hold it for 60 seconds
  • If yes, try it. If no, try 50% bodyweight to establish a baseline
  • Show them how to safely put the weights down before they start
  • Stop the set the moment form breaks down
  • After adequate recovery, test every safely attainable level
  • Record weight, verified seconds held, and highest fully attained level

Test 4: Spinal Control Test

Exercise: Plank → Side Plank → Toes to Bar

What it reveals: Core stability, spinal control, anti-rotation strength

Why it matters: A client who can hold a 2-minute plank is significantly less likely to suffer chronic back pain

LevelStandard
Live2:00 plank: hands and toes, shoulders protracted, hips level, no sag or tent
Long2:00 side plank
Perform10 Toes to Bar

How to run it:

  • Demonstrate what disqualifies the hold
  • Call the set the moment form breaks down: no exceptions
  • After adequate recovery, progress to right and left side planks, then Toes to Bar, when safely attainable
  • Record plank seconds, right and left side-plank seconds, Toes to Bar reps, and highest fully attained level

Why the Standards Are Relative to Bodyweight

Every standard at The Evolved is expressed as a percentage of a woman's own bodyweight, not as an absolute number. A 50kg woman and an 85kg woman are assessed against their own biology, not against the same fixed load.

This matters because carrying capacity, fall recovery, stair climbing, and daily physical tasks are all relative to the person performing them. Relative standards also track body composition honestly: as a woman gains muscle and improves composition, her relative strength improves even if the absolute number doesn't change dramatically.

The Three Levels

Most gyms measure performance. We measure capability. These levels exist to answer one question: what should a woman be physically capable of doing if she wants to live a long, healthy, independent life?

Live: Life Standards

The minimum every woman should possess to confidently navigate modern life. Can she lift luggage into an overhead compartment?

Carry groceries without pain? Climb stairs without stopping?

Get up from the floor without help? Live represents the baseline for functioning effectively in everyday life.

If a prospect can't hit Live on any test, the gap is undeniable and the need is real.

Long: Physical Insurance

The heart of The Evolved Method. A woman at Long standard can keep up with grandchildren, walk for hours through European cities without fatigue, climb the steep staircases of Parisian apartments without fear, and explore beaches, mountains, and hiking trails well into later life.

Long standards are about delaying or preventing the physical decline that so often accompanies ageing. When a woman reaches Long, she is building physical insurance for her future.

Perform: Full Potential

Athletic standards. Represent the ability to perform at a high level in strength sports, fitness competitions, obstacle events, or other demanding physical pursuits. Not every woman will reach Perform. Not every woman needs to. But every woman benefits from moving toward it.

Objection Handling

"I don't have the money"

"That's ok [name]. Before you go: if price wasn't an issue, would this be a program you could see yourself using to achieve [goal]?"

  • If YES → "What would have to happen in your life for you to be able to afford to invest in [goal]?" Then stop. Let them think.
  • If NO → find the real objection.

"I don't have the time"

Same structure. Replace price with time.

"I need to run it by my partner"

Work through:

  1. Did your partner know you were coming today?
  2. Aside from price, would they support this?
  3. Do you see yourself achieving [goal] with this program?

Then:

"We have a 48-hour cooling-off period. Would you feel comfortable committing today? The women who leave without committing usually come back 6 or 12 months later worse off."

The rule: A well-run assessment handles most objections before you reach the close. Preparation and the physical assessment do the work for you.

If the prospect needs time and the objection was not properly isolated or handled, allow up to 24 hours and record No Sale before they leave. Update the outcome if they later contact the gym to join.
