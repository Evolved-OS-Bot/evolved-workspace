Why Monthly Check-Ins Matter

Monthly check-ins serve two purposes:

  1. Highlight success and identify the next improvement area: members who feel seen and supported stay longer and get better results
  2. Create an opening for PT: a member who hasn't had 1:1 PT has had time to notice the gap. A well-timed check-in surfaces that need

This is not a chore. It is one of the most valuable things a trainer can do for both the member and the business.

The Call Process

When to call: One month after joining, then every month after that.

Best Times to Call

  • 7–8am
  • 12–1pm
  • 5–6pm

If They Answer: Ask

  • How is your training going?
  • Are you still enjoying it?
  • Do you feel like you're progressing toward your goals?
  • Is there any extra help you're not currently getting?

Record all notes in Trainerize after every call.

If No Answer After 3 Attempts: Send This Text

"Hey [name], I tried calling a few times but didn't hear back. Just wanted to see how you were going with your training and if you need any extra help reaching your goals."

Outcome Triage

They Want More Help → PT Upsell

Walk them through the PT sign-up:

  • Use the PT Agreement Form
  • Confirm their first session date
  • Set the first debit date: this must occur before the first session

They're Doing Fine → Stay Warm

A "no" is a "not right now." Stay the friendly, engaged trainer they love and follow up again next month. Do not disengage.

They Have an Injury, Health Condition, Surgery, or Pregnancy → Modification First

This is not a red flag to escalate. This is a coaching conversation you lead.

The Evolved position is that training modification is almost always possible and preferable to stopping. Evidence shows modified training produces faster recovery and better outcomes than cessation in the majority of health situations.

Step 1: Ask what is going on. Understand the nature and impact before offering anything.

Step 2: Explore what they can still do. In almost every case, some form of modified training is achievable.

Step 3: Offer allied health collaboration. With their consent, The Evolved can work with their GP, physio, or specialist to align on a safe training plan. Say:

"We regularly work with allied health professionals to get the best outcome for our members. With your permission, we can work alongside your practitioner. Are you open to that?"

Step 4: If they are uncertain whether they can train at all, offer an Injury Triage Session: a complimentary 1:1 session to assess what is possible and design a modified program. This is the primary re-offer.

Step 5: If modification is genuinely not possible right now, a membership hold is available.

Step 6: Escalate to management only after the above steps, or if the situation is beyond your scope to assess.

Do not default to "you should rest" or suggest a hold before you have explored modification. The default position is always: find a way to keep training.

Red Flag → Escalate Immediately

A red flag is any of the following:

  • Intention to cancel (without a health or injury reason)
  • Disgruntled or unhappy member
  • Anything you can't handle or answer

Action:

  • Email info@theevolvedgym.com.au
  • Subject line: RED FLAG: [Member Name]
  • Include full details in the body
  • Management will follow up directly; do not attempt to handle it yourself

Formal Re-Assessment & The Future-Proofing Score

Monthly check-in calls handle the ongoing relationship. Formal re-assessments handle something different: they show a member, in objective terms, how much her physical capability has changed.

The recommended re-assessment cadence is every 6 months. Run the full Strength Assessment (all four physical tests) and compare results against the initial assessment on record in Trainerize.

The Future-Proofing Score

The Future-Proofing Score gives every member a single number that summarises where she stands across the Evolved Standards. Use it at re-assessments to make progress concrete and undeniable.

Record the score in Trainerize after every re-assessment.

The Full Standards Framework

The full standards framework is the reference that makes every coaching conversation specific. A member who knows she is 12kg from the Long deadlift standard, or 3 seconds from cracking the Live rowing time, has a concrete gap to close. Gaps are motivating. Vague encouragement is not.

Use this framework in monthly check-ins, in re-assessment conversations, in PT upgrade discussions, and any time a member asks what she should be working toward. Study it until you can recall any exercise and explain which standard applies and why it matters for her long-term health.

How to Use These in Member Care

In a check-in or re-assessment, pull up the member's most recent Trainerize working weights and map them against the standards before you call. Come prepared with one specific number:

"You're at 72% of your bodyweight on the deadlift. The Long standard is 125%. That's the level where the research shows real protection against bone loss through menopause. Your progress this 6 months has you tracking to hit it within the year."

When a member hits a standard: name it, explain what it means, and celebrate it. A woman who crosses the Long standard for the Walk of Independence has just crossed into the category of women most likely to remain physically independent through later life.

That is not a small thing. Tell her.

Standards are also the most honest case for 1:1 PT. If a member has a meaningful gap, particularly in exercises directly tied to bone density, carry capacity, or fall prevention, and she is only attending group classes, 1:1 PT is the fastest path to closing it.

Frame it that way.

The Bigger Picture

Every member you check in with is a person investing in their health. Your job in these calls is not to sell: it's to genuinely care.

Members who feel that their trainer notices them, remembers their goals, and checks in consistently are members who stay for years. Retention is the foundation of everything The Evolved is building.

Take the calls seriously. Keep the notes. Escalate the red flags. The system works when everyone uses it.
