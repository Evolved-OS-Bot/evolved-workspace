### Lesson 3: Why Build & Balance

Build & Balance takes what works from Sculpt & Strength and extends it in a specific direction: bone density.

**The bone density rationale**

Bone density requires mechanical loading to maintain and build — specifically, the impact and plyometric loading that creates the compressive forces bones respond to. Progressive strength training builds muscle and relative strength. Plyometric work signals the skeletal system to adapt and reinforce itself. These are complementary but different stimuli, and Build & Balance delivers both in a single session.

Bone density peaks around age 30. After that, without the right stimulus, it declines. This is not a problem that appears in your 60s; it begins in your 30s. Building and maintaining bone density now is one of the most important physical investments a woman can make for her long-term independence.

**For every woman, not just one demographic**

Build & Balance is not a class for older women or women with bone health concerns only. It is a class for every woman, at every life stage, because the plyometric component delivers benefits well beyond bone density:

- Agility and movement coordination
- Reduced risk of falls through reflexive training
- Improved body awareness and spatial control
- Reduced risk of injury, particularly in sport and recreational activity
- Conversion of strength into athletic power output

These benefits are as relevant at 25 as they are at 55. When recommending classes to members, Build & Balance suits members with athletic goals, members wanting variety in their program, and members who want to develop physical capability that carries outside the gym.

**Unilateral work**

Build & Balance incorporates more unilateral exercises than Sculpt & Strength — more work done one limb at a time. This develops proprioception and balance alongside strength, and makes Build & Balance a useful recommendation for members with asymmetries between sides of the body or members taking a more corrective approach to their training. Both Sculpt & Strength and Build & Balance are corrective in nature; the unilateral emphasis in Build & Balance makes it particularly well-suited for the right member at the right time.

### Lesson 5: Delivering the Session

**Member arrival:**
- Greet every member warmly as they arrive
- Brief overview of the session
- Identify any injuries or concerns before you begin

**Warm-Up (5–10 minutes):**
- Use the prescribed warm-up in Trainerize
- Group members together: highlight the session focus and build rapport
- Have members open Trainerize and identify their working weight for the first exercise
- Group 2–3 people with similar working weights to minimise racking/unracking
- Walk through the Performance Warm-Up before the first working set

**The Performance Warm-Up (applies to the main lift only):**

This protocol is scientifically validated at Melbourne University by Dr Paul Cribb to increase performance by up to 30% through acclimatising the body and priming the central nervous system.

| Set | Load | Reps |
|---|---|---|
| Warm-Up Set 1 | 20% of working weight | 12 reps |
| Warm-Up Set 2 | 60% of working weight | 6 reps |
| Acclimation Set | 100% of working weight | 1 rep |
| → Working Sets begin | | |

The member's target working weight is 100%. Calculate backwards. *Example: 40kg working weight → 8kg × 12 reps → 24kg × 6 reps → 40kg × 1 rep → 40kg working sets.*

These are not working sets: they should not feel like failure. The purpose is neurological priming: by the time the first working set begins, the nervous system has been progressively loaded to handle the weight. Working sets feel controlled, not surprising.

**Main Session (30–40 minutes):**
- Follow the Trainerize workout: adhere to sets, reps, and movement patterns
- Ensure everyone has a spotter where needed
- Have all clients completing working sets in synchronisation: easier to correct form
- **Position yourself so your back is never turned to a member**
- **Provide at least one touch point per client per working set**: each member must receive a cue, adjustment, or acknowledgement at least 8 times per workout
- When giving cues: ask for consent → highlight what they're already doing well → give the cue constructively
- **Do not sit down** at any point during the workout
- **No personal phones during class** except to film and upload Instagram stories; tag @the_evolved_allfemalegym. All other personal phone use is prohibited during class
- Actively preempt space setup as the workout progresses (refer to the laminated diagram)
- You may progress, regress, or change any exercise at any point in the workout: as long as the substitute remains in the same movement pattern and uses an EVOLVE-defined option
- If you change an exercise, swap it in the Trainerize workout at the time of recording; all substitutions must be documented

**Plyometric and lateral stations:**
- Brief technique before the station begins: landing mechanics, joint alignment, controlled deceleration
- Prioritise quality of movement over reps or intensity — poor landing mechanics increase injury risk
- These are not max-effort stations; they are quality-of-movement stations

**Cool-Down (5–10 minutes):**
- Use the prescribed cool-down from Trainerize
