# Trainerize Longitudinal Strength Audit

**Audit date:** 22 July 2026, expanded former-member extraction  
**Purpose:** Determine what Trainerize can defensibly show about strength outcomes after approximately 6, 12 and 24 months and beyond.

## Executive verdict

This dataset is operationally valuable and worth retaining. It provides credible internal evidence that many women who continue training and logging at The Evolved become materially stronger.

It is not yet a scientific outcomes dataset. The records are retrospective training logs, testing was not standardised, later-horizon samples are selected survivors, and historical former-member detail is incomplete.

The strongest near-term use is:

1. identify and validate remarkable member results;
2. quantify plausible improvement ranges for core lifts;
3. refine The Evolved strength standards;
4. design the prospective V2 Women's Strength dataset;
5. generate research hypotheses, not clinical or causal claims.

Forty-four non-test profiles are not recorded as female: 34 have no sex value, 7 are recorded as male and 3 are recorded as other. Together they contain 1,118 tracked programmed workouts. The business owner advises that non-female member classifications reflect onboarding errors, but this audit does not infer or overwrite demographic data. These profiles remain in the 26,304-workout all-account operational total and are excluded from women's outcomes until individually verified.

The targeted second pass recovered 3,130 detailed workouts from 46 additional former members whose logged history spanned at least 120 days. Within the confirmed-female cohort, there are 326 deactivated accounts with completed-workout summaries and 113 with detailed set-level results.

## Most defensible findings

The table below uses each participant's best valid comparable result in the first 60 days and the relevant follow-up window. Loaded lifts use an Epley estimated 1RM from recorded 1-12 rep sets.

| Movement | 6-month paired n | Median change at 6 months | 12-month paired n | Median change at 12 months | Interpretation |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Barbell bench press | 143 | +14.3% | 81 | +18.4% | Strongest sample; plausible and useful internally |
| Barbell deadlift | 76 | +14.2% | 55 | +26.0% | Strong signal; active members improve more than former members in this sample |
| Barbell Romanian deadlift | 70 | +46.0% | 30 | +61.2% | Large signal, but low starting loads and programming progression may inflate percentage gains |
| Canonical Nexus Point Squat | 129 | +35.7% | 64 | +24.4% | Combines Nexus Point Squat, Barbell Front Squat and Barbell Back Squat, which coaches confirmed are the same exercise recorded under unintended names |

Bench press and deadlift are currently the best movements for external-facing outcome development because they combine comparatively large paired samples with more interpretable movement conventions.

## Six, twelve and twenty-four months

Six-month evidence is usable for several core movements. Twelve-month evidence is usable for bench press and deadlift, and directionally useful for Romanian deadlift and Nexus Point Squat.

Twenty-four-month evidence is sparse at the individual-movement level. Bench press has 25 paired participants, deadlift 18, Romanian deadlift 9 and canonical Nexus Point Squat 17. These are case-finding signals rather than stable population estimates.

Beyond 24 months, most individual movement samples are in single digits. Those rows should be used to find member stories and design follow-up testing, not to quote population averages.

The audit database retains 2,588 failed daily-workout request attempts from the initial network-restricted run as operational history. Those attempts were superseded by successful extraction and do not represent 2,588 missing workouts.

## Current versus former members

Former members who have recoverable detail also show improvement, which is important: the outcome signal is not confined entirely to current members.

However, active members generally have denser records and often stronger gains in bench press and deadlift. That may reflect longer exposure, more consistent training, better logging, selection into membership retention, or all four. It cannot be interpreted as proof that membership status caused the difference.

## Strength standards

The audit mapped canonical comparable movements to the current Live, Long and Perform bodyweight thresholds where a bodyweight observation existed within 45 days of the result. Nexus Point Squat, Barbell Front Squat and Barbell Back Squat are combined as one coach-confirmed canonical exercise.

There are 174 participant-movement-horizon rows with usable baseline and follow-up standard levels. These transitions are valuable for internal review but provisional because:

- the lift score is often an estimated 1RM rather than a formal test;
- bodyweight is not always measured near the lift;
- several standards may have been designed for a different testing protocol;
- the Farmer Walk historical load convention may be total load or per-hand load.

Use the standards-transition sheet to identify records for coach validation. Do not publish transition rates until V2 standardises the protocol.

## Remarkable-result candidates

The confirmed-female screening generated 118 unique candidates under a deliberately broad rule. This is a review queue, not a claim that 118 members have publishable transformations.

- 45 participants improved at least three comparable movements by at least 20% and 5kg at one follow-up horizon.
- 8 participants advanced at least one standard level in two or more movements.
- 5 participants met both criteria.

Start manual validation with those five, then the remaining multi-movement candidates. For every candidate:

1. verify exercise naming and load convention;
2. inspect the original workout history;
3. confirm the result was not a logging or equipment change;
4. speak with the coach and member;
5. obtain explicit consent before using an identifiable story.

## Formal Strength Assessment limitation

The formal Strength Assessment is not currently the longitudinal backbone. Only five people had a second formal assessment in the historical extract, one was near six months, and none were near 12 or 24 months.

Trainerize training logs can therefore describe training performance over time, but they cannot presently answer the cleaner prospective question: "How much did a standardised assessment improve after 6, 12 or 24 months?"

That question belongs in V2.

## Movement-family progression

The workbook now treats Nexus Point Squat, Barbell Front Squat and Barbell Back Squat as one canonical Nexus exercise because the names were used unintentionally for the same movement. Their recorded loads are combined for the longitudinal Nexus outcome, while each original source name remains visible for audit.

Goblet Squat remains a separate introductory stage and its load is not compared directly with Nexus load. Twenty-two women have a clean observed pathway in which their accessible mapped squat history begins with Goblet Squat and later reaches the canonical Nexus exercise. In total, 149 women show the sequence somewhere in their history, but many have earlier Nexus records because the accessible history begins mid-journey or programming cycles revisit earlier exercises.

V2 should use one Nexus exercise name consistently and retain within-exercise load progression while separately recording Goblet-to-Nexus stage, time to progression and the most advanced validated exercise achieved.

## Marketing evidence

The workbook contains a marketing evidence register with recommended wording and explicit caveats. The strongest descriptive scale facts are:

- more than 25,000 tracked completed workouts across 484 confirmed-female accounts;
- more than 22,000 recovered detailed workout records;
- more than 500,000 recorded exercise-result rows;
- 82 women with at least 100 tracked completed workouts;
- 17 women with at least 250 tracked completed workouts.

Use `tracked workouts`, not `sessions attended`, and `recorded exercise results`, not `sets`. Strength-result, standards and progression counts remain internal screening evidence until record validation and member consent.

## Farmer Walk interpretation

Only Farmer Walk rows with an explicit 60-second or one-minute target were included. The recorded `weight` field is reported as load, but the historical convention may be total load across both hands or load per hand.

The six-month median change is negative in the raw audit, which is a warning that protocols, equipment or recording conventions changed. Farmer Walk data should be retained, but it should not support external claims until the convention is confirmed from Trainerize programming history and coach practice.

## What can be claimed now

### Defensible internal claim

> Among members with comparable logged training results, many women materially improved key strength movements over six and twelve months. Bench press and deadlift currently provide the most interpretable evidence.

### Defensible public claim with careful wording

> An internal retrospective audit of The Evolved's training logs found meaningful strength improvements among members with comparable six- and twelve-month records. The business is now building a standardised prospective assessment system to validate those outcomes more rigorously.

### Not defensible yet

- "The Evolved causes a specific percentage improvement."
- "These results represent all members or all women."
- "The Women's Strength Index is clinically validated."
- "The strength standards predict health, longevity, falls or disease risk."
- Any population, clinical or cost-effectiveness claim based only on these records.

## Recommended next steps

1. Manually validate the five strongest multi-movement plus standards candidates.
2. Standardise future Trainerize programming on one Nexus Point Squat exercise name and retire the two unintended barbell aliases from new data entry.
3. Validate a sample of the 22 clean Goblet-to-Nexus records against the original programming history.
4. Confirm historical load conventions for Farmer Walk, dumbbell exercises and any custom Evolved exercise.
5. Create a fixed V2 assessment battery with explicit units, equipment, setup, assessor and pass/fail rules.
6. Schedule formal re-tests at 6 and 12 months, with 24 months as a long-term outcome.
7. Record bodyweight at every assessment to support relative-strength standards.
8. Store assessment data independently of Trainerize, with Trainerize treated as a training-delivery source rather than the research system of record.
9. Pre-register outcome definitions before analysing the first V2 cohort.
