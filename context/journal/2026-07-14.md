**LinkedIn**

Building a cancellation retention system is teaching me something I didn't expect: the details that seem small in the spec are the ones that matter most in a real conversation.

Today's example: the MC: Schedule/Time workflow.

The brief version of the re-offer script was: "1:1 PT is flexible, Hybrid gives you some access, Online Only if you can't make it in."

Fine. But when Piper picks up the phone, that's not enough. She needs to know that 1:1 PT is $120/hr off-peak and $180/hr peak — and that she should ask what the member's schedule looks like before quoting. She needs to know Hybrid is $69/week and what that gets you (one 30-minute session every four weeks, personalised programming). She needs to know Online Only is $27/week and what it doesn't include (no coaching, no modifications — just the program).

The same information needs to be in Step 2 (initial call), Step 11h (if they reply at day 14), Step 11hi (if they don't reply), Step 11hii (the mid-notice call task), and Step 13 (the owner escalation). Every touchpoint. Same facts, same offers, same prices. Every time.

That's what uniformity across a system actually means. Not just the same structure — the same information at every point where a decision might be made.
