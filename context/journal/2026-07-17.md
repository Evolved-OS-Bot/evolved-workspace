### Social Copy (Session 2)

**LinkedIn**

We confirmed our email nurture sequences have a 0% click-through rate on every single CTA. 162 emails. Open rates of 75–100%. Zero clicks.

First instinct: rewrite the copy. But the diagnosis matters. Tracking was confirmed on every step. The 0% was real.

The actual problem: every SA booking CTA is a plain text link at the bottom of a P.S. Weakest CTA format in email. Contacts read the email, absorb the P.S., scroll past without registering it as an action prompt.

Fix: replace every plain text link with an HTML button. Same URL. Same copy. One button element per email. Also cut two competing CTAs (seminar, survey) and a $44 product sitting alongside the SA link at Day 30 — giving warm leads an off-ramp before committing.

48 subscribers and 27 leads over 30 days, all organic. Moving 10% to click and book is meaningful SA volume from leads already in the system. Plan documented and ready to execute.

### Social Copy

**LinkedIn**

Five email sequences. 162 emails. One blind spot.

I spent part of this session auditing our 30-day waitlist nurture system — the email sequences that run in GHL after someone joins our waitlist for The Evolved.

We have five life-stage segmented sequences: Teens, 20s & 30s, Planning/Pregnant/Post-Partum, Perimenopause, Post-Menopause. Each runs for 30 days, sending educational content at 6AM AEST.

When I mapped where the Strength Assessment booking CTAs actually appear across those 30 days, I found this: Days 0, 1, 2b, 5, 7, 9. Then nothing.

21 consecutive days with no booking prompt. Day 10 through Day 30 — 21 emails — zero asks.

These are warm leads who opted in, confirmed their interest, and are actively opening and reading content. But for three weeks, we never once ask them to take the next step.

The fix isn't complicated. Five emails (one per life stage) added at Day 30. Then mid-sequence P.S. lines on Days 14, 21, and 28. Then a post-Day 30 re-engagement sequence. None of this requires new infrastructure — it's edits inside an existing published workflow.

The audit also surfaced two copy issues: the Perimenopause and Post-Menopause Day 2b emails open with nearly identical framing, and the 20s & 30s sequence has a tone inconsistency in the social proof section that doesn't match the rest of the voice.

All of this is now documented in the workspace so it's not lost between sessions.

The lesson here isn't unique to fitness businesses. If you have a nurture sequence, map where your CTAs actually sit. You might be warming people up for weeks and never asking them to act.
