**LinkedIn**

We're three workflows into the cancellation retention system build this week.

Two things have come out of the build that I didn't expect.

The first: the Moving/Travel workflow needed a permanent move branch. We'd originally planned to offer a hold for everyone — but you can't hold a membership indefinitely. So we built a proper split: temporary moves get a standard or extended hold, permanent moves get offered our remote products (Online Only at $27/week, or Hybrid Remote at $69/week with a monthly PT session and coaching support).

What looked like a simple workflow turned into a product decision. Those two remote offerings had never been formally documented anywhere in the business — they existed, but had no pricing on record, no structured description, nothing. We fixed that at the same time.

The second: building the Health/Injury workflow forced us to document something that's been implicit in how we coach but was never written down — that modification is always the default response to injury, not cessation.

"Evidence shows training modifications have faster recovery times and better outcomes in the majority of injuries and health conditions — even in cases of pregnancy or surgery."

That line is now in the retention call script, in our trainer manual, in our professional standards SOP, and in the trainer onboarding course. One insight from a GHL task, cascaded across the whole system.

That's what building a business OS looks like. You can't just document what you already know. You have to build until you hit the gap, then fix the gap, then document what you found.

3 of 9 workflows complete. 6 to go.
