Wave 2B originally had a pre-SMS notification telling Piper to "try a different angle — swap the offer." We cut it. By day 14 Piper's already had the retention call. She knows the situation. What she doesn't need is another push prompt — she needs a reason for the member to respond.

### Social Copy

**LinkedIn**

Something clicked today building out the cancellation retention system for The Evolved.

We started with a simple question: how long should a retention workflow run?

The obvious answer was "until the farewell session" — about 5-7 days after someone submits their cancellation form.

The better answer: the entire 30-day notice period. Every day inside that window is an opportunity. Stopping at day 7 leaves 23 days of potential on the table, on a member who's worth an average of $2,677 in demonstrated LTV.

So we redesigned the skeleton. Three waves:

Wave 1 (days 0–5): retention coach calls within 24 hours, twice-daily attempts, escalation to owner at 48 hours, farewell session offer at day 5.

Wave 2A (day 14): if no contact was ever made — owner steps in personally with a warmer, different-angle message. Not Piper. The owner.

Wave 2B (day 14): if contact was made but they're still leaving — a soft mid-notice check-in. "Has anything changed?" Not another offer. Just a human question.

Wave 3 (day 25): last-chance owner task and a final SMS. Door always open.

The other thing we built: a `cs: contact made` tag that Piper applies the moment she has a live conversation. The workflow checks this tag before sending reminders and escalations — so if Piper's already on it, the noise stops. Clean signal, no gaming, GHL's call log handles the audit trail.

9 workflows. One template. A system that doesn't give up on a member until their notice period actually ends.

If you're running a membership business and your cancellation process is a single email — there's a lot of ground to cover.
