## Part 1: Initial Consultation

### Before the Assessment

Know your prospect. Take 5–10 minutes — the night before, before your shift, or prior to the assessment — to read their pre-qualification summary or review their conversation history in Lead Connector. This level of preparation is non-negotiable.

Pre-qualification is a shared team process. The owner, admin or a coach may have contributed, so read the complete conversation and treat it as the source of truth.

An injury branch closes once the information is trainer-actionable and no unresolved safety issue requires another pre-assessment question. Do not restart the injury interview simply because more questions could be asked.

Treat observations from a trusted allied-health or manual-therapy provider as useful context, not as a diagnosis independently confirmed by The Evolved. Build on the handoff and confirm only what the PAR-Q or current safety requires.

**Set up the Trainerize account before they arrive:**
- The trainer purchases the `Initial Consultation` Trainerize product for the prospect
- Open the resulting sign-up link on the Sales iPad
- Use Lead Connector to pre-fill their details
- Password: Evolved2026
- Agree to terms and continue
- Set timezone to Brisbane
- Enter their phone number
- Have this screen loaded and ready before the assessment begins

**Be outside to greet them.** Stand at the top of the stairs or ramp with Lead Connector open, ready to call or text if they need help finding us. If they're lost — go upstairs and find them.

---

### Introduction

First impressions last. We are a private space that can feel hard to find and intimidating. Nothing fixes that faster than a big, warm smile and genuine energy. Be the coach they hoped they'd meet.

**When the client walks in:**
- Greet them warmly, iPad in hand
- Build rapport — *"How did you go finding us? Do you live close by?"*
- Pre-frame the session:

> *"Today I'll be working with you to find your starting point. By the end of the assessment you'll know exactly where you're strong, what needs strengthening, and how long it will take to reach your goals. At the end, if I think we can help you, I'll ask — and you can say yes or no. Either answer is fine. Does that sound fair?"*

- Get their buy-in — wait for a yes
- Once they agree: ask for height and weight, then take a seat

---

### Consultation Form

This is an information-gathering exercise — not a coaching session. Your job is to go deeper on what you already know, not re-ask what's been captured. Do not offer advice, make recommendations, or get into lengthy discussions. You're listening for the emotion behind what they want.

**1. What is your main fitness goal?**

Reference what you already know:
- *"I read through your pre-qualification conversation and saw your main goal is to lose 7kg by the end of the year: tell me more about that."*
- *"I remember from our conversation you had some injuries and wanted to get stronger — tell me more about that."*

Select the relevant goal option and add detail as they speak.

Follow-up: *"Do you have any other fitness goals?"*
Ask once, then sit in silence and let them think. Ask again: *"And you're sure there's nothing else?"* Sit in silence again.

> Note: Women often need space and time to fully open up. Don't rush this.

---

**2. Why are these goals important to you?**

> *"[Name], I can clearly see these goals matter to you — otherwise you wouldn't be here. You strike me as someone who takes action. Tell me, why are these goals important to you?"*

If they hesitate:
> *"That's ok if you can't quite put it into words. You've taken an hour out of your day / travelled [X] distance / built up the courage to come in. What's driven you to do all of this?"*

> Note: Slow down. Let them think. Re-ask in a different way if needed. When they open up — don't reply. Listen and let them run until they stop.

---

**3. When would you like to achieve your goal by?**

- *"Do you have a deadline coming up?"*
- *"An event you'd like to hit this by?"*
- *"Should we put a timeframe on it?"*

> Note: Pick up the pace here — this is action energy. Be honest about unrealistic timeframes.

**Timeframe guide to share with clients:**
- **Months 1–3:** Learning phase — building the habit and the movement patterns
- **Months 4–6:** Results become visible and start to compound
- **12 months+:** Every successful client has stayed at least this long
- Every cell in the body is replaced over a 6-month period — results take time
- Weight loss benchmark: 200–500g per week when following the meal plan and training 3–5x per week

---

**4. What have you tried in the past to achieve this goal?**

Let them think. Ask why those attempts failed. Ask if they liked that style of support or are looking for something different.

> Note: This is where you build the case for structure, support, and expert guidance. Don't lead — let them arrive there themselves.

---

**5. What do you do for work?**

Note whether their role is sedentary or active.

> Note: You're listening for two things — their ability to invest in the level of service we provide, and their ability to show up consistently.

---

**6. Lifestyle questions**

- *"Do you smoke tobacco products on a weekly basis?"*
- *"Do you drink more than 7 standard alcoholic drinks per week?"*

> Note: You're identifying where money and habits are currently directed — and whether those habits conflict with their goals.

---

**7. What do you need from us to make this goal a success?**

Slow down again. Let them think. Connect the dots using what they've already told you:
- *"You mentioned earlier that you didn't like how crowded your old gym was — so would you be looking for something more private and personal, like what we offer here?"*
- *"You said CrossFit felt too fast and you got injured — so are you looking for something smarter, with coaches who can slow things down and make sure you're moving correctly?"*

---

**8. Do you have any existing injuries or conditions?**

Reference what you already have on file:
- *"Nothing came up for injuries in your pre-qualification conversation: is that still correct?"*
- *"I saw you mentioned osteoporosis in your conversation with us — is there anything more I should know before we move to the physical assessment?"*

Add any additional detail gathered.

---

**9. Any roadblocks in the next 6 months?**

Listen for objections — work, travel, time. Handle them here and now:

- **Work:** *"At your busiest, can you find 3 hours a week to invest in yourself — something that actually makes you more productive?"*
- **Travel:** *"How often do you travel? We have clients who travel a lot — they train with us when they're home and we give them programming to follow when they're away. Can you see yourself doing the same?"*

---

### Transition to Physical Assessment

**Mandatory PAR-Q safety gate:** Do not begin physical testing until the PAR-Q is complete and reviewed.

- If every answer is No, proceed while continuing to monitor the prospect.
- If any answer is Yes, clarify whether the issue is current or historical, managed or uncontrolled, and whether there are symptoms, treatment, restrictions, or professional advice in place. Record the facts without diagnosing.
- A cautious Yes may be cleared only when it is historical or well managed, there are no current warning signs, and existing health advice supports activity.
- A current, uncontrolled, recent, unexplained, materially changed, or unclear issue requires written medical or allied-health clearance before physical testing.
- Modify or omit a test when pain, apprehension, restriction, poor control, or a managed condition makes the standard test unsuitable.
- Stop immediately for chest pain or pressure, fainting or near-fainting, severe unusual breathlessness, sudden neurological symptoms, acute or rapidly increasing pain, loss of function, or any new symptom that makes continuing unsafe. Escalate to the appropriate health or emergency service.

> *"Excellent — thank you for taking the time to go through that with me. Let's move on to the physical assessment. Are you ready?"*

### Why the Physical Assessment Exists

The consultation opens up the gap. The physical assessment makes it undeniable.

Most fitness sales processes never leave the chair. A prospect can nod along for 45 minutes and still feel uncertain. The strength assessment changes that — it holds up a mirror and shows them, in real time, exactly where they are.

Three exercise categories producing four scored assessment results. Clear standards. A card they take home.

---

### The Strength Assessment Card

Have the card ready before moving to the physical assessment. It contains:
- The three exercise categories, four scored results, and their standards
- The three strength levels: **Live**, **Long**, and **Perform**
- A longevity timeline showing average progress

Record results in shorthand on both the digital form and the physical card. The card goes home with them.

> Example shorthand: "20cm (each 15kg plate = 5cm) front foot raise, bodyweight, 10 reps"

---

### Results 1 and 2: Single-Leg Capacity Test

**Exercise:** ATG Split Squat

**Below-Live regression:** Front-foot-elevated ATG Split Squat

**What it reveals:** Ankle mobility, knee strength, hip mobility, and overall leg function

**Why it matters:** Translates directly to glute strength, running, carrying kids, returning from injury, and reducing chronic pain in the back, knees, and hips.

**Setup:**
- Take an educated guess and use a height achievable for 10 reps (can always increase if needed)
- Progressively lower the height using individual plates for smaller increments

**Testing scope:**
- Assess the right and left sides independently
- Use the below-Live regression when full-depth floor ATG Split Squat is not attainable
- Record elevation, reps, load, and the highest fully attained level for each side
- After adequate recovery, progress through Live, Long, and Perform while technique and safety permit
- Stop when the next standard is not fully attained or continuing would be unsafe

**The Live standard:**
- Heel flat and grounded — no lifting
- Knee tracking over the toe, midline
- Hamstring and calf in full contact at the bottom
- 10 clean reps each side

**How to run it:**
- Demonstrate the standard clearly before they begin
- Start with the higher box height, have them perform ~6 reps
- If it looks easy, reduce the height and repeat
- Progress downward until form breaks down
- Record height, rep count, load, and result independently for each side

> Note: The prospect is warming up as they work through levels — this is fine and expected. The goal is to find their honest starting point, not exhaust them.

---

### Test 2 — Farmer's Carry

**What it reveals:** Grip strength and full-body carrying capacity

**Why it matters:** Grip strength is one of the strongest predictors of independence in the final decade of life.
- For clients over 30: this lands hard as a longevity marker
- For clients under 30: explain the correlation between grip strength and compound movements — glutes, V-shaped waist, chin-ups, etc.

**The standards:**
- 75% of bodyweight for 60 seconds, walking
- Long: 100% of bodyweight for 60 seconds
- Perform: 150% of bodyweight for 60 seconds
- Example: 60kg client = 45kg total = 22.5kg dumbbell each hand

**How to run it:**
- Tell them the target upfront — *"This is what we're working towards"*
- Calculate 75% of bodyweight — ask if they think they can hold it for 60 seconds
  - If yes: give it a try
  - If no: calculate 50% and ask again
- Show them how to pick up from the bench and how to safely drop if needed
- Stop a set early and increase weight if it looks effortless
- Stop the set the moment form breaks down
- After adequate recovery, test each safely attainable level and record weight, duration, and highest fully attained level

> Note: Run this dynamically. You don't need four strict one-minute sets. Start light, build up, and find their edge without pushing unsafely.

---

### Test 3 — Full Plank

**What it reveals:** Core stability and foundational strength

**Why it matters:** A client who can hold a full plank for 2 minutes is significantly less likely to suffer from chronic back pain.

**The standards:**
- Hands and toes — not forearms
- Shoulders protracted
- Hips in line — no sagging, no tenting
- 2 minutes continuous
- Long: 2-minute side plank, recording right and left seconds independently
- Perform: 10 Toes to Bar

**How to run it:**
- Demonstrate the standard — specifically what disqualifies the hold (sag or tent)
- Start the timer when they're in position
- Call the set the moment form breaks down — no exceptions
- After adequate recovery, test each safely attainable level and record plank seconds, right and left side-plank seconds, Toes to Bar reps, and highest fully attained level

> Note: The card also includes side planks and hanging leg raises at the higher levels. These come into play when presenting the Long and Perform standards.

---

### Recording Results

| Field | What to Capture |
|---|---|
| Single-Leg Capacity Right | Elevation, reps, load, highest fully attained level |
| Single-Leg Capacity Left | Elevation, reps, load, highest fully attained level |
| Grip Endurance | Weight, verified seconds held, highest fully attained level |
| Spinal Control | Plank seconds, right and left side-plank seconds, Toes to Bar reps, highest fully attained level |

Enter results into the digital form and write them onto the physical card. The digital record stays in the CRM. The card goes home with the prospect.

---

### The Levels Explained

| Level | What it Means |
|---|---|
| Live | Foundational capacity for confident everyday movement |
| Long | Strength to maintain independence and function as you age |
| Perform | Athletic and performance-level strength |

The levels are milestones, not verdicts. Record the highest fully attained standard and the exact raw result. A result below a threshold identifies current capacity and the next progression; it is not a failure or a diagnosis.

---

### Presenting the Results

Once all four scored results are complete, bring their attention to the longevity scale on the card.

**If any scored result is below Live:**

> *"[Name], your results show gaps between your current capacity and the Live standard. Those gaps could be contributing to [difficulty / pain / limitation], but this assessment cannot diagnose the cause. What it does show us is where building strength and control may help. A structured training plan gives us a clear way to work on those gaps and measure progress over time. How does that feel? Do you want help closing that gap?"*

Wait in silence. Let them answer. When they say yes — move into the price presentation.

## Part 3: The Close

### Present the Offer

Open the offer document on the computer and turn the screen to face them.

**Lead with the most popular package, Strong, Fit & Flexible Membership:**

> *"This is our most popular membership. It's designed for the woman who wants support, structure, and a community, all focused on building real strength. It includes everything you need to get started: one KickStart onboarding session, a custom meal plan, and access to our full group training program."*

**Then introduce Fast Track:**

> *"For women who want a little extra — maybe they've got some injuries to work around, a medical condition we need to be mindful of, or they simply want to move faster — we offer the Fast Track. That adds a weekly one-on-one PT session so you get more personalised coaching on top of everything else."*

Then stop talking. Sit in silence while they look at the screen.

> Note: Silence is the most powerful tool you have at this moment. Let it feel slightly uncomfortable. Do not fill it.

---

### The Close Question

> *"Is there anything on the screen that jumps out as something you can do — something you'd like to do?"*

Then go silent again. Wait for them to speak first.

### If They Ask About 1:1 PT

Most PT questions will come up early in pre-qualification or the initial consultation. If already raised, skip the price presentation card and go straight to PT rates.

If the standard packages have been presented and they specifically ask about PT — present all PT rates at that point.

**Steps to complete PT sign-up:**
1. Open the personal training agreement form
2. Enter their contact details
3. Walk them through terms and conditions briefly — offer time to read in full if they'd like
4. Have them sign at the bottom
5. Form redirects to the correct checkout — complete payment
6. Checkout redirects to 1:1 booking page
7. Get them booked into their first session within 2–3 days, or the next business day

---

### If There Are Objections

Handle all objections before moving to the form.

If the prospect says they need to think and the real objection has not been isolated or handled well, allow up to 24 hours. Record the outcome as **No Sale** before they leave; if they later message to join, update the outcome and complete the sale path.

> Note: If you prepared thoroughly, consulted well, and ran the physical assessment correctly, most objections should be minor. A well-run assessment does most of the objection-handling before you reach the close. Objection handling is covered in a separate SOP.
