# Evolved Exercise Doctrine

**Owner:** Peter Brown  
**Status:** Canonical consolidation of existing Evolved authority; no new live-service change authorised  
**Version:** 1.4  
**As of:** 18 August 2026

This is the canonical authority for interpreting Evolved's exercise positions. Progressive strength remains the foundation, while conditioning, power, impact, mobility, movement control and sport-specific work are governed by their intended outcomes. The doctrine consolidates the existing Evolved Method and Manual without pretending that every coaching convention is a scientific finding. The Method remains the delivery authority for current parameters and the Manual remains the trainer-education authority until an authorised downstream regression and cascade updates either one.

## Evidence and method boundary

Evolved uses four different kinds of truth deliberately:

- **Evidence-supported findings** describe what research can support within the studied population, intervention and outcome.
- **Evolved positions** define what Peter has selected as the method and standard.
- **Practice-informed coaching heuristics** help trainers make bounded decisions when research cannot specify the individual answer.
- **Service and clinical boundaries** define what Evolved currently delivers and when a trainer must modify, refer or defer.

Research strengthens and constrains the Method; it does not have to prove every framework, exercise taxonomy, frequency standard or coaching sequence before Evolved may use it. Equally, an Evolved standard cannot be presented publicly as a uniquely proven biological threshold unless the evidence supports that wording.

## Protected service boundary

Standard Evolved coaching may assess, teach, programme, supervise, record and progress general resistance training within trainer competence. Megan's separate one-to-one nutrition service may support individual nutrition and fuelling within its recorded scope. Diagnosis, rehabilitation, treatment of pain or disease, medication and supplement advice, psychotherapy, and condition-specific clinical nutrition remain with appropriately qualified clinicians. A trainer may work alongside a clinician, with the woman's consent, and translate documented restrictions into fitness programming without claiming to provide the clinical care.

## DOC-TRN-0001 — Strength-Led Physical Development

| Field | Record |
| --- | --- |
| Domain | Strength Training |
| Statement | Evolved makes progressive strength training the central physical stimulus in its method because it directly develops strength and muscle and can contribute to physical function, capability and healthy ageing. Other forms of movement remain valuable for outcomes they are suited to; strength is central, not exclusive. |
| Primary status | Evolved position, supported by evidence |
| Owner | Peter Brown |
| Accepted | 16 August 2026 |
| Review due | 16 August 2027 |
| Immediate-review triggers | Material contrary evidence; change to the Evolved service model; evidence of harm; a life-stage or clinical application that exceeds the general position. |

### Why Evolved holds this position

- **Evidence:** Resistance training improves strength and muscle-related outcomes across many adult populations and can improve or preserve aspects of function. Associations between strength, muscle and later health do not prove that one programme prevents disease or guarantees longevity.
- **Evidence:** Women who remain inactive face a material age-related risk to muscle and strength. The trajectory is not uniform: quantitative evidence shows wide variation by age and method, and strength commonly declines faster than measured muscle mass. Female longitudinal cohorts also show age-, region- and menopause-related differences, with physical activity contributing to maintenance.
- **Evolved judgement:** Strength is the organising physical priority of the four-pillar transformation architecture.
- **Practice:** Aerobic exercise, sport, Pilates, mobility and ordinary physical activity can be useful complements and may be primary for other goals. They are not automatically interchangeable with a recorded progressive resistance programme.

### Coaching application

Anchor the programme in resistance training, then add conditioning, movement practice and recovery according to the woman's goals, capacity and preferences. Explain the purpose of each mode without demeaning the activities she enjoys.

### Scope and exclusions

This does not establish strength training as the only worthwhile exercise or as uniquely superior for every health outcome. It does not promise hormone correction, disease prevention, injury prevention, a particular body composition or longer life.

A loss in the order of 2.5 kg across a decade may be used as a possible illustration for some women, not as a validated average, inevitable rate or personal forecast.

| Primary status | Evolved position |

| Accepted | 16 August 2026 |
| Review due | 16 August 2027 |
| Immediate-review triggers | Owner changes the standard; service capacity changes; harm or systematic non-adherence; clinical guidance requires a different temporary path. |

### Why Evolved holds this position

- **Evidence:** Resistance-training benefits can occur across different frequencies when total dose and adherence are considered. Research does not establish one universally necessary weekly frequency for every outcome and person.
- **Evolved judgement:** Peter has set three adequate weekly sessions as the practice standard Evolved asks women to make time for.
- **Practice:** Current capacity may require a temporary, lower-demand or lower-frequency pathway. That modification does not redefine the Evolved standard.

### Coaching application

Plan toward three adequate strength exposures each week. When illness, pregnancy, postpartum recovery, pain, caring demands, low capacity or clinician restrictions intervene, adjust dose and route safely, record why, and reassess rather than treating a constrained week as failure.

### Scope and exclusions

This is not a universal medical prescription, a moral test or proof that one or two sessions are useless. Clinical instructions and immediate safety override the standard.

## DOC-TRN-0003 — Adequate Challenge, Progression and Recovery

| Field | Record |
| --- | --- |
| Domain | Strength Training |
| Statement | Strength training must create enough challenge to practise and stimulate the intended adaptation, be recoverable, and progress over time. Evolved records performance and changes load, repetitions, sets, range, tempo, complexity or exercise selection according to response. |
| Primary status | Evolved position, supported by evidence and practice |
| Owner | Peter Brown |
| Accepted | 16 August 2026 |
| Review due | 16 August 2027 |
| Immediate-review triggers | Programming standard changes; systematic adverse response; evidence materially changes the general progression model. |

### Why Evolved holds this position

- **Evidence:** Progressive resistance training supports adaptation across a broad range of loads. The Currier et al. network meta-analysis (`STU-EXE-0007`) found that many prescriptions improved strength and hypertrophy, while higher loads ranked highest for strength; most direct between-prescription differences were small or uncertain. PMC10559623 directly tested 75% 1RM for sets of 10 with progressive loading, planned deloads and 6–8 sets per muscle group per week in middle-aged women; strength improved. Its 50% condition also improved strength and the study did not find an intensity-by-time advantage, so it supports 75% as effective without independently proving that 75% was superior in that trial.
- **Evolved judgement:** The current KPH parameters are 6–12 RM, approximately 70–85% 1RM and 6–8 working sets total per strength session. Loads below 70% can produce adaptation, but Evolved regards them as generally less direct and less effective for maximal-strength development than appropriately programmed heavier loading. Across the six patterns and three weekly strength sessions, programming normally gives two primary pattern emphases to each session and distributes approximately 3–4 working sets to each. Overlapping recruitment across the compound patterns accumulates approximately 6–8 effective working sets for each major muscle group over the week. These are Evolved delivery standards for its primary strength formats, not a claim that 70–85% is universally superior for hypertrophy or every adaptation.
- **Practice:** Those parameters guide coaching; they are not universal biological cut-offs. Exercise history, technique, symptoms, goal, fatigue and clinical restrictions can justify a different starting dose or progression variable.

### Coaching application

Record what was actually performed. Progress only when execution and response support it. A regression, substitution or deload is a programming decision, not evidence that the member has failed.

### Scope and exclusions

This does not mean every set must reach failure, every exercise must sit in one rep range or a session outside KPH has no value. It does not authorise ignoring pain, technique loss or clinical restrictions to preserve a number.

## DOC-TRN-0005 — Measure Direction, Not Human Worth

| Field | Record |
| --- | --- |
| Domain | Strength Training |
| Statement | Evolved measures relevant performance and capability so programming can be individualised and progress made visible. Standards and assessments provide direction; they are not diagnoses, guarantees, pass-fail judgements of a woman or substitutes for clinical assessment. |
| Primary status | Evolved position |
| Owner | Peter Brown |
| Accepted | 16 August 2026 |
| Review due | 16 August 2027 |
| Immediate-review triggers | Assessment or consent standard changes; a measure shows poor validity, safety or member impact; privacy or service scope changes. |

### Why Evolved holds this position

- **Evidence:** Strength, power, function, physical activity and cardiorespiratory-fitness measures can be informative and are often associated with later outcomes. Prospective women-specific evidence links lower grip with later loss of vigorous or daily function, but grip is neither a complete causal explanation nor a deterministic forecast. Association at population level does not predict one woman's lifespan or diagnose her health.
- **Evolved judgement:** PRIME, EVOLVE and the Evolved Standards turn data into coaching direction and visible capability.
- **Practice:** Use only measures that are safe, relevant, consented and interpretable in context.

### Coaching application

Compare the member primarily with her own valid baseline, explain uncertainty, record actual performance and use the result to guide the next block. Refer when symptoms or findings require clinical interpretation.

### Scope and exclusions

Grip, loaded carry, sit-to-stand, VO2 estimates and strength standards are not standalone longevity tests. A result cannot establish frailty, disease, dementia, future independence, fall risk or life expectancy for an individual. Clinical screening thresholds, population reference values and Evolved performance milestones are three different classes and must not be silently exchanged.

| Field | Record |
| --- | --- |
| Domain | Strength Training |
| Statement | Evolved uses the Eight Physical Assets—strength, muscle mass, bone density, mobility, balance and coordination, work capacity, independence, and grip strength and carrying capacity—as a coaching map for broad physical capability across life. |
| Primary status | Evolved position informed by evidence |
| Owner | Peter Brown |
| Accepted | 16 August 2026 |
| Review due | 16 August 2027 |
| Immediate-review triggers | Asset framework changes; evidence or assessment review exposes an invalid mapping; terminology creates public or clinical overreach. |

### Why Evolved holds this position

Research supports the importance of several underlying capacities, while the eight-part grouping and its assessment mappings are Evolved's own organising framework. Prospective cohorts and meta-analysis support grip strength as one marker of later disability risk, while walking speed, chair rise, balance, lower-body strength, health and current daily function add independent information. The Walk of Independence Live threshold preserves Peter Attia's published benchmark that carrying 75% of bodyweight for one minute is an excellent achievement for a woman. Attia describes the wider Centenarian Decathlon as a mental model for individual task backcasting; neither the framework nor the 75% carry is a cohort-validated mortality or independence cut-off. Evolved therefore retains 75%, 100% and 150% as progressively demanding grip-and-carry capability milestones. Independence is the valued life outcome, not a physiological asset that any one gym test can certify.

### Coaching application

Use the assets to connect programme choices to what matters in the woman's life and to identify neglected capacities. Programme and measure the underlying qualities rather than promising the life outcome.

### Scope and exclusions

The framework does not prove that every listed test measures every asset, that one standard prevents loss of independence or that muscle is literally the sole organ of longevity.

## DOC-TRN-0007 — Complementary Modes Serve Distinct Purposes

| Field | Record |
| --- | --- |
| Domain | Strength Training |
| Statement | Strength, conditioning, lower-intensity activity, power, impact, mobility and movement-control work serve overlapping but distinct purposes. Evolved keeps progressive strength central while selecting complementary modes for the woman's goals, capacity, enjoyment and recovery. |
| Primary status | Evolved position supported by evidence and practice |
| Owner | Peter Brown |
| Accepted | 16 August 2026 |
| Review due | 16 August 2027 |
| Immediate-review triggers | Service formats change; concurrent-training evidence materially changes; life-stage safety guidance changes. |

### Why Evolved holds this position

Concurrent training does not automatically prevent hypertrophy or maximal-strength development, although mode, sequencing, fatigue and the outcome being trained matter. Pilates and mixed-modal sport can be valuable without automatically providing the same progressive resistance dose.

Cardiorespiratory fitness is a separate, trainable reserve. In a women-only cohort, each 1-MET higher exercise capacity was associated with 17% lower adjusted mortality risk; broader meta-analysis found a graded association of similar magnitude. These are population associations, not guaranteed benefits from adding 1 MET. When measured precisely, VO2max or VO2peak should be interpreted against age-, sex- and protocol-aware reference values such as FRIEND rather than one universal number. Research in older adults suggests approximately 15 mL/kg/min may be an approximate aerobic floor compatible with independent life for women around age 85, but it is not a validated pass mark or an appropriate midlife target. Evolved uses the idea to build reserve above later task demands, not to promise independence.

Skeletal-muscle mitochondrial content and function contribute to aerobic energy production, but VO2max also depends on the heart, lungs, blood, circulation and muscle oxygen extraction. Endurance, high-intensity and sprint training can all stimulate mitochondrial adaptation; current evidence does not establish unique “zone 2” mitochondrial magic. Lower-intensity aerobic work remains useful because it can accumulate sustainable volume, while higher-intensity work provides a distinct stimulus. The programme should earn the mix through purpose, capacity and recovery.

### Coaching application

Name the goal of each session and protect sufficient quality and recovery for the priority adaptation. Where cardiorespiratory reserve is a goal, combine sustainable aerobic volume with appropriately dosed higher-intensity work rather than treating either as the sole valid route. Use laboratory or supervised clinical testing when exact VO2 measurement or medical interpretation matters; field and wearable estimates are trend tools. Screen and scale power, impact and conditioning where symptoms, pregnancy, postpartum status, bone health or clinical guidance require it.

### Scope and exclusions

This position does not demean cardio, CrossFit, HYROX, Pilates or sport and does not claim that combining training modes always creates interference. It does not treat a wearable VO2 estimate as a direct mitochondrial test, set a universal VO2 pass mark or promise that a given fitness gain changes lifespan, dementia or independence by a fixed amount.

### Why Evolved holds this position

Appropriately selected exercise can be useful for many people with persistent musculoskeletal pain and across pregnancy and postpartum, but average study findings do not prescribe one person's load or establish diagnosis. Symptoms, red flags, preferences and clinical instructions matter.

### Coaching application

Clarify what changed, screen within scope, modify load/range/volume/frequency/exercise where appropriate, monitor response, and refer or coordinate care when the trainer's boundary is reached. Do not use “modification first” as pressure to continue a membership or session.

## DOC-TRN-0009 — Strength Foundation, General Conditioning, Specific Conditioning

| Field | Record |
| --- | --- |
| Domain | Exercise and Athletic Performance |
| Statement | Strength is the foundation of Evolved physical preparation. When strength is a meaningful limiting quality, getting an athlete stronger is usually the highest-leverage general intervention for improving performance. General conditioning builds the capacity to perform and recover from work, while specific conditioning and skill practice convert that base into performance in the actual sport. |
| Primary status | Evolved position supported by evidence and practice |
| Owner | Peter Brown |
| Accepted | 18 August 2026 |
| Review due | 18 August 2027 |
| Immediate-review triggers | Material contrary transfer evidence; change to the Evolved service or athlete-development model; a sport or population repeatedly fails the hierarchy; the claim is proposed without its limiting-quality boundary. |

### Why Evolved holds this position

- **Evidence:** Resistance training improves strength and commonly improves power, speed and jumping in trained female athletes (`STU-FAP-0002`). A sprint meta-analysis found that increases in lower-body strength transferred positively to sprint performance (`STU-FAP-0011`). A broader elite-athlete meta-analysis found an overall improvement in sport-specific performance, with substantial heterogeneity by outcome and context (`STU-FAP-0012`). Concurrent aerobic and strength training generally did not compromise maximal strength or hypertrophy, although same-session training could attenuate explosive-strength gains (`STU-FAP-0013`).
- **Evolved judgement:** Strength raises general force-producing capacity and therefore offers the broadest first investment across many athletic tasks. General conditioning then develops the capacity to perform and recover from broader work. Specific conditioning finally prepares the athlete for the energy-system, movement, duration and work-to-rest demands of her sport or event.
- **Practice:** Transfer is strongest when strength is genuinely limiting. As the athlete develops, the performance constraint may shift toward skill, rate of force development, speed, power, movement quality, tactical execution or specific conditioning.

### Coaching application

Protect progressive strength as the physical foundation. Build general aerobic and work capacity without allowing fatigue to erase the quality of the priority strength and skill work. As the target event or sport approaches, increase the proportion of conditioning that resembles its actual demands while retaining enough strength work to preserve the base.

The hierarchy is a priority model, not a rigid waiting list. Appropriate aerobic work and sport practice may begin from the start. “After” means that general and specific conditioning are layered without displacing the strength base, and that sport-specific fatigue is not mistaken for general physical preparation.

### Scope and exclusions

This is not a claim that the strongest athlete always wins, that strength alone produces sport skill or that more maximal strength improves performance indefinitely. It does not require a novice to complete a fixed strength threshold before performing any conditioning, and it does not make generic conditioning sport-specific merely because it is difficult.
