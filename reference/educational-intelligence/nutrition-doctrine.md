# Evolved Nutrition Doctrine

**Owner:** Peter Brown  
**Status:** Canonical foundational Nutrition Doctrine; full-public-education boundary accepted; downstream article and trainer-course cascade in progress  
**Version:** 2.6  
**As of:** 18 August 2026

This is the canonical authority for accepted Evolved nutrition positions. Acceptance as an `Evolved position` defines the coaching method and service boundary; it does not establish treatment efficacy or convert source teaching into scientific evidence.

## Protected service boundary

Evolved may publish detailed, evidence-linked nutrition education under Megan Brown's public authorship and Peter Brown's research and shadow-writing role. Public educational depth does not grant delivery authority to every trainer who reads it.

Peter and Megan are currently the only Evolved people recorded as suitably nutrition-qualified for detailed nutrition education or individual nutrition discussion within their qualifications. Standard trainers may deliver only the approved Level 1 habits, assign or locate the Smart Meal Plan where the membership includes it, and direct a member to an approved Evolved article; they do not discuss personal calories, protein targets, supplement use, meal plans or clinical nutrition during personal-training sessions.

The Trainerize Smart Meal Plan is assigned at sale to every Strong and Fast Track member, and a standard trainer shows her where to find it. Member use is optional; trainers do not customise the resource, prescribe individual targets, monitor adherence or represent it as a complete solution.

An individual nutrition service with Megan is not currently packaged for sale. Therapeutic diets, medication interactions, nutrient-deficiency treatment and condition-specific clinical nutrition remain with a doctor, clinic or Accredited Practising Dietitian, regardless of what Evolved teaches publicly.

## DOC-NUT-0001 — Flexible Meal-Building Framework

| Field | Record |
| --- | --- |
| Domain | Nutrition |
| Statement | Evolved teaches general meal construction as a flexible skill: establish a practical eating opportunity; include a useful protein source; add plant variety; include carbohydrate and dietary fat according to the person's general context and preferences; make the meal satisfying and repeatable; then use non-diagnostic feedback to guide the next coaching conversation. |
| Primary status | Evolved position |
| Owner | Peter Brown |
| Accepted | 14 August 2026 |
| Review due | 14 August 2027 |
| Immediate-review triggers | New nutrition evidence materially changes the general scaffold; service or qualification changes; eating-disorder concerns; repeated adverse effects; new life-stage or clinical application; doctrine regression exposes conflict. |

### Why Evolved holds this position

- **Source teaching:** The corpus repeatedly supports simple meal construction, protein-containing foods, plant variety, practical preparation and skill development rather than permanent dependence on one rigid plan.
- **Evidence position:** Current evidence supports dietary-pattern fundamentals, flexible variety, adequate total protein and some optional self-monitoring uses. It does not establish one mandatory plate, meal frequency or tracking method. Exact quantities, nutrient timing, fixed food taxonomies and broad hormonal or disease outcomes remain individual, narrowed or rejected.
- **Evolved judgement:** Peter has selected flexible meal building as the general Evolved coaching scaffold because it fits current service truth and can be taught without prescribing a therapeutic diet or personal clinical target.

### Coaching application

1. **Create a practical eating opportunity.** Work with the person's routine rather than imposing a universal meal frequency.
2. **Include a useful protein source.** Standard coaching teaches the category and meal-building skill, not a body-weight-linked gram prescription.
3. **Add plant variety.** Use foods the person can access, tolerate, afford and enjoy; do not attach cure or detoxification claims.
4. **Include carbohydrate and dietary fat.** Use general context, preferences and meal satisfaction. Exact workout fuelling or personal targets require qualified individual work with Peter or Megan when that service is arranged.
5. **Make it workable.** Consider taste, culture, preparation, household needs, portability and cost.
6. **Review feedback.** Hunger, energy, enjoyment, digestion and training experience may inform discussion but are not diagnostic biomarkers.
7. **Progress toward autonomy.** Reduce unnecessary dependence as the member becomes able to build and adapt meals herself.

### Scope and exclusions

- The framework is a general construction prompt, not a mandatory plate, calorie target, macronutrient formula or fixed meal schedule.
- It does not require six meals, a three-hour workout window, higher- or lower-energy carbohydrate categories, an omega ratio, supplements or continuous tracking.
- It does not establish that a particular food prevents, treats or cures a disease, hormone issue, menstrual symptom or mental-health condition.
- Hunger, fatigue, cravings, digestive symptoms and body-weight change have multiple possible causes. Persistent, severe or concerning symptoms require appropriate clinical assessment.
- Tracking may be used only when suitable, consented and proportionate; it is neither universally necessary nor universally harmful.

## DOC-NUT-0002 — Adequacy Before Optimisation

| Field | Record |
| --- | --- |
| Domain | Nutrition |
| Statement | Evolved first supports an adequate, sustainable pattern of food and fluid that can meet ordinary life, health and training demands. Fine timing, supplements and optimisation tactics do not compensate for chronically insufficient or unworkable intake. |
| Primary status | Evolved position supported by evidence |
| Owner | Peter Brown |
| Accepted | 16 August 2026 |
| Review due | 16 August 2027 |
| Immediate-review triggers | Evidence materially changes energy-availability guidance; eating-disorder or REDs concern; pregnancy, illness or medication changes the boundary; service scope changes. |

### Why Evolved holds this position

Low energy availability can impair health and performance in relevant populations, while aggressive or poorly supported restriction may undermine training and recovery. Research does not allow a trainer to diagnose energy deficiency from hunger, fatigue, weight change or training performance alone.

### Coaching application

Begin with whether the woman has reliable access to practical meals, protein-containing foods, carbohydrate, dietary fat, plant variety and fluid. Reduce avoidable under-fuelling and friction before adding precision. Ask, observe and refer when risk patterns are present; do not calculate a therapeutic correction in standard coaching.

### Scope and exclusions

Adequacy does not require one meal frequency, calorie level, body size or food pattern. This position does not diagnose REDs, malnutrition or an eating disorder and does not prohibit a clinically appropriate energy deficit.

## DOC-NUT-0003 — Protein Supports the Loading Signal

| Field | Record |
| --- | --- |
| Domain | Nutrition |
| Statement | Protein adequacy supports muscle maintenance and adaptation, while progressive resistance training supplies the loading signal. For healthy adult women who strength train, Evolved uses 1.4–2.2 g/kg/day as the public evidence-based range, with the upper end prioritised in perimenopause, postmenopause, high training demand and selected energy-deficit contexts. Higher individual intakes may be used by a suitably qualified practitioner when the woman, goal, total diet and clinical context justify them; they are not the universal public target. |
| Primary status | Evidence-supported principle translated through an Evolved position |
| Owner | Peter Brown |
| Accepted | 16 August 2026 |
| Review due | 16 August 2027 |
| Immediate-review triggers | Material protein dose-response evidence in women; renal or clinical scope change; life-stage evidence changes; service boundary changes. |

### Why Evolved holds this position

The ISSN female-athlete position places women across reproductive stages within 1.4–2.2 g/kg/day and directs peri- and postmenopausal athletes toward the upper end. The wider ISSN protein position says 1.4–2.0 g/kg/day is sufficient for most exercising adults, while 2.3–3.1 g/kg/day may help preserve lean mass in lean resistance-trained people during an energy deficit and emerging studies above 3 g/kg/day concern selected healthy trained populations rather than a universal requirement.

Ageing and the menopausal transition can reduce anabolic sensitivity, so meal distribution and the upper end of the range may matter more. Protein remains one part of the system: progressive loading, total energy, carbohydrate, dietary fat, sleep and recovery still affect adaptation.

### Coaching application

Public Evolved education may explain the complete range, show worked calculations and identify the contexts that move a qualified decision upward or downward. Peter or Megan may discuss an individual target within recorded qualification; standard trainers teach only approved Level 1 habits and redirect the member to the article, Smart Meal Plan or qualified support.

Kidney disease, malnutrition, unexplained weight loss, frailty treatment and medically complex intake remain clinical. A response observed in an Evolved client may be presented as Evolved experience, but it does not prove that 3 g/kg/day is superior for all women.

### Scope and exclusions

No grams-per-kilogram target is imposed by a standard trainer. Protein does not replace total energy, carbohydrate, dietary fat, micronutrients, sleep or progressive loading.

## DOC-NUT-0004 — Dietary Pattern and Variety Over Superfoods

| Field | Record |
| --- | --- |
| Domain | Nutrition |
| Statement | Evolved prioritises a varied, workable dietary pattern over single-food, nutrient or superfood storytelling. Protein foods, plant variety, carbohydrate, dietary fat and fluids are assembled around access, culture, preference, tolerance, goals and repeatability. |
| Primary status | Evolved position supported by dietary-pattern evidence |
| Owner | Peter Brown |
| Accepted | 16 August 2026 |
| Review due | 16 August 2027 |
| Immediate-review triggers | Dietary-guideline or pattern evidence materially changes; allergy, pregnancy or clinical use exposes a scope conflict. |

### Why Evolved holds this position

Dietary-pattern evidence is more defensible than broad causal stories about one food, omega ratio or nutrient. Mediterranean-style and other minimally processed, varied patterns may support selected outcomes, but much evidence is observational, population-specific or outcome-specific.

### Coaching application

Help the woman create variety with foods she can access, afford, tolerate and enjoy. Use examples and substitutions, not moral categories. A pattern may accommodate cultural foods, convenience foods and pleasure without ceasing to be useful.

### Scope and exclusions

This is not a compulsory Mediterranean diet, clean-eating rule, processed-food ban or fixed omega-6:omega-3 ratio. It does not claim food treats hormonal, inflammatory, reproductive, mental-health or chronic-disease conditions.

| Field | Record |
| --- | --- |
| Domain | Nutrition |
| Statement | Energy balance remains relevant to body-weight change, while calorie estimates, food records, photographs, scales and body-composition devices are imperfect tools. Evolved may use optional, temporary and proportionate tracking to answer a defined learning question, then reduce it when it no longer helps. |
| Primary status | Evolved position supported by evidence and measurement limits |
| Owner | Peter Brown |
| Accepted | 16 August 2026 |
| Review due | 16 August 2027 |

### Why Evolved holds this position

Daily variation and estimation error do not invalidate energy balance, but they limit precision. Self-monitoring can help in some contexts and burden or harm in others. Body weight, fat mass, lean mass, skeletal muscle, strength and health are related but not interchangeable.

### Coaching application

Choose the least burdensome measure capable of answering the question. Explain uncertainty, obtain consent, review trends rather than one reading, and stop or refer when tracking becomes distressing, compulsive or clinically concerning. Explicit personal calorie or macronutrient targets require qualified individual work with Peter or Megan when arranged, or clinical care as applicable.

### Scope and exclusions

Tracking is neither mandatory nor inherently harmful. A device estimate is not a diagnosis, and body-weight change is not a complete measure of programme success or personal worth.

### Why Evolved holds this position

Carbohydrate can support demanding training and glycogen restoration; dietary fat contributes energy, essential fatty acids and food satisfaction; hydration supports ordinary function and performance. Evidence does not support a universal high/low-energy carbohydrate taxonomy, a fixed fluid-per-kilogram rule for everyone or the claim that carbohydrate outside a narrow training window becomes body fat.

### Coaching application

Standard coaching includes these categories in general meal building and observes practical response. ### Scope and exclusions

No universal carbohydrate window, fat percentage, omega ratio or water target is created here. Symptoms such as headache, nausea, fatigue or intense thirst are not interpreted as proof a plan is working.

Total intake and a workable distribution generally matter more than timing theatre. ## DOC-NUT-0008 — Supplements and Deficiencies Require a Defined Reason

| Field | Record |
| --- | --- |
| Domain | Nutrition |
| Statement | Supplements are optional tools for a defined, evidence-bounded purpose; they are not default requirements for being female, ageing, training or transforming. Possible deficiency or medication interaction prompts qualified assessment rather than trainer diagnosis or a generic supplement script. |
| Primary status | Evolved position and protected clinical boundary |
| Owner | Peter Brown |
| Accepted | 16 August 2026 |
| Review due | 16 August 2027 |
| Immediate-review triggers | Australian regulatory guidance changes; adverse event; qualification or service scope changes; material product evidence. |

### Why Evolved holds this position

Creatine, protein powders, omega-3, vitamin D, iron and other products have evidence in defined contexts, but effects vary with population, baseline status, dose, outcome and co-interventions. Symptoms such as fatigue are nonspecific. Therapeutic dosing, deficiency treatment and medication interactions require appropriately qualified care.

Ingredient evidence, commercial-product integrity and individual suitability are separate questions. The cross-pillar `supplementation-evidence-register.md` applies a five-gate test before any Evolved translation.

### Coaching application

Standard trainers may ask what a member uses when needed for safe training, avoid contradicting clinicians, recognise risk and refer. They do not recommend a supplement, dose it or use an Evolved article as authority to prescribe it.

Public Evolved education may explain that creatine monohydrate at 3–5 g/day is a well-supported adult strength and training option, with current evidence suggesting small lean-mass and strength benefits in postmenopausal women when combined with resistance training. Cognitive evidence is mixed: some syntheses report modest memory or processing effects, while another 2024 systematic review found the cognition results equivocal.

Current pooled evidence has not established a bone-mineral-density benefit. Creatine may support a wider strength and function strategy, but it must not be marketed as osteoporosis treatment, a proven bone-density supplement or a guaranteed mental-clarity intervention.

### Scope and exclusions

This does not mean supplements never work. It prohibits universal necessity, “no drawbacks,” deficiency inference from lifestyle or symptoms, and routine treatment claims.

Routine public creatine guidance does not extend to minors, pregnancy, breastfeeding, kidney disease, medication interaction or another clinically complex context. Those decisions require the appropriate qualified or clinical pathway.

## DOC-NUT-0009 — Preserve Function During Body-Composition Change

| Field | Record |
| --- | --- |
| Domain | Nutrition |
| Statement | When body composition or weight changes are goals, Evolved protects dietary adequacy, strength, function, training tolerance and sustainable behaviour rather than pursuing scale loss alone. Weight, fat, lean mass, skeletal muscle, strength and health are separate outcomes and must not be treated as interchangeable. |
| Primary status | Evolved position supported by evidence |
| Owner | Peter Brown |
| Accepted | 16 August 2026 |
| Review due | 16 August 2027 |
| Immediate-review triggers | Body-composition measurement evidence changes; medical weight-management service is created; adverse eating or training outcomes. |

### Why Evolved holds this position

Diet often drives more weight loss while resistance training contributes strength, function and body-composition effects. Exercise cannot guarantee fat-only loss or perfect preservation of measured lean mass. Medically assisted weight loss may involve variable lean-mass change, but Evolved does not oppose, prescribe or manage medication.

### Coaching application

Track outcomes relevant to the member's goals, including performance and capability where appropriate. Support prescribed-medication users without stigma, stay aligned with the prescriber, and escalate unexplained weight loss, intolerance, low intake or clinical concern.

### Scope and exclusions

No weekly weight-loss guarantee, muscle-preservation guarantee or medication judgement is permitted. A body-composition device's lean-mass estimate is not identical to skeletal muscle.

## DOC-NUT-0010 — Symptoms Are Information, Not Nutrition Diagnoses

| Field | Record |
| --- | --- |
| Domain | Nutrition |
| Statement | Hunger, cravings, fatigue, mood, digestion, menstrual symptoms, body-weight fluctuation and training performance may inform a coaching conversation but do not diagnose nutrient deficiency, metabolism, hormones or disease. Persistent, severe, unusual or concerning patterns require appropriate referral. |
| Primary status | Evolved position and clinical boundary |
| Owner | Peter Brown |
| Accepted | 16 August 2026 |
| Review due | 16 August 2027 |
| Immediate-review triggers | Red-flag or referral guidance changes; adverse event; qualification or service pathway changes. |

### Why Evolved holds this position

Subjective feedback is useful but nonspecific. The source corpus contains unsupported explanations involving cortisol, metabolic fuel shifts, cravings, inflammation, hormones, contraception and reproductive conditions. These cannot be rescued by anecdotes or general nutrition evidence.

### Coaching application

Listen without dismissing the symptom, clarify context and duration, avoid assigning a mechanism, make only a safe general coaching adjustment, and refer when scope or risk requires it. Trainers never advise starting, stopping or changing medication or contraception.

### Scope and exclusions

Standard nutrition coaching does not treat PMS, PCOS, endometriosis, infertility, gestational diabetes, menopause symptoms, gastrointestinal disease, mental-health conditions or eating disorders. Member stories establish individual experience, not causality.

## Current doctrine boundary

These ten statements form the foundational parent Nutrition Doctrine. On 18 August 2026 Peter replaced the earlier cautious public-education posture with the full-public-education and restricted-delivery model: Evolved articles may teach detailed evidence-linked frameworks, while only suitably nutrition-qualified people may individualise or discuss those frameworks with a member.

The protein framework now follows the ISSN female-athlete range of 1.4–2.2 g/kg/day, with perimenopause and postmenopause biased toward approximately 1.8–2.2 and higher intakes reserved for qualified individual use rather than a universal claim. Creatine 3–5 g/day may be taught for appropriate nonpregnant adults, with strength and lean-mass evidence separated from uncertain bone-density and mixed cognitive evidence.

The Trainerize Smart Meal Plan remains assigned at sale to every Strong and Fast Track member, while use is optional. Standard trainers assign and locate it but do not customise or coach from it; no commercial individual nutrition offer is currently defined.
