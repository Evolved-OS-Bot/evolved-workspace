# Evolved Sleep and Recovery Doctrine

**Owner:** Peter Brown  
**Status:** Canonical foundational Sleep/Recovery Doctrine; evidence expansion and local regression complete; downstream implementation not authorised  
**Version:** 2.2  
**As of:** 17 August 2026

This is the canonical authority for general Evolved sleep and recovery coaching. It governs what trainers may notice, discuss and adjust without turning fitness coaching into sleep medicine, psychotherapy, supplement prescription or diagnosis.

## Protected service and clinical boundary

Standard Evolved coaching may discuss sleep opportunity, regularity, practical routines, bedroom friction, caffeine and alcohol context, subjective recovery and training-demand adjustment.

Trainers do not diagnose or treat insomnia, sleep apnoea, a mental-health condition, endocrine or “adrenal” dysfunction, chronic fatigue, a medication effect or another clinical cause. They do not deliver CBT-I, sleep-restriction therapy, supplement protocols or medication advice.

Persistent sleep difficulty, material daytime impairment, witnessed breathing pauses, gasping or choking during sleep, dangerous sleepiness, concerning mood or behaviour change, or another red flag follows the appropriate GP, sleep clinician, mental-health or urgent-care route.

## Doctrine relationship

```text
Protect realistic sleep opportunity
  -> reduce one useful source of friction
  -> observe sleep and daytime function without diagnosis
  -> match training demand to present capacity
  -> review the response across time
  -> retain, adjust or refer
```

## DOC-SLP-0001 — Protect Sleep Opportunity and Rhythm

| Field | Record |
| --- | --- |
| Domain | Sleep and Recovery |
| Statement | Evolved first protects a realistic opportunity for sleep and a repeatable sleep-wake rhythm. For a generally healthy adult, seven or more hours is an appropriate population-level opportunity floor, while individual need, caring demands, shift work, life stage, symptoms and clinician advice remain relevant. |
| Primary status | Evidence-supported consensus translated through an Evolved position |
| Owner | Peter Brown |
| Accepted | 14 August 2026 |
| Review due | 14 August 2027 |
| Immediate-review triggers | New AASM or Australian guidance; application to minors; pregnancy or clinical sleep pathway; the number becomes a rigid compliance score. |

### Coaching application

1. Ask whether the member has enough realistic time and conditions for sleep.
2. Explore the pattern across several nights rather than judging one night.
3. Identify constraints such as caring, work, symptoms, pain, shift schedules or environment.
4. Select one feasible protection step only when the member wants it.
5. Refer when the problem is persistent, impairing or clinically concerning.

### Scope and exclusions

- Seven hours is a general adult floor, not a guaranteed optimum for every person.
- Time in bed, wearable-recorded sleep and perceived sleep are not interchangeable.
- Coaches do not shame short sleep or imply it is always a choice.

## DOC-SLP-0002 — The Sleep-Support Experiment

| Field | Record |
| --- | --- |
| Domain | Sleep and Recovery |
| Statement | General sleep coaching uses the smallest practical experiment that may reduce friction: choose one modifiable factor, test it without perfectionism, observe the response and retain, adjust or stop it. |
| Primary status | Evolved position with component evidence |
| Owner | Peter Brown |
| Accepted | 14 August 2026 |
| Review due | 14 August 2027 |
| Immediate-review triggers | Sleep advice delays clinical care; a practice increases anxiety; a trainer begins delivering insomnia treatment; accessibility or caring constraints are ignored. |

### Coaching application

Possible general experiments include protecting a wind-down transition, making the room more comfortable, reducing avoidable interruption, reviewing late stimulant use or moving a demanding task earlier. The member selects what is realistic.

The experiment has a clear purpose and a short review period. Failure is information about fit or context, not evidence of low discipline.

### Scope and exclusions

- General sleep-support education is not CBT-I and is not treatment for chronic insomnia.
- Trainers do not prescribe sleep restriction, supplements, devices or medication changes.
- No universal “perfect routine,” screen cutoff, bedroom temperature or bedtime is accepted.

## DOC-SLP-0003 — Caffeine and Alcohol Are Context, Not Character

| Field | Record |
| --- | --- |
| Domain | Sleep and Recovery |
| Statement | Caffeine dose and timing, and alcohol use near sleep, are practical sleep inputs that may be discussed without moral judgement. The coaching response is awareness and a voluntary timing or dose experiment, not a universal ban or biochemical diagnosis. |
| Primary status | Evidence-supported general position |
| Owner | Peter Brown |
| Accepted | 14 August 2026 |
| Review due | 14 August 2027 |
| Immediate-review triggers | New interaction or safety concern; pregnancy or clinical application; substance-use concern; product-specific recommendation. |

### Coaching application

- Establish the actual product, approximate amount and timing before drawing a conclusion.
- Consider bedtime, sensitivity, shift work and the member's observed response.
- Test an earlier or smaller amount if suitable.
- Treat persistent reliance, withdrawal concerns, medication questions or substance-use concerns through the appropriate qualified route.

### Scope and exclusions

- Reject the claims that caffeine peaks nine hours after use or “fries” the adrenal system.
- Do not claim alcohol improves restorative sleep because it can make someone feel sleepy.
- Do not prescribe L-theanine, tyrosine or another supplement to offset caffeine or sleep loss.

| Field | Record |
| --- | --- |
| Domain | Sleep and Recovery |
| Statement | Sleep quality, sleep opportunity, daytime sleepiness, fatigue and wearable data may inform a coaching conversation, but no single report or device score diagnoses sleep quality, readiness or a disorder. |

| Accepted | 14 August 2026 |
| Review due | 14 August 2027 |
| Immediate-review triggers | New device introduced into service; score-based eligibility or training automation; tracking anxiety; suspected sleep disorder. |

### Coaching application

Use the least burdensome observation that answers the question. Look for repeated patterns, the member's daytime function and training experience.

Wearable data is optional. It may support curiosity, but it cannot overrule symptoms, clinician guidance or safe coaching judgement.

### Referral boundary

Refer rather than coach around persistent insomnia, significant daytime impairment, witnessed breathing pauses, loud snoring with gasping or choking, dangerous sleepiness, or another concerning symptom. Urgent safety and mental-health concerns use the applicable emergency pathway.

## DOC-REC-0001 — Demand-Capacity Matching

| Field | Record |
| --- | --- |
| Domain | Sleep and Recovery |
| Statement | The training decision matches the planned demand to the member's current capacity using symptoms, sleep, subjective recovery, recent training, energy availability, life stress, clinical restrictions, technique and goals. No single signal automatically determines the session. |
| Primary status | Evolved position with partial evidence support and existing Manual authority |
| Owner | Peter Brown |
| Accepted | 14 August 2026 |
| Review due | 14 August 2027 |
| Immediate-review triggers | New safety evidence; conflict with clinician instruction; automated readiness scoring; adverse event; programming-authority change. |

### Coaching application

The trainer may retain the session, reduce load or volume, change exercise selection, extend rest, change the session goal, use lower-intensity work, or stop and refer. The choice must preserve safety and the purpose of the programme without treating discomfort, fatigue or one poor night as a moral failure.

Reduced sleep does not automatically cancel training. It changes the information available for the session decision.

### Scope and exclusions

- Trainers do not diagnose overtraining, low energy availability, illness, hormone dysfunction or chronic fatigue.
- No proprietary readiness score or acute:chronic workload ratio is accepted as a universal rule.
- Symptoms and current clinical restrictions outrank performance ambition.

## DOC-REC-0002 — Recovery Is Built Across the Week

| Field | Record |
| --- | --- |
| Domain | Sleep and Recovery |
| Statement | Recovery is created by the relationship between training demand, sleep opportunity, adequate food and fluid, lower-demand periods, ordinary life stress and time. Evolved plans demanding work, lower-intensity work and deload or reassessment periods as a coherent week and block rather than selling one recovery hack. |
| Primary status | Evolved programming position with bounded evidence support |
| Owner | Peter Brown |
| Accepted | 14 August 2026 |
| Review due | 14 August 2027 |
| Immediate-review triggers | Programme architecture change; supplement or recovery-device inclusion; new evidence materially changes spacing or deload practice; clinical application. |

### Coaching application

- Preserve the programmed purpose of high-, moderate- and lower-demand sessions.
- Avoid adding demanding work automatically when capacity or recent load does not support it.
- Use lower-intensity activity as an option, not a punishment or method to erase food.
- Preserve deload and reassessment periods under current Manual authority.
- Review persistent recovery problems rather than repeatedly overriding them.

### Scope and exclusions

- Recovery is not guaranteed by a food, supplement, sauna, wearable or precise nutrient window.
- Standard coaching does not prescribe supplement stacks or clinical recovery protocols.
- “More recovery” is not always “less training”; the useful balance depends on demand, capacity and goal.

## DOC-SLP-0005 — Sleep Loss Changes Capacity, Not Character

| Field | Record |
| --- | --- |
| Domain | Sleep and Recovery |
| Statement | Reduced sleep can affect attention, perceived effort, mood, skill, strength, power and recovery, but the direction and magnitude vary. Evolved treats sleep loss as capacity information, not a character judgement or an automatic command to cancel or complete the planned session unchanged. |
| Primary status | Evidence-supported principle translated through an Evolved position |
| Owner | Peter Brown |
| Accepted | 17 August 2026 |
| Review due | 17 August 2027 |
| Immediate-review triggers | Dangerous sleepiness; safety-critical work or travel; acute illness; rapidly changing mood or behaviour; new sleep-loss performance synthesis. |

### Coaching application

Ask about sleep opportunity, current alertness, symptoms, recent load and the demands of the planned session. Retain, reduce, substitute, shorten or stop the session according to the whole picture. Treat dangerous sleepiness and substantial functional impairment as safety and referral issues.

### Scope and exclusions

- One poor night does not prove lost adaptation, hormonal disruption or overtraining.
- Exercise can remain useful under some sleep-loss conditions, but it does not cancel sleep loss.
- Reduced motivation or performance after poor sleep is not a discipline failure.

## DOC-REC-0004 — Recovery Modalities Are Optional Adjuncts

| Field | Record |
| --- | --- |
| Domain | Sleep and Recovery |
| Statement | Heat, cold, massage, compression, foam rolling and similar modalities may alter soreness, comfort or selected short-term performance measures in some contexts. They are optional adjuncts and do not replace sleep opportunity, adequate food and fluid, sensible programme design, time or clinical care. |
| Primary status | Evidence-supported boundary and Evolved position |
| Owner | Peter Brown |
| Accepted | 17 August 2026 |
| Review due | 17 August 2027 |
| Immediate-review triggers | A modality becomes a service inclusion; safety or contraindication evidence changes; a modality is presented as necessary or therapeutic. |

### Coaching application

Begin with the recovery foundations, the training adaptation being pursued and the next performance demand. If a safe modality is desired, choose it for a defined purpose, explain uncertainty and stop if it is unpleasant or unsuitable. Reduced soreness does not prove restored tissue capacity.

When strength or hypertrophy is the priority, routine immediate cold-water immersion after every lifting session is not the default because repeated use may compete with adaptation. Occasional cold may still be reasonable for a defined short-term comfort or competition purpose. Sauna or heat is also an additional dose: it may support heat acclimation or selected performance outcomes, but it is not automatically superior recovery.

### Scope and exclusions

- Immediate, next-day and long-term adaptation outcomes are different.
- Cold may reduce soreness yet impair immediate explosive performance in some settings.
- Evidence that repeated post-lifting cold can blunt hypertrophy is credible but predominantly male; direct female recovery trials are small, short and mixed. Evolved does not claim cold is uniquely harmful to women.
- Female heat research supports heat acclimation and contains promising performance signals, but does not establish that sauna improves hypertrophy, speeds general recovery or is better than cold for women.
- Standard coaching does not prescribe therapeutic heat, cold or recovery-device protocols.

## DOC-REC-0005 — Define the Recovery Outcome

| Field | Record |
| --- | --- |
| Domain | Sleep and Recovery |
| Statement | Evolved defines what “recovery” means before interpreting a practice or measurement. Soreness, comfort, fatigue, range of motion, strength, power, tissue healing, sleep, biomarkers, readiness scores and long-term adaptation are related but not interchangeable outcomes. |
| Primary status | Evidence-supported measurement boundary and Evolved position |
| Owner | Peter Brown |
| Accepted | 17 August 2026 |
| Review due | 17 August 2027 |
| Immediate-review triggers | A biomarker, wearable, modality or symptom is proposed as a stand-alone readiness, healing or adaptation measure; a new validated multidimensional recovery standard becomes available. |

### Coaching application

Ask what outcome matters and when it matters. “I feel less sore” may be a valid comfort outcome; it does not by itself show that strength, tissue capacity or adaptation has returned. A lower CK value, faster lactate clearance, altered blood flow, higher HRV or greater sweat loss is not automatically a better member outcome.

Use member report, symptoms, recent load, observed movement and performance, life context and relevant clinical guidance together. When those signals disagree, do not let the easiest number erase the rest of the picture.

### Scope and exclusions

- DOMS is not caused by retained lactate.
- Soreness is neither required for adaptation nor a complete marker of workout quality.
- Stretching and cool-downs may serve preference, transition or mobility without being promised as soreness prevention or faster recovery.
- Massage, rolling, compression and similar tools may change comfort without proving healing.
- Sweat volume is not detoxification, fat loss or training quality.
- Standard trainers do not interpret clinical biomarkers or diagnose tissue damage, dehydration, electrolyte disorders or overtraining.
