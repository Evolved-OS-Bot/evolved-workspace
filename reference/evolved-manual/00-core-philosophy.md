## The Core Belief

Women don't need less intensity — they need the right intensity, applied consistently.

Most women are undertrained. Not because they lack effort, but because they've been directed toward cardio, low-load circuits, and modalities that feel hard but don't create the physiological stimulus required for lasting change. The Evolved exists to correct that.

Strength training — done properly, with progressive overload and adequate volume — is the single most important thing a woman can do for her body composition, hormonal health, bone density, metabolic rate, and functional capacity as she ages. Everything we do is built around this.

---

## The Key Principles

### Principle 1 — Strength is Healthcare

Strength training at the right parameters is the primary evidence-based intervention against sarcopenia, osteoporosis, metabolic decline, and loss of functional independence. It is not an optional lifestyle choice — it is the most important health decision a woman can make.

Every trainer at The Evolved carries this conviction. When a client pushes back, questions the method, or asks for cardio instead — the trainer's job is to educate, not to comply.

### Principle 2 — Muscle is the Organ of Longevity

**Sarcopenia** — the age-related loss of muscle mass — begins in a woman's 30s and accelerates through menopause. Without an adequate training stimulus, women lose approximately **3–8% of muscle mass per decade**. This compounds into:

- Reduced metabolic rate
- Decreased bone density
- Hormonal disruption
- Loss of functional independence in the final decades of life

Muscle is not an aesthetic goal — it is the biological mechanism of longevity. Every session delivered at The Evolved is, in effect, a health intervention.

### Principle 3 — Consistency Beats Intensity

The minimum effective dose is 2–3 structured strength sessions per week, held consistently over months and years. A woman who trains at 7–8 RPE twice a week for 12 months will outperform a woman who maxes out for 3 weeks and stops.

The Evolved is not built for transformation sprints. It is built for the long arc. Coaches should frame every conversation — initial consultation, monthly check-in, difficult moment — in terms of the long game.

**Timeframe guide for client conversations:**
- **Months 1–3:** Learning phase — building the habit and the movement patterns
- **Months 4–6:** Results become visible and start to compound
- **12 months+:** Every successful client has stayed at least this long
- Every cell in the body is replaced over a 6-month period — results take time

### Principle 4 — The Key Performance Habits (KPH)

These are not suggestions. Every trainer applies these standards across every client, every session.

**KPH 1 — Training Intensity**

Women must lift with adequate intensity **2–3 times per week** to achieve optimal results.

| Parameter | Target |
|---|---|
| Rep range | 6–12 RM |
| Intensity | 70–85% of 1RM |
| Volume | 6–8 sets per session |

Working within these parameters ensures the training stimulus is sufficient to drive adaptation — muscle retention, body composition change, strength gains, and metabolic improvement. Below this threshold, sessions feel like exercise but don't produce transformation.

**KPH 2 — Movement Pattern Coverage**

Every week, sessions must cover all 6 foundational movement patterns at least once:

**Squat · Hinge · Lunge · Push · Pull · Carry**

These patterns map to how the human body moves in real life. Together, they ensure balanced development, reduce injury risk, and build functional strength that serves women across decades — not just in the gym.

Intensity without breadth creates imbalance. Breadth without intensity creates activity. Both together create results.

## How KPH Is Applied

### Group Classes

All group classes (Sculpt & Strength, Build & Balance, Metabolic Burn, Hybrid Fitness Racing, Evolved Pilates) are programmed to cumulatively meet the intensity and movement standards across the week. Members attending 2–3 classes per week will meet minimum KPH if classes are delivered to standard.

### 1:1 Personal Training

| Logic | Membership | Application |
|---|---|---|
| **Logic A** | Fast Track (PT + SGPT) | 1:1 session must meet intensity and movement criteria. If client has attended all 3 S&S sessions that week: use regular program. If not: use a Sculpt & Strength workout (Kettlebell icon in Trainerize) |
| **Logic B** | 2+ PT sessions per week, no SGPT | Base all sessions on the Evolved 1:1 Periodised Program. Meet intensity and movement benchmarks every session |
| **Logic C** | 1 PT session per week, no SGPT | Follow the 1:1 Periodised Program. Hit intensity and movement benchmarks |
| **Logic D** | Client requests cardio / HIIT / alternate focus | Adapt only after educating the client on the strength-first rationale |

## Coach Scripts

### Scenario: Member asks "Why is your gym different?"

> *"Most gyms put women on treadmills and tell them to burn calories. We do the opposite — we build strength. Here's why that matters: from your 30s onward, women start losing muscle mass. That leads to a slower metabolism, weaker bones, and less independence as you age. The research is clear — strength training is the single most effective intervention. We're a women-only space, so there's no intimidation, no ego. Just structured, science-backed training delivered by coaches who specialise in female physiology. Our members don't just feel better in the gym — they feel stronger in their actual lives."*

### Scenario: Client requests cardio or HIIT instead of strength work

> *"I hear you — and I want to be upfront about where our expertise is. Our specialty is strength-based transformation. The research shows that for your goals — [insert their goal] — strength training produces better long-term results than cardio alone. Cardio feels harder, but it doesn't produce the hormonal, metabolic, or body composition changes that strength does. Our responsibility is to make sure you know the most effective path. Can we try it our way for a few weeks and see how it feels?"*

## Common Mistakes

- Overemphasising weight loss over strength and health
- Talking about class formats before outcomes
- Using bodybuilding or aesthetic-first language
- Softening the KPH standard to accommodate a client preference
- Framing strength training as one option among many — it is the primary recommendation

## How This Shapes the Business

- **Copy and marketing** — we lead with strength, not calorie burn. We speak to what women's bodies actually need, not what they've been told to want.
- **Member education** — every new member is taught the KPH before their program begins. Understanding the why creates commitment to the how.
- **Member results** — transformation stories are grounded in these standards. When members hit results, it's because they met the threshold.
- **Trainer hiring and onboarding** — we hire for conviction in this method, not just technical skill. Trainers who don't believe in strength for women don't belong here.
- **Course design** — the trainer onboarding portal is built around internalising and applying this method, not just following checklists.
