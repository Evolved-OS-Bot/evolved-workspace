## Purpose

PRIME is the real-time coaching framework used by every trainer at The Evolved. It is not a cue system — it is a **decision-making framework**. It defines what coaches observe, how they diagnose, and when they progress.

PRIME ensures that a session delivered by any trainer, to any member, on any day produces the same quality of outcome.

---

## Learning Outcomes

After reading this section coaches should be able to:
- Apply the PRIME framework to any exercise in the Evolved program
- Use PRIME to diagnose and correct in real time without stopping the session
- Identify the correct limiting factor for each member before intervening
- Earn member progression through PRIME — not give it automatically
- Explain why PRIME exists and what problem it solves

---

## Why PRIME Exists

The challenge at The Evolved was never programming.

The challenge was: **how do we deliver a world-class strength session to 15 women regardless of which coach is standing on the floor?**

Most gyms coach exercises. They coach workouts. They coach programs.

PRIME coaches **movement quality, observation, and decision-making.**

It creates a repeatable coaching lens. Every coach at The Evolved is looking at the same things, asking the same questions, making decisions the same way. The result is consistent outcomes regardless of who delivers the session.

---

## Where PRIME Sits in the Evolved System

```
Assessment → Programming → PRIME → Results
```

- **Assessment** (Section 02) tells us: what needs improving?
- **Programming** (Section 04) tells us: what exercises will improve it?
- **PRIME** tells us: how do we coach it in real time?

PRIME is the delivery engine of the entire Evolved Method. Without it, the assessment and the program exist on paper. PRIME is what makes them work on the floor.

## The PRIME Framework

| Stage | Coach Question |
|---|---|
| **P** — Posture | Are they in the right position? |
| **R** — Range of Motion | Can they access the movement safely? |
| **I** — Identify Areas of Improvement | What is the limiting factor? |
| **M** — Measure & Monitor | How do we know they're improving? |
| **E** — Evaluate & Evolve | What does their performance show, and what is the next progression? |

> PRIME ensures every coaching decision at The Evolved follows a consistent process. Coaches do not simply instruct exercises. They observe posture, assess range, identify limitations, measure progress, evaluate performance, and evolve the member toward greater strength, health, and independence.

---

### P — Posture

**Can the member achieve and maintain the correct position?**

This is the foundation. Position determines performance. A member who cannot achieve the correct starting position cannot execute the movement safely or effectively.

Posture checklist:
- Is the spine neutral?
- Is the pelvis controlled?
- Is the ribcage positioned correctly?
- Are the shoulders stable?

**Coaching principle:** Never progress past Posture until the position is owned. A heavier load on a compromised position produces injury, not adaptation.

---

### R — Range of Motion

**Can the member access the movement safely?**

This is not about flexibility for its own sake. The goal is **usable range** — the ability to move through the full intended pattern under control.

Range of motion questions:
- Can they squat to the nexus point?
- Can they hinge with a neutral spine through full range?
- Can they reach overhead without compensating at the ribcage?
- Is mobility limiting their performance?

**Coaching principle:** Work within the member's available range and expand it progressively. Never force range — earn it.

---

### I — Identify Areas of Improvement

**What is the limiting factor?**

This is the diagnostic stage — where coaching becomes individualised. Two women performing the same exercise may have completely different limiting factors. The exercise is the same; the coaching response is different.

Possible limiting factors:
- Mobility
- Stability
- Strength
- Confidence / fear of load
- Coordination
- Breathing and bracing mechanics

**Coaching principle:** Observe first. Diagnose before you intervene. A coach who corrects without identifying the root cause addresses symptoms, not causes.

---

### M — Measure & Monitor

**How do we know they're improving?**

Progress must be visible — to the coach and to the member. This aligns directly with the assessment philosophy: Diagnosis → Direction → Data → Retention.

Measurable markers:
- Load (kg lifted)
- Reps completed
- Attendance and consistency
- Strength Assessment scores
- Functional benchmark results (Live / Long / Perform levels)
- Movement pattern quality — can they now access depth, range, or position they couldn't before?

**Coaching principle:** If it isn't tracked, it isn't coached. Record everything in Trainerize every session.

---

### E — Evaluate & Evolve

**What does their performance show, and what is the next progression?**

First evaluate the member's performance against posture, range, competency, load, reps and movement quality. Then decide whether to consolidate, regress or evolve the movement.

The member earns progression. It is not automatic. Once posture is owned, range is accessible and competency is demonstrated consistently, progression is appropriate.

Progression options:
- Progress load (add weight)
- Progress complexity (harder movement pattern)
- Progress volume (more sets)
- Progress capacity (shorter rest, more sessions per week)

**Coaching principle:** Progression that is earned is progression that sticks. Premature progression is one of the most common coaching failures — it looks like progress but produces injury or regression.

## PRIME as the Evolved Movement Standard

In practice, PRIME is not a checklist coaches run through consciously before each cue. It is a coaching lens that becomes second nature.

A trained coach applying PRIME:
- Scans for posture automatically as the member sets up
- Observes range as the movement begins
- Identifies the limiting factor in the first 2–3 reps
- Tracks load and reps mentally (and records them in Trainerize)
- Makes a progression decision at the end of each session

This is what separates a trainer who delivers sessions from a coach who produces results.

## Application by Movement Pattern

### Squat

| Stage | What to look for |
|---|---|
| Posture | Neutral spine, controlled pelvis, braced core, chest up |
| Range | Depth to nexus point — hamstrings and calves in contact |
| Identify | Ankle mobility? Hip mobility? Stability? Load confidence? |
| Measure | Load, reps, depth achieved, exercise level (Goblet → Nexus Point) |
| Evaluate & Evolve | Evaluate posture, range and competency; then progress exercise or load when they are consistent |

### Hinge

| Stage | What to look for |
|---|---|
| Posture | Neutral spine, shoulders pinned back, weight in heels |
| Range | Full hip crease to lockout — no rounding at the bottom |
| Identify | Hamstring mobility? Spinal stability? Hip hinge pattern understanding? |
| Measure | Load, reps, RPE and exercise level (Broomstick Hinge → Dumbbell Romanian Deadlift → Barbell Romanian Deadlift → Trap Bar Deadlift → Barbell Deadlift) |
| Evaluate & Evolve | Advance one stage only after 10 pain-free, repeatable reps meet PRIME and remain below 7 RPE. Before leaving Barbell Romanian Deadlift, require 12 reps at 35 kg total load with a controlled one-second hold just below mid-knee on every rep in a PRIME postural position. Stop at the highest proficient stage at about 7 RPE; regress if PRIME, comfort or safety breaks down |

### Push

| Stage | What to look for |
|---|---|
| Posture | Scapula retraction, arch, active hips, wrists stacked |
| Range | Full chest contact at the bottom, full lockout at the top |
| Identify | Shoulder stability? Scapular control? Rib flare under load? |
| Measure | Load, reps, exercise level (DB → Barbell) |
| Evaluate & Evolve | Evaluate scapula retraction, arch and active hips; then progress to barbell when they are consistent above 15kg total |

### Pull

| Stage | What to look for |
|---|---|
| Posture | Shoulder blades depressed and retracted, no shrugging |
| Range | Full extension at the bottom, chin over bar / elbows to ribs |
| Identify | Grip strength? Lat engagement? Scapular stability? |
| Measure | Load, reps, band assistance level, bodyweight achievement |
| Evaluate & Evolve | Evaluate grip, lat engagement and scapular control; then reduce assistance, add load or increase range |

### Lunge

| Stage | What to look for |
|---|---|
| Posture | Upright torso, controlled pelvis, front knee tracking over toe |
| Range | Rear knee close to the ground without contact |
| Identify | Hip flexor mobility? Quad strength? Balance and stability? |
| Measure | Load, reps, exercise level (split squat → reverse lunge → walking lunge) |
| Evaluate & Evolve | Evaluate control, balance and stability; then progress difficulty, add load or reduce support |

### Carry

| Stage | What to look for |
|---|---|
| Posture | Tall spine, shoulders packed, no lean |
| Range | Full stride — no shortened gait under load |
| Identify | Grip failure? Core collapse? Shoulder elevation? |
| Measure | Load, duration, Live / Long / Perform benchmark progress |
| Evaluate & Evolve | Evaluate posture, grip and gait under load; then increase load or duration, or introduce single-arm variations |

## Coach Standards

- All coaches must be able to explain PRIME in under 60 seconds
- PRIME is applied to every working set — not selectively
- Coaching interventions must follow the framework: observe → diagnose → cue → measure
- Evaluate & Evolve decisions are made using PRIME criteria, not client request
