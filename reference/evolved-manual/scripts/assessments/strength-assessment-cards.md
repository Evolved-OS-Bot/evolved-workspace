# Strength Assessment Cards — Reference

**Source:** Physical Strength Assessment Cards (7 life-stage versions)
**Last Updated:** 2026-06-19

---

## Overview

The Evolved uses 7 life-stage-specific Strength Assessment Cards. All cards use identical numerical standards across the 4 tests. What differs is the messaging, rationale, and framing — tailored to the specific priorities and concerns of each life stage.

**Card subtitle format:** "Strength & Longevity Assessment — For [life-stage-specific benefit]"

---

## The 4 Tests

| Test | Exercise | What it measures |
|---|---|---|
| **Single-Leg Capacity Test (R)** | ATG Split Squat | Unilateral leg strength, balance, mobility, symmetry |
| **Single-Leg Capacity Test (L)** | ATG Split Squat | Unilateral leg strength, balance, mobility, symmetry |
| **Grip Endurance Test** | Farmers Carry | Grip strength, carrying capacity, whole-body endurance |
| **Spinal Control Test** | Plank / Side Plank / Toes to Bar | Core stability, spinal control, anti-rotation strength |

---

## Universal Standards (all life stages)

| Test | Live | Long | Perform |
|---|---|---|---|
| Single-Leg Capacity Test (R) | 10/side — full depth | 10/side + 50% bodyweight | 10/side + 100% bodyweight |
| Single-Leg Capacity Test (L) | 10/side — full depth | 10/side + 50% bodyweight | 10/side + 100% bodyweight |
| Grip Endurance Test | 1:00 + 75% bodyweight | 1:00 + 100% bodyweight | 1:00 + 150% bodyweight |
| Spinal Control Test | 2:00 plank (hands + toes) | 2:00 side plank | 10 Toes to Bar |

---

## Progress Timeline

Each level typically takes approximately 6 months of consistent training to achieve:

```
Start → [6 months] → Live → [6 months] → (midpoint) → [6 months] → Long → [6 months] → Perform
```

*Typical time frames assume consistent, progressive strength training 3 days per week. Progress varies based on training history, health, and recovery.*

## Life-Stage Card Specifications

### Teen
**Subtitle:** For confidence, coordination, and long-term health
**Tagline:** *Strength is a skill you build over time.*
**Back headline:** How your body learns and what strength builds

| Test | Protects | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Grip Endurance | Bone growth, whole-body strength, coordination | Grip endurance reflects overall strength development and bone loading |
| Spinal Control | Posture, coordination, movement confidence | Good spinal control supports confident, controlled full-body movement |
| Single-Leg Capacity | Balance, athletic ability, injury prevention | Single-leg strength supports sport performance and safe movement |
| L-R Symmetry | Movement symmetry, joint health, coordination | Asymmetries affect skill development and movement efficiency |

**Bottom statement:** *Strength helps your body feel capable and confident.*

## Notes on Test Naming by Life Stage

The Post-Menopause card uses slightly different test names that reflect the priority shift at this life stage:
- "Single-Leg Capacity Test" → "Single-Leg **Independence** Test"
- "Grip Endurance Test" → "Grip **Capacity** Test"

The standards remain identical. The naming shift reinforces independence as the primary outcome for this cohort.
