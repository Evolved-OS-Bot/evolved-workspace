## Before the Assessment

**Know your prospect.** Take 5–10 minutes — the night before, before your shift, or prior to the assessment — to read their pre-qualification summary or review their conversation history in Lead Connector. This level of preparation is non-negotiable at The Evolved.

Treat a trainer-actionable injury handoff as complete unless the PAR-Q or a current safety concern requires clarification. Do not restart the injury interview simply because more questions could be asked.

Treat trusted practitioner observations as useful context, not as a diagnosis independently confirmed by The Evolved.

**Set up the Trainerize account before they arrive:**
1. The trainer purchases the `Initial Consultation` Trainerize product for the prospect
2. Open the resulting Trainerize sign-up link on the Sales iPad
3. Use Lead Connector to pre-fill their details
4. Password: `Evolved2026`
5. Agree to terms and continue
6. Set timezone to Brisbane
6. Enter their phone number
7. Have this screen loaded and ready before the assessment begins

**Be outside to greet them.** Stand at the top of the stairs or ramp with Lead Connector open, ready to call or text if they need help finding you. If they're lost — go upstairs and find them.

## Part 1 — Initial Consultation

### Introduction

> First impressions last. We are a private space that can feel hard to find and intimidating. Nothing fixes that faster than a big, warm smile and genuine energy. Be the coach they hoped they'd meet.

**When the client walks in:**

1. Greet them warmly, iPad in hand
2. Build rapport — *"How did you go finding us? Do you live close by?"*
3. Pre-frame the session:
   > *"Today I'll be working with you to find your starting point. By the end of the assessment you'll know exactly where you're strong, what needs strengthening, and how long it will take to reach your goals. At the end, if I think we can help you, I'll ask — and you can say yes or no. Either answer is fine. Does that sound fair?"*
4. Get their buy-in — wait for a yes
5. Ask for height and weight, then take a seat

---

### Consultation Questions

> This is an information-gathering exercise — not a coaching session. Your job is to go deeper on what you already know. Do not offer advice, make recommendations, or get into lengthy discussions. You're listening and feeling for the emotion behind what they want.

---

**Question 1 — What is your main fitness goal?**

Reference what you already know:
> *"I read through your pre-qualification conversation and saw your main goal is to lose 7kg by the end of the year: tell me more about that."*
> *"I remember from our conversation you had some injuries and wanted to get stronger — tell me more about that."*

Follow-up:
> *"Do you have any other fitness goals?"*

Ask once, then sit in silence. Ask again: *"And you're sure there's nothing else?"* Sit in silence again.

> Note: Women often need space and time to fully open up. Don't rush this.

---

**Question 2 — Why are these goals important to you?**

> *"[Name], I can clearly see these goals matter to you — otherwise you wouldn't be here. You strike me as someone who takes action. Tell me, why are these goals important to you?"*

If they hesitate:
> *"That's ok if you can't quite put it into words. You've taken an hour out of your day / travelled [X] distance / built up the courage to come in. What's driven you to do all of this?"*

> Note: Slow down. Let them think. Re-ask in a different way if needed. When they open up — don't reply. Listen and let them run until they stop.

---

**Question 3 — When would you like to achieve your goal by?**

- *"Do you have a deadline coming up?"*
- *"An event you'd like to hit this by?"*
- *"Should we put a timeframe on it?"*

> Note: Pick up the pace here — this is action energy. Be honest about unrealistic timeframes.

**Timeframe guide to share:**
- Months 1–3: Learning phase — building the habit and the movement patterns
- Months 4–6: Results become visible and start to compound
- 12 months+: Every successful client has stayed at least this long
- Every cell in the body is replaced over a 6-month period — results take time
- Weight loss benchmark: 200–500g per week when following the meal plan and training 3–5x per week

---

**Question 4 — What have you tried in the past to achieve this goal?**

Let them think. Ask why those attempts failed. Ask if they liked that style of support or are looking for something different.

> Note: This is where you build the case for structure, support, and expert guidance. Don't lead — let them arrive there themselves.

---

**Question 5 — What do you do for work?**

Note whether their role is sedentary or active.

> Note: You're listening for their ability to invest in the level of service we provide, and their ability to show up consistently.

---

**Question 6 — Lifestyle questions**

- *"Do you smoke tobacco products on a weekly basis?"*
- *"Do you drink more than 7 standard alcoholic drinks per week?"*

> Note: You're identifying where money and habits are currently directed — and whether those habits conflict with their goals.

---

**Question 7 — What do you need from us to make this goal a success?**

Slow down again. Let them think. Connect the dots using what they've already told you:

> *"You mentioned earlier that you didn't like how crowded your old gym was — so would you be looking for something more private and personal, like what we offer here?"*
> *"You said CrossFit felt too fast and you got injured — so are you looking for something smarter, with coaches who can slow things down and make sure you're moving correctly?"*

---

**Question 8 — Do you have any existing injuries or conditions?**

Reference what you already have on file:

> *"Nothing came up for injuries in your pre-qualification conversation: is that still correct?"*
> *"I saw you mentioned osteoporosis in your conversation with us — is there anything more I should know before we move to the physical assessment?"*

---

**Question 9 — Any roadblocks in the next 6 months?**

Listen for objections — work, travel, time. Handle them here and now:

- Work: *"At your busiest, can you find 3 hours a week to invest in yourself — something that actually makes you more productive?"*
- Travel: *"How often do you travel? We have clients who travel a lot — they train with us when they're home and we give them programming to follow when they're away. Can you see yourself doing the same?"*

---

**Transition to physical assessment:**

Before saying this, confirm the PAR-Q is complete and reviewed. Clarify any Yes answer; do not begin testing when the issue is current, uncontrolled, recent, unexplained, materially changed, unclear, or otherwise requires written medical or allied-health clearance.

> *"Excellent — thank you for taking the time to go through that with me. Let's move on to the physical assessment. Are you ready?"*

---

## Part 2 — Physical Assessment

Record assessment data in Trainerize as the tests are delivered. Submit **SA: Coach Consultation Feedback** after the session so GHL also receives the assessment and outcome data required for marketing, remarketing and operational reporting.

> The consultation opens up the gap. The physical assessment makes it undeniable.

Before beginning: have the **Strength Assessment Card** ready. Record results in shorthand on both the digital form and the physical card as you go. The card goes home with them.

---

### Results 1 and 2: Single-Leg Capacity Test

**Exercise:** ATG Split Squat

**Below-Live regression:** Front-foot-elevated ATG Split Squat

1. Demonstrate the standard clearly before they begin
2. Test the right and left sides independently
3. Use the below-Live regression if full-depth floor ATG Split Squat is not attainable
4. Record elevation, reps, load, and the highest fully attained level for each side
5. After adequate recovery, progress through Live, Long, and Perform while technique and safety permit
6. Stop when the next standard is not fully attained or continuing would be unsafe

> Note: The prospect is warming up as they work through the levels — this is fine and expected. The goal is to find their honest starting point, not to exhaust them.

---

### Test 2 — Farmer's Carry

1. Tell them the target upfront — *"This is what we're working towards"*
2. Calculate 75% of their bodyweight — ask if they think they can hold it for 60 seconds
3. If yes — give it a try
4. If no — calculate 50% of their bodyweight — ask if they think they can hold it for 60 seconds
5. If yes — give it a try
6. Safety: show them how to pick up the weight from the bench and how to throw the weight away from their toes if needed
7. Stop a set early and increase weight if it looks effortless
8. Stop the set the moment form breaks down
9. After adequate recovery, test each safely attainable level and record weight, verified duration, and the highest fully attained level

> Note: Run this dynamically. You don't need four strict one-minute sets. Use common sense — start light, build up, and push to find their edge without pushing them unsafely.

---

### Test 3 — Full Plank

1. Demonstrate the standard — specifically what disqualifies the hold (sag or tent)
2. Start the timer when they're in position
3. Call the set the moment form breaks down — no exceptions
4. After adequate recovery, progress to right and left side planks, then Toes to Bar, when safely attainable
5. Record plank seconds, right and left side-plank seconds, Toes to Bar reps, and the highest fully attained level

---

### Presenting the Results

Once all four scored results are complete, bring their attention to the longevity scale on the card.

**If any scored result is below Live:**

> *"[Name], your results show gaps between your current capacity and the Live standard. Those gaps could be contributing to [difficulty / pain / limitation], but this assessment cannot diagnose the cause. What it does show us is where building strength and control may help. A structured training plan gives us a clear way to work on those gaps and measure progress over time. How does that feel? Do you want help closing that gap?"*

Wait in silence. Let them answer. When they say yes — move into the close.

## Part 3 — The Close

### Presenting the Packages

Open the offer document and turn the screen to face them.

**Lead with Strong, Fit & Flexible Membership:**

> *"This is our most popular membership. It's designed for the woman who wants support, structure, and a community, all focused on building real strength. It includes everything you need to get started: one KickStart onboarding session, a custom meal plan, and access to our full group training program."*

**Then introduce Fast Track:**

> *"For women who want a little extra — maybe they've got some injuries to work around, a medical condition we need to be mindful of, or they simply want to move faster — we offer the Fast Track. That adds a weekly one-on-one PT session so you get more personalised coaching on top of everything else."*

Then stop talking. Sit in silence while they look at the screen.

### The Close Question

> *"Is there anything on the screen that jumps out as something you can do — something you'd like to do?"*

Then go silent again. Wait for them to speak first.

### If They're Ready

> *"Great. Let's get you started today."*

Steps:
1. Open the membership agreement form
2. Enter their contact details
3. Select their package
4. Select the correct upfront cost and weekly debit amount — **double-check this combination before proceeding**
5. Walk them through terms and conditions briefly; offer time to read in full if they'd like
6. Have them sign
7. Complete payment via checkout redirect
8. Follow the membership-specific onboarding path: Fit & Flexible has no onboarding session; Strong, Fit & Flexible Membership has 1; Fast Track has 4

### If There Are Objections

> Note: If you prepared thoroughly, consulted well, and ran the physical assessment correctly, most objections should be minor. A well-run assessment does most of the objection-handling before you reach the close. See `reference/sops/objection-handling.md` for approved responses.

If the prospect needs time and the objection has not been properly isolated or handled, allow up to 24 hours and record **No Sale** before they leave. Update the outcome if they later contact the gym to join.

## Close the Attendance Record

Complete the appointment outcome before finishing the shift:

1. If the assessment was delivered, set the GHL appointment to **Showed**.
2. Submit **SA: Coach Consultation Feedback** and select the person who actually delivered the assessment.
3. If an approved cover coach delivered the assessment and is not listed, select **Approved cover / other**. Do not select the appointment owner unless they delivered it.
4. If the prospect did not attend, set the appointment to **No show** immediately. Do not infer No show from missing feedback.
5. If the prospect cancelled, retain **Cancelled**. Use **Invalid** only for an approved duplicate, test or bad booking.

An appointment still Confirmed 60 minutes after its end becomes an Admin Eve exception. The appointment coach owns the first correction, and matched feedback may support a Showed correction after review.
