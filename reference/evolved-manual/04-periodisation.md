## The Performance Warm-Up Protocol

**Source:** Dr Paul Cribb, Melbourne University — validated to increase performance by up to 30% through acclimatising the body and priming the central nervous system for the working load.

The Performance Warm-Up is an acclimation protocol applied to the main lift of each session. It uses the member's target working weight as the reference point and works through three progressive sets before the working sets begin.

| Set | Load | Reps |
|---|---|---|
| Warm-Up Set 1 | 20% of working weight | 12 reps |
| Warm-Up Set 2 | 60% of working weight | 6 reps |
| Acclimation Set | 100% of working weight | 1 rep |
| → Working Sets | 100% of working weight | 10–12 RM |

**How it works:**

The member identifies their target working weight before the session begins. The three warm-up sets are calculated from that number. By the time they reach the first working set, the nervous system has been progressively loaded to handle the target weight — the working sets feel manageable rather than shocking.

*Example: A member working at 40kg for their Squat performs 8kg × 12 reps → 24kg × 6 reps → 40kg × 1 rep → 40kg working sets.*

**Coaching notes:**
- The warm-up sets are not working sets — they should not be to failure and should feel progressively heavier but controlled
- The 20% set should feel very light — this is neurological priming, not cardio
- The 60% set is where the movement pattern gets locked in — focus on posture and range
- The 1-rep acclimation set confirms the member is ready — if it feels extremely hard, the working weight may need adjusting
- This protocol is used for the main lift only — accessories do not require it

**Application:**
- Used in every Sculpt & Strength and Build & Balance session for the primary lift
- Taught to members in Session 2 of the Intro Sessions
- Available as a video in Trainerize — direct members to watch it and follow along

## Progressive Overload Logic

The Evolved uses a simple, consistent progression rule across all programs:

| Result in current session | Action next session |
|---|---|
| Completed all reps across all working sets (e.g. 3 × 12) | Increase weight by 1–2kg |
| Completed 10–11 reps on any set | Hold weight — aim for one more rep |
| Completed fewer than 10 reps | Hold weight — focus on form and consistency |

This rule applies to every exercise in every program. Members must be taught this explicitly in Session 2 of the Intro Sessions. It is also the primary tool coaches use to drive adaptation over months and years — not program variety, not random loading, not gut feel.

**The compound effect:** A member who adds 1kg to her deadlift every two weeks adds 26kg in a year. A member who adds 1kg per month adds 12kg. Either trajectory, applied consistently, produces meaningful changes in strength, muscle mass, and bone density. The progressive overload rule is the mechanism. The coach's job is to track it and hold the member to it.

## Breathing & Bracing

Breathing and bracing is a foundational coaching skill at The Evolved. It is taught in every Intro Session, applied in every Sculpt & Strength and Build & Balance class, and reinforced in 1:1 PT. It is not a technique reserved for advanced lifters — it is the baseline safety and performance standard for all members under load.

### The Mechanism

Breathing in and bracing before a lift creates **intra-abdominal pressure (IAP)** — a pressurised cylinder of support formed by the diaphragm (top), pelvic floor (bottom), abdominal wall (sides), and spinal extensors (back). This pressure:

- Reduces spinal shear forces during loading
- Increases lumbar spine stiffness and stability
- Enables greater force transfer through the kinetic chain

Research from the University of Queensland, Yale University School of Medicine, and the National Institute of Occupational Health confirms that increased IAP directly increases spinal stiffness and reduces spinal flexion under load. Proper bracing has been shown to increase 1RM squat performance by an average of 12% in trained lifters.

*(Sources: Hackett & Chow, 2013, NSCA Journal of Strength & Conditioning Research; PubMed 20076968)*

### The Standard Technique

**Inhale and brace** before initiating the lift. Maintain the brace through the effort phase. **Exhale at the top** once the load is secured or the repetition is complete.

Teaching cue: *"Big breath in, fill your belly, hold it tight — then lift."*

This is taught in a lying position first, then transferred to standing and loaded movements. The sequence:
1. Lying — feel the diaphragm expand and the floor brace the spine
2. Standing unloaded — replicate the same expansion
3. Loaded movements — apply under progressively heavier loads

### The Pelvic Floor Consideration

For women, intense bracing and breath-holding places additional downward pressure on the pelvic floor. In members with existing pelvic floor dysfunction — leaking, heaviness, or prolapse symptoms — this can exacerbate symptoms.

**The evidence-based modification: "blow as you go"** — exhale through the exertion phase rather than holding the breath. This reduces peak pelvic floor loading while maintaining sufficient spinal support for most working weights.

This modification is particularly relevant for:
- Postpartum members
- Members in perimenopause or post-menopause
- Any member who reports pelvic floor symptoms during loaded exercise

**Referral standard:** Any member reporting persistent pelvic floor symptoms during or after training should be referred to a women's health physiotherapist. Document in Trainerize and notify management.

*(Sources: Bø et al., 2023, Neurourology and Urodynamics; PMC9222935 — Breathing, Straining and the Pelvic Floor, 2022)*

### Coaching Application

| Setting | Application |
|---|---|
| Intro Sessions | Teach in lying first, then standing, then loaded — every session |
| Sculpt & Strength / Build & Balance | Reinforce before working sets: cue as needed during set |
| 1:1 PT | Standard expectation for every working set; modify to "blow as you go" for members with pelvic floor symptoms |
