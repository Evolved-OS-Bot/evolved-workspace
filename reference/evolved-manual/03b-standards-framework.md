## Why Relative Strength Is the Right Standard

The most common mistake in strength assessment is measuring absolute load. A woman who deadlifts 60kg is not inherently strong or weak. A woman who deadlifts 150% of her bodyweight is demonstrably strong — at any bodyweight.

Relative strength standards accomplish what absolute standards cannot:

**They are fair across body sizes.** A 50kg woman and a 90kg woman are assessed against their own biology, not against each other or against a fixed number that favours lighter athletes.

**They predict functional capacity more accurately.** Carrying capacity, fall recovery, stair climbing, and lifting tasks are all relative to the person's own weight. A woman who can deadlift her bodyweight can get herself off the floor. One who cannot may not be able to.

**They track real-world capability over time.** As a woman gains muscle mass and body composition improves, her bodyweight may increase while her relative strength improves. Absolute standards would obscure this. Relative standards reveal it.

**They are consistent across the lifespan.** A 65-year-old woman and a 35-year-old woman can be held to the same relative standard because the standard measures capability proportionate to the body they are living in.

The Evolved Standards Framework uses bodyweight-relative standards for all loaded exercises. Exceptions — where the nature of the movement makes absolute rep standards more appropriate (push ups, pistol squat progressions, plank holds) — are noted in the relevant movement breakdown.

## Movement Breakdown Framework

For each exercise below: purpose, source, what it measures, why it matters for longevity, physical assets assessed, coaching notes, and progression strategy.

---

### Deadlift

**Purpose:** Maximal posterior chain strength. The primary measure of whole-body pulling force from the floor.

**Source:** StrengthLevel.com strength standards for women — relative bodyweight conversion

**What it measures:** The ability to generate force through the hips and posterior chain under a compressive spinal load. The most fundamental expression of functional strength.

**Why it matters for longevity:** Getting up from the floor, lifting objects from low surfaces, picking up children and grandchildren, loading luggage — all require the same fundamental mechanics as a deadlift. A woman who cannot deadlift 50% of her bodyweight will struggle with many of these tasks. A woman who can deadlift 125% will perform them with ease and minimal injury risk.

**Physical assets measured:** Strength, Muscle Mass, Bone Density, Grip Strength & Carrying Capacity

**Coaching notes:**
- Prioritise neutral spine and scapular retraction above all
- Do not progress load until form is consistent across all reps
- If grip fails before posterior chain, address grip independently
- The Live standard (50% BW) is achievable for most members within 3–6 months of consistent training

**Progression strategy:**
- Follow the canonical sequence: Broomstick Hinge, Dumbbell Romanian Deadlift, Barbell Romanian Deadlift, Trap Bar Deadlift, then Barbell Deadlift
- Advance one stage only after 10 pain-free, repeatable reps meet PRIME and remain below 7 RPE
- Treat Barbell Romanian Deadlift as a mandatory gate before Trap Bar Deadlift: 12 reps at 35 kg total barbell load, each with a controlled one-second hold just below mid-knee while maintaining a PRIME postural position
- Stop at the highest exercise performed proficiently at about 7 RPE; regress if PRIME, comfort or safety breaks down
- Increase load by smallest available increment once 3 sets × 8 reps is achieved at current load

### Squat

**Purpose:** Bilateral lower body strength through full range of motion. Measures lower extremity force production and mobility capacity simultaneously.

**Source:** StrengthLevel.com — relative bodyweight standards for women

**What it measures:** Quad, glute, and hamstring strength in combination with hip, knee, and ankle mobility. A constrained squat almost always reflects a mobility deficit, not just a strength deficit.

**Why it matters for longevity:** Sit-to-stand, stair climbing, picking up objects, recovering from low surfaces — all are squat pattern derivatives. The squat is also one of the primary loading mechanisms for bone density in the hips and spine.

**Physical assets measured:** Strength, Muscle Mass, Bone Density, Mobility, Balance & Coordination

**Coaching notes:**
- Use goblet squat as entry point — the counterbalance improves depth without requiring the same mobility as a loaded barbell back squat
- Depth is non-negotiable: hip crease below parallel
- Do not count reps where depth is not achieved — the standard requires full range, not partial range under heavier load

**Progression strategy:**
- Entry: Goblet Squat → Nexus Point Squat → Barbell Back Squat
- Progress when current variation achieves 7 RPE above the stated threshold (see Section 03 Intro Sessions Prescription Standards)

---

### Bench Press

**Purpose:** Horizontal pressing strength. Primary measure of upper body pushing capacity.

**Source:** StrengthLevel.com — relative bodyweight standards for women

**What it measures:** Pectoral, anterior deltoid, and tricep strength under a horizontal load. Also a secondary measure of scapular stability and upper back co-activation.

**Why it matters for longevity:** Pushing open heavy doors, rising from the floor with hands, pushing luggage into overhead compartments, and any pushing task that occurs in daily life. Also a major driver of upper body muscle mass — a critical longevity asset.

**Physical assets measured:** Strength, Muscle Mass

**Coaching notes:**
- Arch, scapular retraction, and active hips are non-negotiable for both performance and shoulder joint safety
- The Live standard (25% BW) is accessible for most members within the first 2–3 months
- DB variation is the appropriate entry point — allows independent arm correction and safer load management for new lifters

**Progression strategy:**
- Entry: DB Bench Press → Barbell Bench Press when DB exceeds 15kg total (per hand) with full technical criteria
- Cue: "Bend the bar. Pull the bar into your chest. Drive your feet through the floor."

---

### Overhead Press

**Purpose:** Vertical pressing strength. One of the most functionally demanding upper body strength tasks.

**Source:** StrengthLevel.com — relative bodyweight standards for women

**What it measures:** Shoulder strength, thoracic mobility, scapular stability, and the ability to produce overhead force without spinal compensation. The overhead press is simultaneously a strength test and a mobility screen.

**Why it matters for longevity:** Lifting bags into overhead compartments, reaching into high shelves, carrying objects overhead, and maintaining shoulder health for the rest of life. Shoulder health and overhead capacity are closely linked to quality of life in later decades.

**Physical assets measured:** Strength, Mobility, Bone Density

**Coaching notes:**
- Restricted overhead range is a red flag: investigate thoracic mobility and lat flexibility before loading
- Rib flare and lumbar extension are compensatory patterns that shift load from shoulder to spine — address immediately
- The Live standard (20% BW) is the lowest threshold in the strength category — clients who cannot reach this should be prioritised for shoulder and thoracic work

**Progression strategy:**
- Entry: Seated DB OHP → Standing DB OHP → Barbell OHP
- Progress when seated variation is clean and repeatable above 10% BW per hand

---

### Lat Pulldown

**Purpose:** Vertical pulling strength. Primary measure of upper back and lats under a downward resistance.

**Source:** StrengthLevel.com — adapted standard

**What it measures:** Lat, rhomboid, and bicep strength. Also a proxy measure for relative pulling capacity before a client can perform bodyweight pull ups.

**Why it matters for longevity:** Upper back strength is the primary counterbalance to the forward-loaded posture that accumulates over decades of sitting. It directly supports posture, spinal health, and shoulder joint integrity. A woman who pulls with adequate strength tends to stand and move differently than one who does not.

**Physical assets measured:** Strength, Muscle Mass

**Coaching notes:**
- The lat pulldown is a teaching tool as much as a strength exercise — it develops the pulling mechanics, scapular depression, and lat engagement that eventually transfer to pull ups
- Prioritise full range: arms fully extended at top, bar to upper chest at bottom
- Do not allow the torso to rock backward excessively — pulling mechanics should drive the movement, not momentum

**Progression strategy:**
- Long-term goal: transition from lat pulldown to reverse grip pull up when the Lat Pulldown standard reaches 60–70% BW
- Pull up progressions: band-assisted → eccentric-only → full reverse grip pull up

---

### Push Ups

**Purpose:** Bodyweight horizontal pressing capacity. Also a reliable proxy for functional upper body strength without equipment.

**Source:** Functional bodyweight standards — widely referenced in applied strength literature

**What it measures:** Relative upper body pushing strength. Because the standard is rep-based against bodyweight, a higher rep count reflects a higher strength-to-bodyweight ratio.

**Why it matters for longevity:** Push ups measure the ability to handle one's own bodyweight under horizontal press. The ability to push up from the floor is both practically useful (fall recovery) and indicative of sufficient upper body relative strength.

**Physical assets measured:** Strength, Muscle Mass

**Coaching notes:**
- Standard requires full chest-to-ground contact on every rep
- No partial range — if a member cannot achieve chest-to-ground, regress to elevated surface push ups and build range
- Elbows at approximately 45° to torso — not flared wide

**Progression strategy:**
- Entry: Elevated push up (hands on bench) → Floor push up from knees → Full push up
- Progress when 15 consecutive reps are achievable at current regression

---

### Barbell Romanian Deadlift

**Purpose:** Posterior chain endurance and hamstring-dominant hip hinge capacity under moderate load.

**Source:** Dr John Rusin Functional Strength Standards

**What it measures:** Hamstring flexibility and loading capacity, glute strength through hip hinge, and the ability to maintain a neutral spine under a demanding eccentric load.

**Why it matters for longevity:** Hamstring health is one of the most underrated contributors to injury prevention and joint longevity. Tight, weak hamstrings are a significant contributor to chronic lower back pain. The RDL directly addresses this — building extensibility under load rather than passive flexibility.

**Physical assets measured:** Strength, Mobility, Bone Density

**Coaching notes:**
- The RDL is not a squat — the hips move back, not down
- "Push the floor away with your hips" — the cue that best captures the movement intent
- Knee soft, not locked — a hyperextended knee under load is a joint risk
- Range is governed by hamstring length, not a fixed distance — the bar drops as far as a neutral spine is maintained

**Progression strategy:**
- Entry: DB RDL → Single-leg DB RDL (for asymmetry correction) → Barbell RDL
- The Single-leg RDL is a powerful symmetry tool — assess left/right independently

---

### Single Arm DB Reverse Lunge

**Purpose:** Unilateral lower body strength with rotational stability demand from the single-arm load offset.

**Source:** Dr John Rusin Functional Strength Standards

**What it measures:** Single-leg strength, pelvic stability, anti-rotation core control, and the ability to absorb force on one leg while producing it on the other.

**Why it matters for longevity:** Most daily movement is unilateral. Walking, stair climbing, and stepping over obstacles are all single-leg tasks. The reverse lunge with a single-arm offset is a functional replication of the demands placed on the body during loaded daily tasks — carrying groceries, a bag, or a child on one side.

**Physical assets measured:** Strength, Balance & Coordination, Mobility

**Coaching notes:**
- The single-arm offset forces the core to resist lateral flexion — do not allow the torso to lean toward the loaded side
- Step backward far enough that the front shin remains vertical — common error is a knee-dominant forward lunge
- Assess each side independently and note any asymmetry

**Progression strategy:**
- Entry: Bodyweight reverse lunge → DB bilateral reverse lunge → Single-arm DB reverse lunge → Deficit reverse lunge

---

### Single Arm DB Overhead Press

**Purpose:** Unilateral overhead pressing strength with anti-lateral flexion demand.

**Source:** Dr John Rusin Functional Strength Standards

**What it measures:** Shoulder strength, scapular control, and the ability to produce overhead force on one side while resisting lateral spinal flexion on the other. A more demanding test of shoulder health than the bilateral press.

**Why it matters for longevity:** Single-arm overhead work is representative of real-life reaching and carrying tasks. Asymmetries in overhead pressing strength often predict shoulder impingement, rotator cuff issues, and long-term joint degradation if not addressed.

**Physical assets measured:** Strength, Mobility

**Coaching notes:**
- Assess each side independently — the single-arm press is as much a symmetry tool as a strength test
- Rib cage down, glutes engaged — prevent the body from using a lateral lean to assist the weaker side
- The Live standard (10% BW) is achievable for most members in the early phase of training

**Progression strategy:**
- Entry: Seated single-arm DB press → Standing single-arm DB press → Single-arm landmine press

---

### Chest Supported Row

**Purpose:** Horizontal pulling strength in a mechanically isolated position. Removes the posterior chain from the equation to assess upper back pulling capacity directly.

**Source:** Dr John Rusin Functional Strength Standards

**What it measures:** Rhomboid, mid-trap, and rear deltoid strength — the posterior shoulder complex that resists the forward shoulder posture accumulated through modern sedentary behaviour.

**Why it matters for longevity:** Postural integrity is one of the most visible markers of physical decline. The chest supported row directly targets the muscles responsible for maintaining upright, open posture. Strong posterior shoulder muscles reduce chronic neck and upper back pain — two of the most common complaints among women entering the gym.

**Physical assets measured:** Strength, Muscle Mass

**Coaching notes:**
- Chest support is non-negotiable — removes the ability to use momentum or posterior chain drive
- Full retraction at the top, full extension at the bottom — both ends of range matter
- Elbows at approximately 45° to torso — not flared wide, not pinned to sides

**Progression strategy:**
- Entry: Single-arm DB row (supported on bench) → Chest supported DB row → Chest supported Barbell row

---

### Reverse Grip Pull Up

**Purpose:** Vertical pulling strength under full bodyweight. The Evolved's primary upper body pulling standard.

**Source:** Dr John Rusin Functional Strength Standards

**What it measures:** Relative upper body pulling strength — the lat pulldown standard expressed as bodyweight capacity. Also measures grip endurance, bicep contribution, and scapular stability through a full range of motion.

**Why it matters for longevity:** The ability to lift one's own bodyweight is a profound functional marker. A woman who can pull her own bodyweight is capable of a class of physical tasks — including certain fall recovery scenarios — that are unavailable to one who cannot. The reverse grip (supinated) variation reduces shoulder impingement risk and increases bicep contribution, making it more appropriate than the overhand pull up for most women.

**Physical assets measured:** Strength, Muscle Mass, Grip Strength & Carrying Capacity

**Coaching notes:**
- The Live standard (30% BW assisted, progressing toward bodyweight) requires systematic progressions — do not skip phases
- Full hang at the bottom, chin clearly above bar at the top
- Kipping is not permitted — this is a strength standard, not a conditioning standard

**Progression strategy:**
- Band-assisted pull up → Eccentric-only pull up (3–5 seconds down) → Full pull up → Weighted pull up (for Long/Perform)

---

### Core (Plank / Side Plank / Toes to Bar)

**Purpose:** Spinal stability under isometric and dynamic load. Assessed as a progression across three increasingly demanding positions.

**Source:** Broadly referenced in functional movement literature; incorporated into the Evolved assessment framework

**What it measures:**
- Plank: anterior core endurance and spinal neutrality under sustained load
- Side Plank: lateral core endurance and hip abductor/adductor contribution
- Toes to Bar: dynamic hip flexion strength and lat/core integration under a hanging load

**Why it matters for longevity:** Core stability is the foundation of all loaded movement. A weak or untrained core shifts compressive and shear forces onto passive structures — discs, ligaments, and facet joints. This is the mechanism behind the majority of chronic lower back pain presentations. A woman who can hold a 2-minute plank with correct form has earned the right to load her spine at progressively higher intensities.

**Physical assets measured:** Strength, Balance & Coordination

**Coaching notes:**
- The 2:00 plank must be held on hands and toes, not forearms — the forearm variation reduces demand and does not meet the standard
- Shoulders protracted, hips in line — sagging lumbar or tented hips both disqualify the hold
- Side plank standard is 1:00 per side — assess both sides and note asymmetry

**Progression strategy:**
- Entry: Elevated plank (hands on bench) → Knee plank → Full plank → Side plank → Toes to Bar

---

### Running

**Purpose:** Aerobic capacity and sustained locomotion under bodyweight over distance.

**Source:** RunningLevel.com — women's relative performance standards

**What it measures:** Cardiovascular efficiency, lower extremity endurance, and the aerobic capacity required to sustain continuous moderate-intensity effort. Running is also a proxy measure of work capacity — the ability to sustain output over time.

**Why it matters for longevity:** Walk/run capacity is one of the most direct correlates of cardiovascular health and metabolic function. A woman who can run 5km in under 30 minutes has a metabolic engine that supports daily life without fatigue. A woman who reaches the Long standard (10km in <50 minutes) is highly unlikely to be limited by cardiovascular fitness in any ordinary life situation.

**Physical assets measured:** Work Capacity, Independence

**Coaching notes:**
- Running is not the primary Evolved modality — it supports the framework, it does not replace strength
- Members who present with running goals should be educated on the strength-first rationale before adding volume
- The PERFORM standard (21km in <90 minutes) represents a well-trained aerobic athlete — this is not the expected destination for most members

**Progression strategy:**
- Entry: Walk/run intervals → 5km continuous → Progressive distance increases with 10% weekly volume rule

---

### Rowing

**Purpose:** Whole-body cardiovascular endurance with a significant upper body pulling component.

**Source:** RowingLevel.com — women's relative performance standards

**What it measures:** Aerobic capacity, upper body pulling endurance, and leg drive power — uniquely, rowing simultaneously tests the cardiovascular system and the posterior chain.

**Why it matters for longevity:** Rowing is a non-impact, full-body conditioning tool that complements the strength-first approach. It develops work capacity without the joint stress of running, making it particularly valuable for peri- and post-menopausal members managing joint health.

**Physical assets measured:** Work Capacity, Strength

**Coaching notes:**
- Rowing technique is the primary determinant of performance — poor technique will limit power output and create injury risk
- Sequence: legs → hips → arms on the drive; arms → hips → legs on the recovery
- For time-pressed assessments, the 1000m row provides a reliable and repeatable conditioning snapshot

**Progression strategy:**
- Entry: Technique focus at low rate → Progressive pace work → Interval training → Time trials

### Beep Test

**Purpose:** Maximal aerobic capacity (VO2max proxy) measured through a progressive shuttle run.

**Source:** Standard multistage fitness test

**What it measures:** The highest level at which a member can sustain the prescribed pace. Widely used as a VO2max correlate in fitness and sports science contexts.

**Why it matters for longevity:** VO2max is one of the single strongest predictors of all-cause mortality and cardiovascular health. A woman with a higher VO2max has a larger safety margin between rest and maximal exertion — and recovers more quickly from all physical tasks. Maintaining VO2max through midlife and beyond is a longevity investment with compounding returns.

**Physical assets measured:** Work Capacity

**Coaching notes:**
- The beep test is a field test, not a training tool — use it for periodic assessment, not regular programming
- Level 6.0 (Live) represents a minimum acceptable aerobic base for a healthy adult woman
- Level 9.0 (Long) reflects meaningfully above-average cardiovascular fitness for the general population

**Progression strategy:**
- Low-level members: build base aerobic capacity through consistent LISS + strength training before testing
- Re-test no more frequently than every 12 weeks

### Pistol Squat

**Purpose:** Maximal single-leg strength and mobility expression. The most demanding unilateral lower body standard in the framework.

**Source:** Ben Patrick (Knees Over Toes Guy) — athletic durability standards

**What it measures:** Single-leg strength through a full range of motion, ankle dorsiflexion, hip mobility, and the ability to control the entire body's weight over a single foot. The pistol squat is simultaneously a strength test, a mobility test, and a balance test.

**Why it matters for longevity:** The ability to lower to and rise from a single-leg squat is a direct functional correlate of fall resistance, joint health, and movement independence. Most falls occur in single-leg stance phases — the pistol squat builds the neuromuscular control that prevents them.

**Physical assets measured:** Strength, Mobility, Balance & Coordination, Independence

**Coaching notes:**
- The Live standard (1 rep below parallel) is a meaningful achievement for most members — do not underestimate its difficulty
- Progressions must address the limiting factor: if ankle mobility prevents depth, address ankle first
- The Long standard (10 reps HCBP) represents genuine athletic capability

**Progression strategy:**
- Entry: Box pistol squat → Elevated surface pistol (heel on small plate) → Band-assisted pistol → Full pistol squat

---

### ATG Split Squat

**Purpose:** Unilateral leg strength and mobility through the deepest achievable range. The primary Strength Assessment test (see Section 03).

**Source:** Ben Patrick (Knees Over Toes Guy) — ATG (Ass to Grass) standards

**What it measures:** Anterior knee strength and resilience, ankle dorsiflexion, hip flexor extensibility, and the ability to produce force in a deep unilateral position. Full hamstring-to-calf contact is the depth criterion — not a subjective estimate.

**Why it matters for longevity:** Knee pain is one of the most common reasons women stop exercising. The ATG Split Squat directly builds the anterior knee musculature — quad strength at end range, patellar tendon resilience — that prevents and resolves this. A woman who can perform 10 reps with 50% bodyweight is building the joint resilience that protects her knees for decades.

**Physical assets measured:** Strength, Mobility, Balance & Coordination, Bone Density

**Coaching notes:**
- This exercise is the Evolved's primary lower body assessment tool — all coaches must be able to coach it to a high standard
- The knee-forward (over-toe) position is intentional and evidence-supported — do not cue knee back
- Begin with front foot elevated to manage the depth requirement for members with limited ankle mobility
- Assess left and right independently and record any asymmetry

**Progression strategy:**
- Entry: Front foot elevated ATG Split Squat → Floor-level ATG Split Squat → Loaded ATG Split Squat → Single-arm loaded for rotational demand

---

### Sled Push / Sled Pull

**Purpose:** Resisted locomotion. Develops lower body drive, work capacity, and mental resilience under load.

**Source:** Standard conditioning and rehabilitation literature; included in functional fitness frameworks by multiple performance coaches

**What it measures:** Leg drive, hip extension power, and the ability to sustain forceful effort over distance. The sled is one of the safest high-intensity tools available — minimal eccentric load means recovery is fast and injury risk is low.

**Why it matters for longevity:** Sled training develops the kind of forceful locomotion capacity that translates directly to hill walking, stair climbing, and any task requiring sustained physical effort. It also develops lower body power that supports fall recovery and reactive strength.

**Physical assets measured:** Strength, Work Capacity, Independence

**Coaching notes:**
- The standard excludes sled weight — load is the additional weight placed on the sled
- Forward sled: low body position, drive through full hip extension
- Reverse sled: upright position, focus on quad drive and steady backward movement
- Sled work is well-tolerated by members with lower back sensitivities due to the absence of spinal compression

**Progression strategy:**
- Entry: Light load at technique pace → Progressive load increases → Combined push/pull circuits at Long-standard loads

## The Future-Proofing Score

The Future-Proofing Score is a simple, coach-applied framework for assessing a member's overall position across the standards.

### How It Works

At any assessment point, record the highest level achieved (Live / Long / Perform / Below Live) for each of the primary standards. Assign scores as follows:

| Level | Score |
|---|---|
| Below Live | 0 |
| Live | 1 |
| Long | 2 |
| Perform | 3 |

### Primary Standards for Scoring

Use the following exercises for the Future-Proofing Score. These represent the most direct measures of the Eight Physical Assets:

| Exercise | Asset Measured |
|---|---|
| Deadlift | Strength / Muscle Mass |
| ATG Split Squat (average of L + R) | Mobility / Bone Density / Balance |
| DB Farmer Walk | Grip Strength & Carrying Capacity |
| Core (Plank progression) | Strength / Balance |
| Running or Rowing | Work Capacity |
| Push Ups | Strength (relative) |

**Maximum score: 18 (6 exercises × 3)**

### Score Interpretation

| Score | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| 0–5 | Significant capability gap. High priority for structured strength and fitness development. |
| 6–9 | Foundational capacity. Live in some areas. Meaningful progress required for long-term independence. |
| 10–13 | Solid base. Approaching Long across most areas. Building physical insurance. |
| 14–17 | High physical capability. Long achieved across most areas. Approaching Perform in select areas. |
| 18 | Exceptional. Full Perform across all primary standards. |

### How to Use It

- Record Future-Proofing Score at every formal re-assessment
- Present it to the member as a single number alongside the individual test results
- Use it in member education: *"Your score is 9 today. Most women who commit to 12 months of consistent training move from here to 13–15. That's the difference between a body that struggles and a body that doesn't."*
- Use it as a retention and re-engagement tool — it gives members a meaningful, personalised number to work toward
