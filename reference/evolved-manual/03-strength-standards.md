## Purpose

Define the performance benchmarks used to assess, prescribe, and track every member at The Evolved. Standards give members a concrete picture of where they are and what they're working toward. They also give coaches an objective anchor for programming decisions.

---

## Learning Outcomes

After reading this section coaches should be able to:
- Identify a member's level for each of the three assessment tests
- Prescribe the correct programming response based on results
- Communicate results to members in a motivating, clear way
- Use the Strength Assessment Card to present results during the close

---

## Why We Created the Evolved Standards

Most gyms measure performance. We measure capability.

The Evolved Standards were not designed to determine who is the strongest woman in the room. They were designed to answer a much more important question:

> **What should a woman be physically capable of doing if she wants to live a long, healthy, independent life?**

To answer that question, we reviewed strength, fitness, and performance standards from some of the world's largest strength databases and leading performance coaches — including StrengthLevel, Dr John Rusin, RunningLevel, RowingLevel, and Ben Patrick (Knees Over Toes Guy). We then organised these standards into a framework specifically designed for women: not athletes, not bodybuilders, not powerlifters — women who want to remain strong, capable, healthy, and independent throughout every stage of life.

The result is a framework built around three stages — Live, Long, and Perform.

We intentionally avoided labels like Beginner, Intermediate, and Advanced. Those labels describe gym experience. They do not describe life. Live, Long, and Perform immediately communicate purpose. They connect training to real life.

> Strength is not built for the gym. Strength is built for life.

---

## The Three Levels

| Level | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Live** | Foundational capacity for confident everyday movement |
| **Long** | Strength to maintain independence and function as you age |
| **Perform** | Athletic and performance-level strength |

### Live

The minimum physical capability every woman should possess to confidently navigate modern life. These are not athletic standards — they are life standards.

Can she lift her luggage into an overhead compartment? Can she carry groceries without pain? Can she climb stairs without needing to stop? Can she get up from the floor without assistance? Can she move through her day with confidence rather than caution?

The Live standards represent the baseline level of strength, fitness, and movement that supports effective everyday function. **Live is the entry standard.** It is not a guarantee of being pain-free and it is not a diagnostic threshold.

### Long

The standards associated with long-term health, resilience, and independence. This is the heart of The Evolved Method.

A woman who achieves the Long standards should be capable of keeping up with grandchildren, walking for hours through European cities without fatigue, climbing the steep staircases of old Parisian apartments without fear, exploring beaches, mountains, and hiking trails well into later life, and remaining physically capable when many people her age are beginning to accept decline as normal.

Long standards are not about becoming an athlete. They are about delaying or preventing the physical decline that so often accompanies ageing. When a woman reaches the Long standards, she is no longer simply exercising — she is building physical insurance for her future.

### Perform

Exceptional physical capability. The Perform standards exist for women who want to go beyond health and longevity. These are athletic standards — representing the ability to perform at a high level in strength sports, fitness competitions, obstacle events, or other demanding physical pursuits.

Not every woman will reach Perform. Not every woman needs to. But every woman benefits from moving toward it. The Perform standards remind us that the human body is capable of far more than most people believe.

## Assessment Standards by Test

The Strength Assessment uses three exercise categories producing four scored assessment results. The Single-Leg Capacity Test produces an independent right-side result and left-side result, so symmetry can be assessed reliably.

| Test | Exercise | What it measures |
|---|---|---|
| **Single-Leg Capacity Test (R)** | ATG Split Squat | Unilateral leg strength, balance, mobility, symmetry |
| **Single-Leg Capacity Test (L)** | ATG Split Squat | Unilateral leg strength, balance, mobility, symmetry |
| **Grip Endurance Test** | Farmers Carry | Grip strength, carrying capacity, whole-body endurance |
| **Spinal Control Test** | Plank / Side Plank / Toes to Bar | Core stability, spinal control, anti-rotation strength |

---

### Results 1 and 2: Single-Leg Capacity Test (R + L)

**Exercise:** ATG Split Squat

**Below-Live regression:** Front-foot-elevated ATG Split Squat

| Level | Standard |
|---|---|
| **Live** | 10 reps each side — full depth (ATG) |
| **Long** | 10 reps each side + 50% bodyweight |
| **Perform** | 10 reps each side + 100% bodyweight |

**What below-Live means:** The result identifies a current capacity gap in the tested movement. It could be contributing to difficulty, pain, or limitation, but the assessment does not diagnose the cause.

**Note on symmetry:** Assess left and right independently. An asymmetry between sides — even if both reach Live — signals a priority area for programming.

**Programming response:**
- Below Live → prioritise unilateral leg work, ankle mobility, and hip flexor development
- At Live → progress elevation, then add load
- Asymmetry present → address weaker side first; do not progress until sides match

---

### Test 3 — Grip Endurance Test

**Exercise:** Farmers Carry (Walk of Independence)

| Level | Standard |
|---|---|
| **Live** | 1:00 + 75% of bodyweight |
| **Long** | 1:00 + 100% of bodyweight |
| **Perform** | 1:00 + 150% of bodyweight |

**Example:** 60kg client = 45kg total (22.5kg per hand) for Live standard.

**What below-Live means:** Grip strength deficiency. Strong predictor of reduced independence in the final decade of life.

**Programming response:**
- Below Live → include Carry twice per week; prioritise grip and loaded carry variations
- At Live → maintain; progress toward Long
- Above Live → progress toward Perform

---

### Test 4 — Spinal Control Test

**Exercise:** Plank progression (Plank → Side Plank → Toes to Bar)

| Level | Standard |
|---|---|
| **Live** | 2:00 plank — hands and toes, shoulders protracted, hips in line, no sagging or tenting |
| **Long** | 2:00 side plank |
| **Perform** | 10 Toes to Bar |

**What below-Live means:** Core stability deficit. Strongly correlated with chronic lower back pain.

**Programming response:**
- Below Live → include plank work in every session; reinforce bracing mechanics
- At Live → progress to side plank
- At Long → progress to Toes to Bar

## The Direction of Progress

The levels are milestones, not verdicts. Record the highest fully attained standard and the exact raw result. A result below a threshold is not a failure; it identifies current capacity and the next progression.

During the initial Strength Assessment, test every safely attainable level. After adequate recovery, progress from Live to Long and then Perform while technique, safety, and the prerequisite level permit. Stop when the next standard is not fully attained or continuing would be unsafe.

A woman who improves her deadlift from 50% of bodyweight to 100% has dramatically improved her future physical capacity. A woman who progresses from a 30-second plank to 2 minutes has improved her resilience. A woman who progresses from struggling with stairs to climbing multiple flights comfortably has improved her quality of life.

Every improvement increases capability. Every improvement builds resilience. Every improvement future-proofs the body.

The standards provide a map. The journey toward them is where the real transformation occurs.

## Using the Strength Assessment Card

The card contains all four tests, their standards across all three levels, and a longevity timeline. It is a physical artefact — the prospect takes it home. There are 7 life-stage-specific versions of the card (Teen, 20–30s, Planning Pregnancy, Pregnancy, Postpartum, Peri-Menopause, Post-Menopause). The standards are identical across all cards — only the messaging and framing differ.

**During the assessment:**
1. Select the correct life-stage card for this prospect
2. Record results in shorthand on the digital form AND the physical card
3. Use the shorthand to reference back when presenting results
4. After all four tests, present results against the longevity scale

**Presenting shortfalls (the key script):**
> *"[Name], your results show gaps between your current capacity and the Live standard. Those gaps could be contributing to [difficulty / pain / limitation], but this assessment cannot diagnose the cause. What it does show us is where building strength and control may help. A structured training plan gives us a clear way to work on those gaps and measure progress over time. How does that feel? Do you want help closing that gap?"*

Then wait in silence.

## Intro Sessions Prescription Standards

After the Strength Assessment, Intro Sessions establish the member's exercise level for ongoing program delivery. These are the progression thresholds used in Sessions 1–4:

### Squat Pattern Thresholds

| Progression Trigger | Exercise Assigned |
|---|---|
| Goblet Squat achieves 7 RPE above 10kg with consistent depth and control | Progress to Nexus Point Squat |
| No progression reached | Stay at highest achieved level |

### Hinge Pattern Thresholds

| Progression Trigger | Exercise Assigned |
|---|---|
| Broomstick Hinge: 10 pain-free, repeatable reps meet PRIME and remain below 7 RPE | Progress to Dumbbell Romanian Deadlift |
| Dumbbell Romanian Deadlift: 10 pain-free, repeatable reps meet PRIME and remain below 7 RPE | Progress to Barbell Romanian Deadlift |
| Barbell Romanian Deadlift: 12 reps at 35 kg total barbell load, each with a controlled one-second hold just below mid-knee while maintaining a PRIME postural position | Progress to Trap Bar Deadlift |
| Trap Bar Deadlift: 10 pain-free, repeatable reps meet PRIME and remain below 7 RPE | Progress to Barbell Deadlift |
| Any stage reaches about 7 RPE with PRIME maintained | Assign that exercise as the working exercise |
| PRIME, comfort or safety breaks down | Assign the previous proficient exercise |

### Bench Pattern Thresholds

| Progression Trigger | Exercise Assigned |
|---|---|
| DB Bench Press: scapula retraction + arch + active hips above 15kg total | Progress to Barbell Bench Press |

---

## Key Coaching Principle

**Never progress an exercise when form breaks down.** The standard is a floor, not a ceiling to be stretched toward with compromised technique. A member who stays at Goblet Squat for 3 months with perfect form is making more progress than one who rushes to Barbell Squat with poor mechanics.
